## 1. Purpose

This log records material decisions affecting Everneed's company direction, product, technology, operations, finances, legal position, partnerships and people.

Its purpose is to make decisions traceable. It should show:

- What was decided, proposed, deferred or rejected.
- Who had authority to make the decision.
- Why the decision was made.
- What evidence and assumptions informed it.
- What alternatives were considered.
- What work or risks follow from it.
- When it should be reviewed.
- Whether the decision has been implemented and verified.

This is an internal governance record. It is not a board minute book, statutory company register, shareholder agreement, contract, or substitute for professional legal and financial advice.

## 2. Decision authority and principles

### 2.1 Authority

Everneed is currently founder-led. The founder retains final decision authority unless a specific responsibility is explicitly delegated and documented.

Future directors, employees, advisers, contractors and partners must not be assumed to have authority to bind the business merely because they contribute to a project.

Where a legal entity is later established, this register must be aligned with its constitutional documents, board processes, shareholder arrangements and applicable law.

### 2.2 Decision principles

1. **Founder accountability:** Material company decisions require founder approval or a clearly recorded delegation.
2. **Evidence before claims:** Distinguish facts, test results, estimates, assumptions and opinions.
3. **Stage-appropriate execution:** Avoid commitments that exceed product, cash or operating readiness.
4. **Customer and seller trust:** Consider privacy, security, fairness, safety and service reliability.
5. **Financial discipline:** Record commercial impact, cash commitments and liabilities.
6. **Reversibility:** Prefer small, measurable experiments when uncertainty is high.
7. **Traceability:** Record the reason, owner, date and consequences of important decisions.
8. **Confidentiality:** Keep sensitive legal, financial, personnel, security and investor decisions in restricted internal records.

## 3. Decision status definitions

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Proposed** | A recommendation exists; no approval is recorded. |
| **Under review** | Evidence or stakeholder input is being gathered. |
| **Approved** | The authorised decision-maker has approved the decision. |
| **In progress** | Approved work is being implemented. |
| **Implemented — unverified** | A change appears to have been made, but the intended result has not been tested sufficiently. |
| **Verified** | Acceptance evidence confirms the intended result in the stated environment and scope. |
| **Deferred** | A decision is intentionally postponed; a review trigger should be stated. |
| **Rejected** | The proposal was considered and not accepted. |
| **Superseded** | A later decision replaces this one; link the replacement record. |
| **Blocked** | A dependency prevents implementation or a decision. |

Do not mark a decision verified merely because a document, code change, migration file, interface or configuration entry exists.

## 7. Approval and change control

- Record material decisions as soon as practicable after approval.
- Do not backdate approvals. If recording a past decision, state the actual decision date if known and the date it was entered into this log.
- Link supporting documents, pull requests, issues, test reports, contracts or financial schedules.
- If a decision changes, create a new record and mark the earlier one superseded. Preserve the history.
- Record dissenting views or material unresolved risks where relevant.
- A change to a strategy document does not automatically mean the underlying business decision has been approved.
- A code merge does not automatically mean a product or operational decision has been verified.
- Decisions involving legal obligations, investment, equity, employment, data protection, payment custody or material financial exposure should receive appropriate professional review.
- Do not place passwords, API keys, private customer data, bank details or sensitive identity documents in this register.

## 8. Monthly governance review

At each monthly review, the founder or delegated owner should:

1. Review all open, overdue, blocked and deferred decisions.
2. Check whether assumptions have changed.
3. Confirm that completed actions have evidence.
4. Reassess risks and financial commitments.
5. Record decisions arising from product, customer, seller and operational feedback.
6. Mark superseded decisions and link replacements.
7. Identify decisions required before the next milestone.
8. Update the monthly progress report.
