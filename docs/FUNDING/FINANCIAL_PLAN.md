# EVERNEED
## Financial Plan & Financial Management Framework

**Document type:** Internal financial planning and control framework  
**Document status:** Working model and planning templates; no budgets, prices or forecasts approved  
**Business status:** Founder-led startup; not yet registered with Nigeria's Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC)  
**Initial market:** Nigeria  
**Initial product:** Multivendor digital marketplace with a planned hub-supported fulfilment option  
**Project reference branch:** `web-portal`  
**Last updated:** November 2026

## 1. Purpose

This plan establishes how Everneed should budget, forecast, track cash, understand marketplace economics and prepare credible financial information as the business moves toward validation and launch.

It is designed for a founder-led startup whose product foundation is under development and whose live commercial performance has not yet been verified. It therefore provides a financial structure and calculation methods rather than invented revenue, costs, salaries, transaction volumes or profit projections.

The plan is an internal management framework. It is not audited financial information, tax advice, a commitment to spend, a lender forecast or an investor promise.

## 2. Current Financial Position and Evidence Standard

Everneed's current planning position should be treated as **pre-validation** until actual financial records establish otherwise.

The product status review does not establish live completed transactions, recurring platform revenue, seller payouts, a functioning hub operation or verified logistics unit economics. The business model identifies potential revenue streams, but no commission rate, subscription price or advertising price is approved.

Consequently:

- Do not report projected revenue as actual revenue.
- Do not treat sign-ups, waitlist entries or seller applications as sales.
- Do not treat total customer checkout value as Everneed revenue.
- Do not treat seller balances or carrier collections as company cash available to spend.
- Do not report a profit until all relevant costs, refunds, taxes and obligations are accounted for.
- Label every estimate, assumption, target and scenario clearly.
- Update this plan when actual costs, operating decisions or transaction evidence become available.

### Financial status labels

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Actual | Supported by invoices, receipts, bank/payment statements or reconciled system records |
| Committed | Contractually or otherwise formally approved obligation not yet fully paid |
| Budget | Approved spending limit for a defined period or purpose |
| Forecast | Expected future result based on stated assumptions |
| Scenario | Alternative outcome used to test uncertainty |
| Target | Desired performance level, not a forecast or achieved result |
| Unverified | Evidence is not sufficient to confirm the amount or outcome |

## 3. Financial Objectives

Everneed's financial priorities should be:

1. Preserve available cash while the product and operating model are being validated.
2. Know the cost of maintaining and improving the platform.
3. Establish clear separation between company funds and money collected on behalf of sellers or delivery providers.
4. Understand the economics of each marketplace order and fulfilment route.
5. Avoid recurring commitments before demand and workload justify them.
6. Prepare a realistic cash forecast and funding requirement.
7. Maintain accurate records for tax, legal, investor and management purposes.
8. Reinvest only from funds that are genuinely available to the business.

## 5. Revenue Model and Recognition

Potential revenue streams should be modelled separately. They are not assumed to be active.

### 5.1 Marketplace commission

Potential formula:

`Platform Commission = Eligible Merchandise Value × Agreed Commission Rate`

The eligible value and rate must be defined in approved seller terms. Specify treatment of seller-funded discounts, platform-funded promotions, cancellations, partial refunds, disputes and taxes.

Commission should be recognised only when the relevant contractual and accounting conditions are met. Do not recognise a commission merely because an order was created or a payment was initiated.

### 5.2 Seller subscriptions

Potential formula:

`Subscription Revenue = Active Paid Plans × Price per Plan`

Define billing period, trial rules, upgrades, downgrades, failed payments, cancellations, refunds and service obligations. Do not assume subscription income before a paid plan is approved and customers are actually billed.

### 5.3 Promoted listings and advertising

Potential formula:

`Advertising Revenue = Valid Paid Campaigns × Contracted Campaign Price`

Define placement, duration, delivery evidence, refund rules and advertiser obligations. Protect customer trust and do not sell visibility that the product cannot reliably provide.

### 5.4 Fulfilment and logistics services

Potential sources may include disclosed hub handling, packaging, consolidation or delivery coordination charges, where the business is entitled to retain them.

Calculate the net contribution after direct costs:

`Logistics Contribution = Earned Logistics Revenue - Direct Logistics Costs`

Delivery charges collected for a carrier or rider may be pass-through funds, not Everneed revenue. Accounting treatment must reflect the actual contractual arrangement and applicable accounting standards.

### 5.5 Other business services

Future services such as merchant tools or integrations should enter the model only after their scope, price, delivery cost and customer demand have been established.

## 6. Marketplace Money Flow and Seller Balances

The financial model must distinguish customer money from Everneed's own earnings.

For each seller package, maintain a reconciliation of:

- Merchandise subtotal.
- Seller-funded discount.
- Platform-funded discount.
- Customer-paid delivery or logistics amount.
- Taxes, where applicable.
- Payment-provider fee and responsible party.
- Platform commission.
- Refunds, reversals and adjustments.
- Amount due to the seller.
- Any other deduction authorised by contract.

An illustrative control equation is:

`Customer Collection = Seller Payable + Earned Platform Amount + Other Payables/Pass-throughs + Refunds/Adjustments`

The exact accounting entries depend on the legal and payment structure. Obtain qualified accounting advice before deciding whether Everneed acts as principal, agent or payment intermediary for any flow.

**Required controls**
- Reconcile payment-provider transactions to internal order and settlement records.
- Keep seller liabilities identifiable and separate from operating cash.
- Do not use money owed to sellers to pay payroll, advertising or unrelated bills.
- Record every adjustment with reason, approver and supporting evidence.
- Match refunds and chargebacks to the original transaction.
- Use controlled approval for seller payout changes and manual adjustments.
- Investigate and resolve reconciliation differences promptly.

Do not enable real seller payouts until the payment structure, permissions, reconciliation and applicable regulatory requirements have been reviewed.

## 7. Order-Level Unit Economics

Everneed should calculate unit economics separately for:

1. A single-seller order delivered directly by the seller.
2. A single-seller order routed through the hub.
3. A multi-seller order consolidated at the hub.
4. A multi-seller order requiring more than one final parcel.
5. Cancelled, returned, refunded or failed-delivery orders.

### 7.1 Marketplace contribution

A practical management formula is:

`Marketplace Contribution per Order = Net Platform Revenue - Variable Marketplace Costs`

Variable marketplace costs may include:
- Payment-provider charges borne by Everneed.
- Order-related support cost, where measurable.
- Platform-funded promotions.
- Fraud, chargeback and refund costs borne by Everneed.
- Other transaction-dependent costs.

Do not subtract seller payable from revenue a second time if the accounting model already excludes seller funds from recognised platform revenue.

### 7.2 Fulfilment contribution

`Fulfilment Contribution = Earned Fulfilment Fees - Direct Fulfilment Costs`

Direct costs may include:
- Seller-to-hub transfer, if Everneed pays it.
- Hub receiving and handling labour.
- Sorting and package reconciliation.
- Packaging materials.
- Consolidation labour.
- Rider, courier or carrier charges.
- Reattempts, failed delivery and return transport.
- Claims, damage and loss costs.
- Other route-specific variable costs.

### 7.3 Combined contribution

`Combined Contribution = Marketplace Contribution + Fulfilment Contribution`

This is not net profit. It excludes fixed operating costs and may exclude tax, financing, depreciation and other overheads.

### 7.4 Key unit-economic measures

| Measure | Calculation |
|---|---|
| Average order value (AOV) | Merchandise value of completed orders ÷ completed orders |
| Average platform revenue per order | Net platform revenue ÷ eligible completed orders |
| Average fulfilment cost per parcel | Direct fulfilment costs ÷ parcels handled |
| Average delivery cost per completed delivery | Direct last-mile delivery costs ÷ completed deliveries |
| Order contribution | Relevant order revenue less relevant variable costs |
| Refund rate | Refunded order value or count ÷ corresponding completed-order value or count |
| Failed delivery rate | Failed delivery attempts or consignments ÷ relevant dispatched consignments |
| Seller acquisition cost | Attributable seller acquisition spend ÷ newly activated sellers |
| Customer acquisition cost | Attributable customer acquisition spend ÷ newly acquired purchasing customers |
| Repeat purchase rate | Customers with a repeat qualifying purchase ÷ customers in the defined cohort |

State the denominator and period for every metric. Do not combine orders, packages, parcels and delivery attempts as though they were the same unit.

## 8. Cost Structure

Potential costs:
- Hosting, databases, storage, bandwidth and monitoring.
- Domains, email and communications services.
- Payment and third-party integration fees.
- Developer tools and software subscriptions.
- QA devices and testing.
- Security reviews, backups and recovery.
- Engineering, design and maintenance support.

Separate one-time development expenditure from recurring service costs. Track free-tier limits and the expected cost when usage grows; do not assume a free tier will remain sufficient.

### 8.2 People and professional services

Potential costs:
- Employees and contractors.
- Recruitment and onboarding.
- Legal, accounting and tax services.
- Security, privacy and compliance advice.
- Training, equipment and connectivity.
- Statutory employment-related costs, where applicable.

Do not add proposed roles from the organisational plan to payroll until the hire is approved and engaged.

### 8.3 Marketplace operations

Potential costs:
- Seller onboarding and verification.
- Catalogue preparation and moderation.
- Customer support.
- Returns and dispute handling.
- Customer communications.
- Trust and safety processes.

### 8.4 Logistics and fulfilment

Potential costs, only if the relevant operation is approved:
- Hub rent, utilities and security.
- Shelving, scales, labels and handling equipment.
- Packaging supplies.
- Hub personnel.
- Seller collection or transfer.
- Delivery partner charges.
- Insurance and safety requirements.
- Returns, failed delivery and claims.

Do not include a physical hub's rent, staff or equipment as current expenditure unless those commitments actually exist. Model hub scenarios separately from a partner-only or direct-delivery model.

### 8.5 Growth and administration

Potential costs:
- Seller and customer research.
- Marketing tests and creative production.
- Referral incentives.
- Business communications.
- Registration, filings and professional fees.
- Banking and transaction charges.
- Travel and field visits.
- General administration.

Every marketing experiment should have a defined budget, objective, measurement period and stop condition.

## 11. Cash-Flow Forecast and Runway

Cash flow is a critical management tool. A profitable accounting result does not guarantee that cash is available when bills fall due.

### 11.1 Monthly cash forecast

For each month, record:

`Closing Cash = Opening Cash + Cash Inflows - Cash Outflows`

Cash inflows may include:
- Founder contributions actually received.
- Investment or grant funds actually disbursed and available for use.
- Customer payments to the extent they are legally and contractually Everneed's funds.
- Earned seller subscriptions, commissions and other revenue.
- Other documented operating receipts.

Cash outflows may include:
- Payroll and contractor payments.
- Infrastructure and software.
- Marketing and seller acquisition.
- Professional and statutory costs.
- Hub and delivery costs.
- Refunds, chargebacks and other obligations.
- Equipment and other approved capital expenditure.

Show restricted grant funds, seller payables and carrier pass-through amounts separately. Do not treat them as unrestricted operating cash.

### 11.2 Runway

A simple initial estimate is:

`Runway (months) = Unrestricted Available Cash ÷ Average Monthly Net Cash Burn`

This simplified formula is useful only when monthly burn is reasonably stable. A detailed forecast should instead identify the month in which unrestricted cash falls below the required reserve.

### 11.3 Cash controls

- Reconcile bank and payment accounts regularly.
- Maintain a rolling cash forecast.
- Review upcoming bills and contractual commitments.
- Keep receipts and invoices.
- Record founder contributions and loans separately.
- Avoid mixing personal and business funds.
- Use appropriate business banking arrangements when legally available.
- Set approval limits for expenditure and refunds.
- Maintain a minimum cash reserve based on actual obligations and risk.

## 12. Financial Scenarios

Prepare at least three scenarios. Do not assign numerical values until the assumptions have sources.

### Downside scenario
Test:
- Slower seller activation.
- Lower customer conversion and order frequency.
- Higher acquisition costs.
- Delayed product readiness.
- Payment or settlement delays.
- Higher delivery, refund or failed-delivery costs.
- Lower seller retention.
- Delayed funding.

### Base scenario
Use the most defensible assumptions supported by research, actual costs and early evidence. Do not simply use the founder's preferred outcome.

### Upside scenario
Test stronger seller activation, customer conversion and repeat usage, while including the additional support, infrastructure, fulfilment and working capital required to serve that demand.

For every scenario, show:
- Active sellers and customers.
- Orders and packages.
- Average merchandise value.
- Net platform revenue.
- Fulfilment revenue and direct costs.
- Fixed operating costs.
- Monthly net cash movement.
- Closing unrestricted cash.
- Funding requirement and runway.

An upside case must not assume that more orders are cost-free.

## 13. Break-Even Analysis

Break-even should be calculated only after Everneed has credible contribution and fixed-cost data.

A simplified formula is:

`Break-even Orders per Month = Monthly Fixed Operating Costs ÷ Average Contribution per Order`

Use this only if average contribution is positive and representative. If contribution varies materially by category, seller, order size or fulfilment route, calculate break-even using a weighted order mix or separate route/category analysis.

If average contribution is zero or negative, increasing order volume may increase losses. The priority should be to improve pricing, costs, order quality or the operating model—not simply increase marketing.

## 14. Financial Reporting and Controls

### Monthly management pack

Prepare a monthly pack containing:

1. Cash position and bank/payment reconciliations.
2. Actual-versus-budget report.
3. Profit and loss summary, with accounting treatment explained.
4. Cash-flow forecast and runway.
5. Marketplace order and revenue reconciliation.
6. Seller payable and payout reconciliation.
7. Logistics income, pass-through and cost report.
8. Refunds, chargebacks and unresolved variances.
9. Key unit economics and operating metrics.
10. Material commitments, risks and decisions required.

### Separation of duties

As the team grows:
- Separate payment initiation, review and approval where practical.
- Restrict access to banking and payment-provider accounts.
- Require evidence and approval for refunds and manual seller adjustments.
- Keep a record of who changed financial data and why.
- Review privileged access periodically.
- Use an independent accountant or reviewer for periodic checks when appropriate.

At the earliest stage, the founder may perform multiple duties. In that case, use documented reconciliations, supporting evidence and periodic independent review as compensating controls.

## 15. Nigerian Tax, Accounting and Legal Considerations

Before commercial operations, Everneed should obtain current advice from qualified Nigerian accounting, tax and legal professionals on the obligations that apply to its actual entity, activities, workforce, transaction flows and locations.

Areas for review may include:
- Appropriate CAC registration and business structure.
- Federal and state tax registration, filing and payment obligations.
- VAT treatment and invoicing, where applicable.
- Company income tax and other applicable taxes.
- PAYE and employment-related obligations, where applicable.
- Withholding tax treatment of relevant payments.
- Proper accounting for marketplace agency/principal arrangements.
- Treatment of seller funds, payment-provider settlements and refunds.
- Record retention and financial reporting.
- Consumer, data protection and payment-related obligations that affect financial operations.

Tax rates, thresholds, exemptions and filing requirements can change and depend on the facts. This plan intentionally does not state rates or legal conclusions. Confirm current requirements with professional advisers and official authorities before relying on them.

## 16. Funding Requirement and Use of Funds

The funding requirement should be derived from a defined milestone plan.

1. Identify the next business milestone and expected evidence.
2. List the work, people, services and operating resources required.
3. Obtain quotes or documented estimates for material costs.
4. Build monthly cash needs and payment timing.
5. Include a justified contingency.
6. Deduct only unrestricted cash and confirmed resources available for the plan.
7. Test downside scenarios and delayed receipts.
8. Calculate the capital required to complete the milestone and maintain a prudent reserve.
9. Specify what will be delivered, measured and reported.
10. Define actions if the milestone is delayed or the budget is exceeded.

Potential use-of-funds categories include product verification, QA and security, legal and financial preparation, seller/customer validation, controlled pilot operations, and approved fulfilment trials. The allocation must be based on actual quotes and priorities.

Do not announce a funding ask or valuation until this model is populated and reviewed.

## 18. Immediate Financial Priorities

1. Establish a simple record of every actual business-related receipt, contribution and expense.
2. Separate personal spending, company spending, seller obligations and delivery pass-through funds.
3. Collect current quotes for essential technology, legal, accounting and pilot costs.
4. Build a monthly operating budget using only approved commitments.
5. Define the accounting treatment for marketplace collections and seller balances with a qualified accountant.
6. Create order-level marketplace and fulfilment reconciliation before accepting live transactions.
7. Prepare downside, base and upside cash scenarios once evidence is available.
8. Update the funding strategy with a milestone-based capital requirement only after the model has been populated.

## 19. Review and Change Control

Review this plan:
- Monthly once regular financial activity begins.
- Before approving material recurring expenditure.
- Before setting seller fees or customer delivery charges.
- Before beginning a hub or delivery pilot.
- Before applying for funding or sharing financial projections.
- When tax, payment-provider or legal arrangements change.
- When actual results materially differ from forecast.

Record the assumptions changed, supporting evidence, decision owner and effective date. Keep previous forecasts so actual performance can be compared with what was expected at the time.
