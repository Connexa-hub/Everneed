# EVERNEED
## Investment Strategy & Capital Readiness Plan

**Document type:** Internal funding and investment strategy  
**Document status:** Strategic framework; no funding round, valuation or investment terms approved  
**Business status:** Founder-led startup; not yet registered with Nigeria's Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC)  
**Initial market:** Nigeria  
**Initial product:** Multivendor digital marketplace with a planned hub-supported fulfilment model  
**Project reference branch:** `web-portal`  
**Last updated:** November 2026

---

## 1. Purpose

This document sets out how Everneed should prepare for, evaluate and pursue external funding as the business develops.

The immediate objective is not to raise the largest possible amount. It is to build a credible, evidence-backed business, identify the capital required for the next measurable stage, and select funding that supports sustainable progress without creating avoidable ownership, repayment or governance risks.

This is an internal strategy. It is not an announcement of an active fundraising round, an offer of securities, a valuation, a financial forecast or a promise of investor returns.

## 2. Investment Position Today

Everneed is a founder-led startup building a digital commerce ecosystem. Its initial product is a multivendor marketplace intended to connect Nigerian shoppers with independent sellers through shared digital infrastructure.

The longer-term operating model includes a hub-supported fulfilment option for suitable multi-seller purchases and selected single-seller orders. The wider ambition includes additional business tools and commerce services, but the marketplace must be validated first.

The reviewed `web-portal` product status record describes a substantial implementation foundation across the Android application, public web experience, seller portal, admin console and Supabase integration. It also records that important production and operational workflows remain unverified.

Accordingly, Everneed should currently present itself to prospective funders as a **founder-led, pre-validation technology startup with product implementation underway**, not as a proven marketplace with established traction, recurring revenue or a live delivery network.

### What must not be represented as established without evidence

- CAC incorporation or other legal status not yet completed.
- A production-ready public marketplace.
- Verified end-to-end multi-seller checkout and payment settlement.
- Live seller payouts or completed financial reconciliation.
- An operating physical hub, owned delivery fleet or contracted delivery network.
- Confirmed active sellers, customers, orders, revenue or retention.
- Funding commitments, grants, partnerships or investor interest.
- A validated valuation, market share or forecast.

The founder should update this position whenever reliable new evidence becomes available.

## 3. Investment Thesis

Everneed's investment case should be built around a clear commercial hypothesis:

**Independent businesses need accessible digital storefronts and customer reach; shoppers benefit from discovering and buying from multiple sellers through a coherent marketplace; and coordinated fulfilment may make multi-seller purchases more convenient where the economics and operations support it.**

The potential opportunity has four connected parts:

1. **Marketplace infrastructure:** shared customer discovery, seller storefronts, catalogue, checkout and order management.
2. **Seller enablement:** tools and operating support that help smaller businesses participate in digital commerce.
3. **Fulfilment coordination:** a proposed hub-assisted model for receiving seller packages, checking and consolidating eligible orders, and coordinating final delivery.
4. **Future commerce services:** potential subscriptions, promoted listings, advertising, integrations and other merchant services after the core marketplace is proven.

This is a hypothesis to test, not proof of product-market fit. Investors should be shown the problem evidence, customer and seller behaviour, transaction quality, operating performance and unit economics that support each claim.

## 4. Funding Objectives

Capital should be raised against defined milestones rather than a broad aspiration to build an ecosystem.

Potential funding objectives, in sequence:

### Objective A — Complete and verify the product
- Finish critical customer, seller and administrator workflows.
- Validate authentication, access control and data isolation.
- Test multi-seller order creation and package separation.
- Verify payment events, order states, refunds and reconciliation in a controlled environment.
- Complete device, security, reliability and release testing.

### Objective B — Prepare and run a controlled pilot
- Complete legal and operational preparation.
- Recruit and activate a limited group of suitable sellers.
- Confirm a defined customer service area.
- Validate the chosen delivery and hub approach before making public promises.
- Provide customer support and operational issue resolution.
- Measure actual orders, fulfilment performance and customer feedback.

### Objective C — Demonstrate repeatable marketplace activity
- Improve seller activation and retention.
- Measure customer conversion and repeat purchase.
- Establish dependable order and package handling.
- Measure marketplace contribution and logistics contribution separately.
- Establish reliable financial controls and reporting.

### Objective D — Expand carefully
- Increase seller and customer density in the first market.
- Add operating zones only when capacity and demand justify them.
- Consider additional Nigerian markets before international expansion.
- Introduce additional revenue services only when customers or sellers demonstrate demand.

A funding proposal should specify which objectives the proposed capital will finance and what evidence will exist at the end of the funded period.

## 5. Funding Options and Recommended Sequence

Everneed should compare several sources. No source is guaranteed, and each has different eligibility, timing, cost and control implications.

| Funding source | Potential fit | Main consideration |
|---|---|---|
| Founder bootstrapping | Early validation, essential tools and limited operating costs | Protect personal finances; record founder contributions clearly |
| Customer / seller-funded revenue | Reinvesting genuine commercial income after operations begin | Do not treat gross collections or funds owed to sellers/carriers as company income |
| Grants and innovation programmes | Product development, inclusion, entrepreneurship, technology or local business outcomes | Eligibility, application effort, reporting, restrictions and disbursement timing |
| Incubators and accelerators | Mentorship, networks, structured validation and sometimes capital | Review equity, fees, programme terms, time commitment and actual value |
| Angel investors | Early capital, relevant expertise and introductions | Ownership dilution, governance rights, investor fit and legal documentation |
| Strategic partners | Commercial support, distribution, infrastructure or sector expertise | Avoid exclusivity or dependency without clear measurable value |
| Venture capital | Larger growth capital after evidence of a scalable market and model | High growth expectations, dilution, governance and future fundraising pressure |
| Debt or revenue-based finance | Potentially suitable for predictable cash flows or specific assets later | Repayment risk; avoid borrowing before revenue and cash-flow capacity are demonstrated |

### Recommended sequence

1. Keep early spending disciplined and prioritise product verification.
2. Explore non-dilutive grants, competitions and founder-support programmes where eligibility is genuine.
3. Consider accelerator participation where the network, expertise and terms are useful.
4. Build an angel-investment case around demonstrated pilot evidence and a defined use of funds.
5. Consider institutional venture capital only when market size, growth, retention, unit economics and expansion potential can support the expectations attached to it.
6. Use debt cautiously and only after a credible repayment source exists.

Do not make a financing decision solely because an application is open or an investor expresses informal interest.

## 6. Capital Strategy by Stage

| Stage | Business evidence expected | Capital approach |
|---|---|---|
| Product foundation | Working product components, test plan, security and release gaps documented | Founder resources, limited project-based support, eligible grants |
| Pilot readiness | Critical workflows verified, operating area and support model defined, legal preparation underway | Grants, accelerator support, carefully scoped angel discussions |
| Controlled pilot | Real sellers and customers, completed transactions, support and fulfilment records | Angel or strategic capital where it accelerates measurable validation |
| Repeatable operation | Retention, transaction quality, service performance and contribution economics | Larger seed investment may be considered |
| Geographic expansion | Repeatable launch playbook, local demand and proven operating economics | Institutional or strategic growth capital may become appropriate |

The stages are evidence gates, not promised dates or a statement that Everneed currently qualifies for a particular investment category.

## 7. Use-of-Funds Framework

Every funding proposal should contain a milestone-based budget. Potential categories include:

### Product and engineering
- Critical feature completion.
- Independent quality assurance and device testing.
- Security and privacy review.
- Hosting, monitoring, backups and developer tooling.
- Payment and integration testing.

### Market validation
- Seller discovery and onboarding.
- Customer research and controlled acquisition experiments.
- Catalogue preparation and seller education.
- Support processes and customer feedback.

### Fulfilment pilot
- Hub feasibility and setup, if approved.
- Package handling materials and tracking procedures.
- Delivery partner trials and route testing.
- Insurance, safety and operating controls where required.
- Returns, failed delivery and exception handling.

### Legal, finance and administration
- Appropriate business registration and professional advice.
- Contracts, privacy notices and seller/customer terms.
- Accounting, bookkeeping and reconciliation.
- Compliance and required insurance or statutory costs.

### Working capital and contingency
- Essential operating costs during the funded milestone period.
- A reasonable contingency based on identified risks.
- Cash reserves for timing differences and unexpected operating needs.

Do not allocate a large marketing budget before product reliability, seller supply and fulfilment capacity are sufficient. Do not count seller balances, delivery-partner pass-through collections or restricted grant funds as freely available operating cash.

## 8. Investment Readiness Gates

Before actively seeking equity investment, Everneed should assemble evidence in the following areas.

### 8.1 Company and legal readiness
- Confirm the appropriate legal entity and complete required registration before entering arrangements that require it.
- Clarify founder ownership and intellectual-property ownership.
- Prepare appropriate founder, contractor and employee agreements.
- Review company authority to issue shares or other investment instruments.
- Obtain qualified Nigerian legal and tax advice on the proposed transaction.
- Maintain accurate company records and a cap table once applicable.

Everneed's current profile records that the startup is not yet CAC-registered. Any change must be verified and reflected consistently before investor materials are updated.

### 8.2 Product readiness
- Maintain a current product status report tied to a specific branch, commit or release.
- Demonstrate customer, seller and admin workflows.
- Demonstrate secure seller data separation and administrator controls.
- Test multi-seller checkout, parent orders and seller packages.
- Verify payment, refund and reconciliation paths.
- Demonstrate backup, recovery, monitoring and incident response.

Screenshots and prototypes can explain the product, but they do not replace a working demonstration.

### 8.3 Market evidence
- Document seller interviews and the problems they describe.
- Record qualified, approved, active and retained sellers separately.
- Record customer research and actual shopping behaviour.
- Measure catalogue quality and product availability.
- Track completed and repeat transactions.
- Capture customer and seller feedback with appropriate permission.

Do not report sign-ups, waitlist entries or expressions of interest as completed transactions or active customers.

### 8.4 Operational evidence
- Document seller preparation and package handover.
- Test package receipt, reconciliation, consolidation and dispatch if hub operations are piloted.
- Record delivery success, delays, damage, loss and returns.
- Measure support response and resolution.
- Verify that advertised delivery coverage matches actual service capability.

### 8.5 Financial evidence
- Maintain separate records for marketplace revenue and logistics activity.
- Reconcile customer collections, payment fees, refunds, seller payables and delivery costs.
- Calculate contribution per order and per seller package.
- Record customer acquisition and seller onboarding costs.
- Maintain a cash forecast and budget-versus-actual report.
- Explain all material assumptions in forecasts.

## 9. Metrics Investors Should See

The reporting pack should prioritise a small set of reliable measures, with definitions and reporting periods.

| Area | Metric | Definition / caution |
|---|---|---|
| Seller supply | Approved sellers | Sellers that passed the required approval process |
| Seller activation | Active sellers | Sellers meeting a documented activity definition during the period |
| Catalogue | Published available products | Exclude drafts, rejected listings and unavailable stock |
| Customer demand | Active customers | Use a defined meaningful activity, not account creation alone |
| Conversion | Visitor-to-purchase conversion | State denominator, attribution window and completed-order definition |
| Transactions | Completed orders | Exclude failed, cancelled and unpaid orders |
| Multi-seller usage | Multi-seller parent orders | Count only orders with more than one valid seller package |
| Retention | Repeat purchase rate | Define cohort and measurement period |
| Marketplace economics | Net platform revenue | Exclude funds collected on behalf of sellers and pass-through amounts |
| Fulfilment | On-time delivery and package accuracy | Define promised time, completion event and exception treatment |
| Customer experience | Support resolution and complaints | Include volume and severity, not just average response time |
| Financial health | Contribution per order | Include relevant payment, support, fulfilment and promotional costs |

Every metric should have a named owner, source of truth, calculation and reporting period. Correct known data-quality problems before presenting a number externally.

## 10. Financial Model Requirements

Before setting a fundraising amount, prepare a financial model with:

- Monthly cash flow and opening/closing cash.
- Product, infrastructure and personnel costs.
- Seller acquisition and onboarding costs.
- Customer acquisition experiments.
- Order volume assumptions by seller and customer cohort.
- Average merchandise value, discounts and refunds.
- Platform commission assumptions, clearly labelled as unapproved until decided.
- Payment-provider charges and settlement timing.
- Seller payable balances and payout timing.
- Delivery charges, carrier/rider costs and hub handling costs.
- Failed delivery, returns, damage and claims assumptions.
- Tax and professional-service costs.
- Base, downside and upside scenarios.
- Funding requirement and runway under each scenario.

Separate the marketplace economics from fulfilment economics. Delivery collections are not automatically Everneed revenue or profit. Include only the amount the business is entitled to retain, and deduct the actual cost of providing the service.

Do not invent a commission rate, subscription price, average order value, customer acquisition cost, valuation or forecast. Build these from validated research and observed transactions.

## 11. Determining the Funding Ask

The funding amount should be calculated from a defined operating plan, not selected because it sounds attractive.

Use this sequence:

1. Choose the next milestone that materially reduces business risk.
2. Define the work and duration needed to reach it.
3. Estimate one-time and recurring costs.
4. Include realistic staffing, professional, infrastructure and operating costs.
5. Add a reasoned contingency.
6. Subtract cash and other resources genuinely available for that purpose.
7. Exclude seller funds, carrier pass-through collections and restricted funds.
8. Calculate the resulting funding requirement and expected runway.
9. Test a downside case with slower adoption, delayed payments or higher costs.
10. State what will be delivered and measured before the capital is exhausted.

The funding ask should be accompanied by a clear statement of what happens if the milestone is not achieved. Define stop, reduce, pivot or additional-funding decisions in advance.

## 12. Investor Targeting and Fit

Prioritise investors and programmes whose mandate aligns with Everneed's stage and business model.

Potential investor profiles:
- Nigerian or African early-stage technology angels.
- Investors familiar with marketplaces, commerce infrastructure, SME digitisation or logistics.
- Funds supporting African technology businesses at an appropriate stage.
- Strategic operators with relevant distribution, payments, commerce or fulfilment expertise.
- Grant makers and programmes with relevant entrepreneurship or digital-economy objectives.

Evaluate each prospective funder on:
- Stage and geography eligibility.
- Typical investment size and instrument, where publicly stated.
- Relevant portfolio and operating experience.
- Value beyond capital.
- Decision process and expected timeline.
- Governance expectations and reporting.
- Conflicts, exclusivity and commercial restrictions.
- Reputation and references from founders they have supported.

Do not pay an unverified intermediary for a guaranteed grant or investment. Verify the programme or investor through official channels and review all fees and terms independently.

## 13. Investor Materials

Prepare the following materials as the business becomes ready:

1. **Company overview:** problem, solution, positioning and current legal status.
2. **Pitch deck:** market problem, product, target customer, business model, go-to-market, evidence, competition, team, financial logic, funding ask and use of funds.
3. **Product demonstration:** a real, tested build or controlled demonstration with limitations disclosed.
4. **Market validation pack:** seller interviews, customer research, pilot evidence and cohort metrics.
5. **Financial model:** assumptions, cash flow, scenarios, unit economics and funding requirement.
6. **Product and technical status:** architecture overview, security controls, release evidence and known gaps.
7. **Operating model:** seller onboarding, support, order handling, fulfilment and delivery responsibilities.
8. **Legal and corporate documents:** registration records, ownership, contracts and approvals as applicable.
9. **Data room index:** controlled list of approved documents and evidence.

Maintain separate internal and external versions. Remove credentials, personal data, confidential seller information, security-sensitive details and unapproved commercial terms from investor-facing material.

## 14. Fundraising Process

When the founder approves an active fundraising process:

1. Confirm the milestone, amount, instrument and minimum acceptable terms.
2. Complete a readiness review and identify unresolved legal or financial gaps.
3. Prepare a concise, consistent investor narrative and evidence pack.
4. Build a targeted list of relevant investors and programmes.
5. Record introductions, meetings, questions, follow-ups and outcomes.
6. Provide only accurate information supported by the current evidence.
7. Obtain professional advice before signing a term sheet or investment agreement.
8. Complete due diligence and verify source of funds and counterparties.
9. Obtain all required corporate approvals and filings.
10. Record receipt, restrictions, investor rights and use of funds.
11. Report progress to investors according to agreed terms.

An informal conversation, verbal indication or draft term sheet is not completed funding. Do not announce an investment until it is legally committed and the relevant funds have been received, unless a carefully worded announcement is otherwise approved.

## 15. Investment Terms and Founder Protection

Do not agree to equity, valuation or control terms without understanding their long-term consequences.

Professional review should cover:
- Pre-money and post-money valuation.
- Share class and dilution.
- Option pools and future hiring needs.
- Board composition and observer rights.
- Reserved matters and investor consent rights.
- Information and inspection rights.
- Liquidation preference and participation.
- Anti-dilution provisions.
- Pro-rata and pre-emption rights.
- Founder vesting and leaver provisions.
- Transfer restrictions and exit rights.
- Intellectual-property representations.
- Warranties, indemnities and liability.
- Governing law, dispute resolution and regulatory filings.

Do not promise equity informally or issue shares before the entity, ownership records and legal process are properly established. Keep founder control and investor protections clear, fair and documented.

## 16. Funding Risks and Controls

| Risk | Control |
|---|---|
| Raising before the business has credible evidence | Tie fundraising to readiness gates and a specific milestone |
| Giving away excessive ownership too early | Model dilution and obtain independent legal and financial advice |
| Choosing investors who do not fit the business | Check stage, sector, reputation, terms and founder references |
| Treating grants as guaranteed cash | Confirm eligibility, award, conditions and actual disbursement |
| Spending ahead of product or operational readiness | Use milestone budgets and release funds against approved plans |
| Misstating traction or technical readiness | Maintain evidence-backed status and metric definitions |
| Confusing gross collections with revenue | Reconcile seller payables, carrier costs, refunds and platform income separately |
| Accepting restrictive strategic terms | Review exclusivity, data, IP, pricing and termination clauses |
| Borrowing without repayment capacity | Require conservative cash-flow analysis and a credible repayment source |
| Exposing confidential information during diligence | Use controlled access, redaction and a staged data-room process |

## 17. Immediate Priorities

1. Keep the current product status register accurate and tied to the reviewed `web-portal` branch.
2. Complete the critical technical and security verification work before making production-readiness claims.
3. Conduct structured seller and customer discovery and preserve the evidence.
4. Define a realistic first-market pilot only after the service area and fulfilment approach are confirmed.
5. Establish basic financial records and a model that separates marketplace and logistics economics.
6. Clarify legal registration, founder ownership and intellectual-property position with qualified Nigerian professionals.
7. Identify relevant grant and accelerator programmes without assuming eligibility or award.
8. Delay setting a valuation or public fundraising target until the funding milestone and supporting evidence are clear.

## 18. Review and Change Control

Review this strategy when:
- CAC or other relevant legal registration is completed.
- A controlled pilot begins or ends.
- Reliable transaction and retention data becomes available.
- The operating model or funding requirement materially changes.
- A grant, accelerator, angel or strategic funding opportunity is evaluated.
- The founder approves an active fundraising process.

Record material changes, their evidence and the decision owner. Ensure all investor materials use the same current company, product, legal and financial facts.
