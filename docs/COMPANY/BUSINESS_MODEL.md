## Business Model & Revenue Strategy

**Document type:** Company commercial model  
**Document status:** Working strategy — assumptions require market validation  
**Business status:** Founder-led startup; not yet registered with Nigeria's Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC)

## 1. Purpose

This document explains how Everneed intends to create value, how money may flow through the marketplace, and how the business can develop sustainable revenue without making the platform unnecessarily expensive or difficult for small sellers to join.

It is a decision framework, not a statement that all listed fees, payment flows or services are already active. No commission rate, subscription price or advertising price is approved by this document. Commercial terms must be tested with sellers and customers before launch.

## 2. Business Model in One View

Everneed is building a digital commerce ecosystem. Its first commercial engine is a multivendor marketplace.

| Participant | Value received | Expected contribution |
|---|---|---|
| Customer | Product discovery, access to multiple independent sellers, a connected shopping experience and order visibility | Purchases, repeat usage and feedback |
| Seller / merchant | A digital storefront, product visibility, access to customers and marketplace operating tools | Product information, accurate stock and pricing, fulfilment and customer service obligations |
| Everneed | Shared marketplace infrastructure, customer and seller network, platform operations and commercial services | Product development, platform administration, trust and safety, support and payment coordination |

The intended model is a platform connecting independent sellers and customers, supported by an Everneed-operated hub-and-delivery coordination model. Everneed's role may therefore include order consolidation, packaging coordination and delivery services in addition to marketplace infrastructure. Fees, payment handling and responsibility boundaries must be clear to customers and sellers.

## 3. Who Everneed Serves First

### Primary supply side: Nigerian small businesses

Initial seller segments may include independent retailers, small shops, specialist merchants and emerging online businesses that need a more accessible route to digital selling.

The first seller proposition should be simple:

- Establish a recognisable store on Everneed.
- Publish products with clear descriptions, prices and availability.
- Reach customers browsing a shared marketplace.
- Receive and manage orders through the available seller tools.
- Build a digital presence without having to create and maintain a standalone commerce platform.

Seller onboarding and product publication must be operationally reliable before broad acquisition campaigns begin.

### Primary demand side: Nigerian shoppers

The initial customer proposition is to discover products from multiple independent sellers in one marketplace, understand who is selling each item, and complete a clear purchase journey.

Trust, accurate product information, transparent delivery terms, responsive support and dependable order updates are essential parts of the value—not optional extras.

### Later markets

Ghana, Kenya, South Africa, the United Kingdom and the United States are potential future markets. They are not part of the initial operating assumption. Expansion requires local validation of payments, currencies, taxes, consumer rules, seller supply and fulfilment.

## 4. Value Creation and the Marketplace Loop

Everneed's marketplace becomes more useful as participation on both sides grows:

1. More suitable sellers create a wider and more relevant product selection.
2. Better product selection gives customers more reasons to browse and return.
3. Customer activity creates potential sales opportunities for sellers.
4. Successful seller experiences encourage retention and referrals.
5. A coordinated logistics network can make multi-seller purchases more convenient by bringing seller packages together at an Everneed hub before final delivery.\n6. Greater marketplace activity creates opportunities for paid services, provided those services improve outcomes rather than undermine trust.

This loop is a strategic hypothesis. It must be measured through active sellers, published and available products, customer conversion, completed orders, repeat purchases and seller retention—not downloads or registrations alone.

## 5. Revenue Model

Everneed should develop revenue in stages. The initial priority is to prove that sellers can receive genuine value and customers can complete successful purchases.

### 5.1 Transaction commission — proposed core revenue stream

Everneed may earn a commission on eligible completed marketplace transactions.

**Recommended principle:** calculate the platform commission from the eligible merchandise value of each seller's completed order package, using the commission terms disclosed to that seller.

A clear calculation should distinguish:

- Merchandise value.
- Discounts and promotions, including who funds them.
- Delivery or logistics charges.
- Payment-provider charges.
- Platform commission.
- Refunds, cancellations and adjustments.
- Final seller payable amount.

Do not assume that delivery charges, taxes or payment-provider fees are commissionable. Their treatment must be explicitly defined in seller terms and the checkout breakdown.

**Illustrative formula (variables, not approved rates):**

`Eligible Merchandise = Merchandise Subtotal - Seller	ext{-}funded Discounts`

`Platform Commission = Eligible Merchandise \times Agreed Commission Rate`

`Seller Proceeds = Collected Funds - Commission - Disclosed Deductions - Refunds/Adjustments`

The actual settlement formula must be aligned with payment-provider fees, applicable taxes, promotions, refunds and the legal payment structure.

**Commercial rules to establish before launch:**

- Whether commission varies by category or seller plan.
- Whether commission is charged on discounts and who funds each discount.
- When an order becomes commission-eligible.
- How partial fulfilment, partial refunds and cancellations affect commission.
- How failed, reversed or disputed payments are handled.
- When seller balances become eligible for payout.
- Which party is responsible for delivery and customer remedies.

Commission should be charged only according to clear, accepted terms. Do not describe an order as earned revenue merely because it was created or payment was attempted.

### 5.2 Delivery, hub handling and fulfilment services — core operating and potential revenue stream\n\nEverneed's delivery model is an important part of the marketplace proposition and may create a distinct source of revenue. It is not simply an external delivery link: for eligible orders, Everneed coordinates the movement of goods through its hub and the final delivery to the customer.\n\n#### A. Multi-seller orders: consolidate through an Everneed hub\n\nWhen a customer buys products from different sellers in one checkout, the intended operating flow is:\n\n1. The platform creates one customer-facing parent order and separate seller packages or sub-orders.\n2. Each participating seller prepares the items belonging to that seller's package and sends or drops them at the designated Everneed hub, following the agreed handover process.\n3. The hub receives and checks the incoming packages against the relevant order and seller records.\n4. Everneed consolidates the items for that customer, performs the agreed packaging and dispatch preparation, and creates one combined delivery consignment where practical.\n5. The consolidated order is dispatched to the customer's delivery address, with status updates and proof of handover/delivery recorded.\n\nThis model is intended to reduce the inconvenience of receiving several separate deliveries for one multi-seller purchase. If items cannot safely or practically be consolidated, the system must support clearly identified separate parcels rather than falsely promising one package.\n\n#### B. Single-seller orders: direct delivery or hub-assisted delivery\n\nWhen all purchased items come from one seller, the seller may deliver directly to the customer where the distance, service area and seller capability make that practical. The seller may instead route the order through an Everneed hub when the destination is far, the seller requests hub assistance, or the platform's operational rules determine that hub handling is more suitable.\n\nThe checkout and order workflow should identify the selected fulfilment route, responsible delivery party, expected delivery charge and tracking method. A seller should not be forced into a hub route where direct delivery is the agreed and suitable option.\n\n#### C. Potential logistics revenue components\n\nEverneed may earn revenue from clearly disclosed logistics services, including:\n\n- **Customer delivery charge:** the amount charged for moving the consolidated or single-seller order to the customer.\n- **Hub handling / consolidation fee:** a disclosed service charge for receiving, checking, sorting and combining packages where applicable. This may be included in the customer delivery price or shown separately, depending on the approved pricing design.\n- **Packaging service fee:** a charge for packaging materials and preparation where this is a separately priced service.\n- **Seller-to-hub transfer charge:** where a seller uses an arranged pickup or transfer service, a disclosed charge may apply to that leg. If the seller drops the package at the hub themselves, this transfer charge would not automatically apply.\n- **Partner delivery coordination margin:** where a third-party carrier is used, Everneed may earn a disclosed coordination margin only where contractually permitted and transparently priced.\n\nThe amount collected for delivery is not automatically Everneed's profit. Carrier charges, rider payments, hub labour, packaging materials, failed-delivery costs, insurance or loss allowances, and other fulfilment expenses must be deducted when calculating logistics contribution. If Everneed merely collects a carrier's charge on the carrier's behalf, the accounting treatment may be pass-through rather than platform revenue; confirm this with an accountant.\n\n#### D. Pricing and service rules\n\nBefore launch, Everneed must define how delivery pricing is calculated, including distance bands or zones, parcel size/weight, number of seller packages, hub handling, special handling, remote destinations, return journeys and failed delivery. Avoid double-charging customers for a consolidated journey simply because several sellers contributed items.\n\nThe platform should show the customer the total delivery cost before payment and explain any separately charged hub or packaging service. Seller terms should state who pays for seller-to-hub movement, packaging, damaged or missing items before hub acceptance, and return-to-seller movement.\n\nHub staff should record package receipt, condition, seller/order reference, consolidation, dispatch and handover. Define custody and responsibility at each handover point, including loss, damage, mismatch, late arrival and uncollected packages.\n\nThis is the intended business operating model; it is not evidence that a physical hub, delivery fleet, carrier contract, live routing system or logistics workflow is already operational. Hub locations, staffing, operating hours, service areas, delivery partners, insurance, pricing and service-level commitments remain to be established and validated.\n\n### 5.3 Seller subscriptions — later, optional revenue stream

Subscriptions may offer enhanced services to sellers who have already experienced value from the basic marketplace.

Potential paid benefits, subject to product development and seller research, include:

- Enhanced storefront presentation.
- Additional store-management or reporting tools.
- Higher operational limits where justified by real platform costs.
- Advanced promotional tools.
- Business insights and performance reporting.
- Additional support services.

**Recommended entry strategy:** keep a useful basic seller experience available without a mandatory subscription during initial marketplace validation. Do not charge sellers simply to access demand that Everneed has not yet demonstrated.

A paid tier should have a specific, understandable benefit and should not imply guaranteed sales, ranking or customer reach.

### 5.4 Promoted listings — after marketplace traffic develops

Sellers may pay to promote eligible products or stores in clearly identified advertising placements.

This stream should only be introduced when Everneed has enough relevant customer activity to provide meaningful visibility.

Requirements:

- Sponsored placements must be clearly labelled.
- Paid placement must not override product safety, seller approval or relevance rules.
- Sellers must understand the placement, duration, price and targeting.
- Promotion performance should be measurable.
- Organic discovery must remain useful and must not become pay-to-play by default.

### 5.5 Advertising — future opportunity

Everneed may offer advertising inventory to relevant brands and businesses as audience scale and measurement capability develop.

Advertising is not an early revenue assumption. It depends on audience quality, sufficient traffic, privacy safeguards, clear labelling, advertiser controls and a user experience that remains useful.

### 5.6 Business services — future opportunity

As the ecosystem matures, Everneed may offer additional services or integrations that solve demonstrated merchant problems. Examples for exploration include business tools, operational integrations, logistics partnerships and merchant services.

Each service should have a clear customer, provider, support model, cost structure and compliance review before being commercialised.

## 6. Recommended Revenue Sequence

| Stage | Revenue focus | Decision gate |
|---|---|---|
| Initial validation | Test seller economics and delivery costs; map the hub workflow and service areas | Sellers understand the proposition and the delivery model can be costed |
| Controlled beta | Test transparent transaction commission and delivery/hub charges on a limited operating area | Order, hub custody, delivery, payment and refund records reconcile reliably |
| Early growth | Test optional seller subscriptions and limited promoted placements | Sellers show repeat usage and can identify value from paid benefits |
| Established marketplace | Expand advertising and business services selectively | Audience, measurement, support and compliance are sufficient |
| Ecosystem expansion | Introduce additional commerce services or market-specific models | Each new service has validated demand and sustainable unit economics |

This is a sequence of strategic gates, not a dated launch commitment.

## 7. Payments, Orders and Seller Settlement

Payment handling is central to the business model. A marketplace must never treat the amount paid by a customer as if the entire amount belongs to Everneed.

The platform's commercial records should be able to reconcile, for each parent order, seller package and delivery consignment:

1. Customer payment amount and payment status.
2. Items, quantities and agreed prices.
3. Discounts and the party funding each discount.
4. Delivery charges and their intended recipient.
5. Payment-provider charges.
6. Platform commission and applicable adjustments.
7. Refunds, reversals and disputes.
8. Seller payable balance and payout status.

### Multi-seller order treatment

Where a customer buys from multiple sellers in one checkout, the commercial records should preserve both:

- One customer-facing parent order for the overall purchase.
- Separate seller packages or sub-orders for each participating seller.

Each seller's proceeds, commission, package readiness, hub handover, fulfilment status, refund exposure and payout eligibility must be traceable to that seller's package. The combined customer delivery should have its own consignment record linked to the parent order and all contributing seller packages. One seller's private financial and customer data must not become visible to another seller.\n\nFor a single-seller order, the record must identify whether fulfilment is direct from seller to customer or routed through an Everneed hub. The selected route must determine the applicable charges, custody events, delivery responsibility and tracking updates.

### Settlement principles

Before real-money beta, Everneed must define:

- Whether funds are collected by Everneed, a payment provider, or another legally appropriate arrangement.
- Whether the payment provider supports the intended marketplace settlement model.\n- How customer delivery charges, hub handling and packaging fees are collected and allocated.\n- How sellers deliver or drop packages at the hub, and when responsibility for package custody transfers.\n- Whether Everneed uses its own riders, contracted carriers or a mixed delivery network.\n- How proof of delivery, failed delivery, returns and damaged/missing parcels are handled.
- The event that makes seller proceeds payable (for example, confirmed fulfilment or an agreed settlement milestone).
- Payout frequency, minimums, fees and failed-payout handling.
- Reserves or delayed settlement where justified by refunds, disputes or fraud risk.
- The process for reconciling payment-provider records with platform ledgers.

Do not hold, pool, split or remit customer funds in a way that creates regulatory or contractual obligations without obtaining appropriate Nigerian legal and payment-provider guidance. Payment-provider capabilities and approval must be confirmed rather than assumed.

## 8. Cost Structure

Everneed's main cost categories are expected to include:

| Cost category | Examples | Cost discipline |
|---|---|---|
| Product and engineering | Development, testing, hosting, maintenance and security | Prioritise reliability and essential marketplace workflows |
| Infrastructure | Database, storage, bandwidth, monitoring and backups | Monitor usage and avoid unnecessary resource consumption |
| Payments | Provider processing, refunds, reversals and dispute costs | Model actual provider fees and reconcile every transaction |
| Seller operations | Onboarding, verification, moderation and merchant support | Create repeatable processes and clear standards |
| Customer support | Enquiries, complaints, returns and order issues | Track response time and resolution quality |
| Growth | Content, campaigns, referrals and acquisition experiments | Measure cost per activated customer and completed order |
| Administration and compliance | Registration, accounting, contracts, tax and professional advice | Budget for obligations as commercial activity develops |
| Hub and fulfilment operations | Hub premises, staff, receiving/sorting, packaging materials, equipment, security and utilities | Cost each hub operation and measure throughput and handling errors |\n| Delivery operations | Seller-to-hub transfers, riders/carriers, fuel, dispatch, insurance, failed delivery and returns | Track cost and service quality by route and delivery leg |

Costs should be separated into fixed operating costs, variable transaction costs and one-off setup costs. This makes it possible to understand whether more orders improve or weaken the business's economics.

## 9. Unit Economics and Performance Measurement

Everneed should measure the economics of completed activity rather than rely on gross order value alone.

### Core definitions

- **GMV (Gross Merchandise Value):** value of merchandise sold through the marketplace over a defined period, using a consistent policy for cancellations, discounts and refunds.
- **Net platform revenue:** platform revenue earned from commissions and other services, less relevant revenue reversals and adjustments, using the company's accounting policy.
- **Contribution per order:** net revenue attributable to an order minus variable costs directly associated with serving that order.\n- **Logistics contribution per consignment:** logistics revenue attributable to a delivery consignment minus the direct costs of seller-to-hub movement, hub handling, packaging, last-mile delivery, failed delivery and returns.
- **Active seller:** a seller meeting a defined activity test, such as having an approved store and at least one available published product or a completed sale during the reporting period. Report the exact definition used.
- **Completed order:** an order meeting the company's documented completion and settlement criteria, not merely an initiated checkout.

### Metrics to track

| Area | Metric | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Seller supply | Approved sellers; active sellers; active stores | Measures usable supply, not just applications |
| Catalogue | Published products; in-stock product rate | Measures customer choice and availability |
| Customer activity | Active customers; product views; add-to-cart rate | Measures meaningful engagement |
| Conversion | Checkout conversion; paid orders; completed orders | Measures whether browsing becomes commerce |
| Retention | Repeat purchase rate; seller retention | Measures durable value |
| Marketplace volume | GMV; average order value | Measures transaction scale |
| Revenue | Commission revenue; delivery and hub service revenue; subscription revenue; promotion revenue | Shows the source and quality of income |\n| Logistics | Delivery revenue per consignment; hub cost per package; delivery cost per kilometre/zone; on-time delivery; failed delivery; damage/loss rate | Tests whether fulfilment is reliable and financially sustainable |
| Economics | Contribution per order; variable cost per order | Tests whether growth is sustainable |
| Operations | Cancellation, refund, dispute and failed-payment rates | Measures reliability and risk |
| Service | Support response and resolution times | Measures customer and seller experience |

### Basic contribution model

`Contribution per Order = Net Platform Revenue - Variable Order Costs`

Variable costs may include payment processing, transaction-related infrastructure, support effort, refunds, promotions funded by Everneed, hub labour, packaging, seller-to-hub transfer, last-mile delivery, failed delivery and returns, plus other costs directly caused by serving the order. Track marketplace contribution and logistics contribution separately as well as together.

Do not count seller proceeds as Everneed revenue. Do not count GMV as platform revenue.

### Validation targets

The master roadmap contains proposed early validation targets (including seller, store, product, customer and transaction goals). Those targets are hypotheses for planning, not achieved results. They should be reviewed against actual activation, completed transactions, repeat behaviour and support capacity.

## 10. Seller Pricing Principles

Everneed's pricing should follow these principles:

1. **Transparent:** sellers can understand every charge before accepting an order or service.
2. **Proportionate:** charges should reflect the value and cost of the service.
3. **Accessible:** initial participation should not create unnecessary financial barriers for small merchants.
4. **Predictable:** avoid hidden deductions and unexplained changes.
5. **Earned:** introduce paid upgrades only when the benefit is real and demonstrable.
6. **Fair:** commission, promotion and ranking rules should be consistent and documented.
7. **Reversible where appropriate:** refunds and adjustments should be reflected clearly in seller statements.

Any pricing experiment should have a defined cohort, duration, success measure and communication plan. Do not change seller economics silently.

## 11. Trust, Responsibility and Commercial Policies

The business model depends on clear responsibilities. Before public commercial launch, Everneed needs written policies covering:

- Seller eligibility, identity checks and store approval.
- Product listing standards and prohibited products.
- Product accuracy, stock and pricing obligations.
- Customer payments and payment failures.
- Delivery responsibility, service expectations and tracking.
- Cancellations, returns, refunds and disputes.
- Seller commission, deductions and payout terms.
- Promotions, sponsored placements and advertising disclosures.
- Customer and seller data handling.
- Complaints, escalation and account suspension.

The exact allocation of legal responsibility between Everneed, sellers, payment providers and delivery partners must be reviewed for the chosen operating model and applicable Nigerian requirements.

## 12. Key Business Model Risks

| Risk | Potential effect | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Low seller activity | Marketplace appears empty or unreliable | Recruit for product relevance and seller readiness, not registration count alone |
| Low customer demand | Sellers see little value | Validate specific customer segments and acquisition channels |
| Unclear fulfilment ownership | Delays, disputes and customer distrust | Define custody, service levels and responsibility at seller, hub and customer handovers |\n| Hub congestion or package mismatch | Delayed consolidation, missing items or incorrect parcels | Use package labels, scan/receipt records, sorting controls and capacity limits |\n| Delivery costs exceed charges | Logistics losses as order volume grows | Cost each route and service leg; review pricing and carrier performance |
| Payment and settlement failures | Financial loss and seller dissatisfaction | Reconcile provider events, order states and ledger entries |
| Excessive early fees | Sellers do not join or remain active | Keep entry accessible and validate willingness to pay |
| Premature advertising | Poor customer experience and weak advertiser results | Wait for relevant traffic and measurement |
| Refunds and fraud | Negative contribution and cash-flow pressure | Establish controls, review processes and settlement rules |
| Expansion too early | Operational complexity and cost | Prove the initial Nigerian model before entering new markets |

## 13. Decisions Still Required

The following are open commercial and operational decisions. They should be resolved through research, product capability review and professional advice—not silently treated as settled policy.

- [ ] Confirm the initial seller categories and ideal seller profile.
- [ ] Interview prospective sellers about current selling costs and willingness to pay.
- [ ] Decide whether the first controlled beta uses zero commission, a trial commission or another clearly disclosed arrangement.
- [ ] Determine the commission base and treatment of discounts, delivery and payment fees.\n- [ ] Decide which logistics charges are customer-facing, seller-facing or included in a combined delivery price.\n- [ ] Define hub receiving, custody, consolidation, packaging, dispatch and proof-of-delivery procedures.\n- [ ] Decide the first hub location(s), operating area, capacity and opening hours.\n- [ ] Choose the initial delivery model: own riders, contracted carriers or a hybrid arrangement.\n- [ ] Cost direct seller delivery against hub-assisted delivery for single-seller orders.
- [ ] Confirm the legally and operationally appropriate payment collection and settlement structure.
- [ ] Define order completion, refund, dispute and payout rules.
- [ ] Confirm whether seller subscriptions are excluded from the initial beta.
- [ ] Define the minimum evidence required before introducing promoted listings.
- [ ] Build a simple monthly financial model using actual provider fees and operating costs.
- [ ] Review contracts, consumer obligations, tax and payment regulations with qualified Nigerian advisers.

## 14. Immediate Execution Priorities

1. **Map the money and goods flow:** document checkout, seller preparation, seller-to-hub handover, consolidation, packaging, dispatch, customer delivery and seller settlement, including failures, returns and refunds.\n2. **Design the hub operating process:** define package identification, receiving checks, custody transfer, sorting, consolidation, dispatch and proof of delivery for multi-seller orders.\n3. **Cost the delivery model:** calculate direct delivery and hub-assisted routes separately, including hub labour, packaging, rider/carrier costs, failed deliveries and returns.
4. **Validate seller economics:** speak with a small group of target sellers and record their expectations, concerns and alternatives.
5. **Verify platform capability:** check the current `web-portal` implementation and backend for order, payment, commission, ledger, refund and payout support. A screen or planned schema is not proof of a working process.
6. **Model one completed order:** calculate GMV, platform revenue, seller proceeds and variable costs using clearly labelled sample assumptions.
7. **Approve beta pricing:** make a founder decision only after the operating and legal questions are sufficiently resolved.
8. **Review monthly:** compare assumptions with real seller activation, completed orders, repeat purchases, contribution and operational issues.

## 15. Document Control

This document is a working business strategy. It should be updated when Everneed approves pricing, confirms payment and settlement arrangements, validates seller demand or changes its commercial scope.

Changes to commission, subscription, promotion or settlement policies should also be reflected in the relevant seller terms, product requirements and customer-facing disclosures before they take effect.

**Guiding principle: Build seller value first, earn from proven value, and expand revenue only when the marketplace can deliver it reliably.**
