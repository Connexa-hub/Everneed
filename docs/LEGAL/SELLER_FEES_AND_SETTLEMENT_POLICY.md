## 1. Purpose and status

This policy explains the proposed principles for seller charges, marketplace commission, optional paid services, transaction records, seller proceeds, settlement, refunds, adjustments and disputes on Everneed.

Everneed's commercial principle is to **earn by creating measurable value**. Sellers should understand what they are paying, why a charge applies, how it is calculated and when proceeds are expected to be settled. The platform must not rely on hidden fees, arbitrary deductions, compulsory upgrades or unexplained withholding of seller funds.

This document is a framework, not an approved price list, executed seller contract, payment-provider approval or evidence of a live settlement service. It does not set a commission rate, subscription price, delivery tariff, payout timetable, minimum payout threshold or reserve percentage. Those items remain subject to founder decision, legal/accounting advice, provider confirmation and written seller acceptance where required.

**No charge is authorised by a blank field or by this policy alone.** The executed Seller Marketplace Agreement and its completed commercial schedule must identify each applicable charge and be accepted before it is applied.

Everneed's internal records currently describe the venture as not yet CAC-registered. The correct legal contracting and payment-receiving entity must be confirmed before binding commercial arrangements or live marketplace settlement begin.

## 3. Commercial fairness principles

Everneed should apply the following principles to every seller:

1. **Clear before commitment:** disclose a fee, rate, calculation base, tax treatment and charging event before the seller accepts the relevant commercial terms.
2. **No hidden deductions:** every debit must have a transaction reference, itemised calculation and contractual or legal basis.
3. **No compulsory basic access charge under the proposed initial model:** the current draft seller agreement provides for no joining/application fee and no basic-store access fee. Any change requires an explicit founder decision, legal review, advance disclosure and seller acceptance before it applies.
4. **No forced subscription or advertising:** paid plans and promotions must be optional unless a distinct paid service is separately and transparently agreed.
5. **No commission on non-qualifying sales:** cancelled, unpaid, failed, fully refunded or reversed transactions do not attract commission under the intended default model.
6. **No retrospective fee changes:** a new or increased charge must not be applied retrospectively to orders already accepted.
7. **No charge for unused services:** sellers must not pay for an optional service they did not select or receive.
8. **No forced discounts:** sellers control whether they participate in promotions and must know who funds the discount.
9. **No arbitrary penalties:** operational problems must be investigated and responsibility established before a cost is allocated.
10. **No indefinite or unexplained holds:** any permitted hold must have a defined basis, be communicated where legally possible, reviewed periodically and be open to challenge.
11. **Separate disputed and undisputed amounts:** where the payment structure and law permit, an unrelated disputed amount should not prevent settlement of an undisputed amount.
12. **Accurate statements:** sellers should be able to reconcile order value, discounts, taxes, commission, delivery, refunds, provider charges and net proceeds.
13. **No misuse of seller funds:** seller proceeds must not be treated as Everneed's operating cash.
14. **Fair correction and appeal:** sellers must have a practical route to question a calculation, deduction, refund allocation or settlement delay.

A seller may be responsible for a direct, evidenced cost caused by the seller's own breach or conduct only where the executed agreement expressly permits recovery, the allocation is lawful and proportionate, and the seller receives notice and a fair opportunity to challenge it.

## 4. Fee categories and approval rules

### 4.1 Seller application, joining and basic store access

The current working Seller Marketplace Agreement schedule states:
- Seller joining/application fee: none under the draft.
- Basic store access fee: none under the draft.

The recommended initial approach is to keep basic seller participation accessible while Everneed validates marketplace demand and seller value. This is a proposed commercial position, not a permanent guarantee or a live price announcement.

No joining or basic access charge may be introduced through a website label, dashboard toggle or informal message. It requires:
- Founder approval.
- Legal and tax review.
- A clear explanation of what the seller receives.
- Advance notice and seller acceptance before the new charge applies.
- An exit route for sellers who do not accept the new commercial terms, subject to open orders and legal obligations.

### 4.2 Marketplace commission

Commission is a possible revenue stream for completed marketplace sales. It must be modest, transparent, commercially sustainable and based on a clearly defined event and calculation base.

**Commission rate: NOT SET.** No percentage or flat amount is approved by this policy.

Before commission can be charged, the commercial schedule must specify:
- Rate and currency.
- Calculation base (for example, eligible product value, with explicit treatment of seller-funded discounts, platform-funded discounts, tax, delivery and other charges).
- Whether commission is calculated per item, seller package or qualifying order.
- The event at which commission is earned.
- Treatment of partial fulfilment, partial refunds, returns, chargebacks and reversals.
- Applicable VAT or other tax treatment, based on qualified advice.
- Rounding rules and examples.
- Effective date and seller acceptance record.

#### 4.2.1 Intended qualifying sale rule

The intended default is that commission becomes due only on a **Qualifying Completed Sale**, as defined in the executed seller agreement and commercial schedule.

The schedule must define completion using reliable order and delivery evidence. It must not leave material ambiguity about:
- Seller-direct delivery.
- Hub-assisted delivery.
- Multi-seller orders with separate packages.
- Partial delivery.
- Customer complaints or unresolved disputes.
- Returns and the relevant return/refund period.
- Payment reversals and chargebacks.

Under the intended default:
- No commission is charged on an unpaid order.
- No commission is charged on an order cancelled before qualifying completion.
- No commission is charged on a failed or reversed transaction.
- No commission is charged on a fully refunded sale.
- A partial refund requires a proportional, itemised commission adjustment according to the agreed calculation.
- An unresolved complaint or payment dispute must not be treated as an undisputed completed sale for deduction purposes.

The exact treatment must be legally reviewed and written into the seller schedule before commission is activated.

### 4.3 Optional subscriptions and seller plans

Everneed may later offer paid plans where the added service provides real, understandable value.

Potential benefits for future research include:
- Enhanced storefront presentation.
- Additional catalogue or store-management tools.
- Advanced analytics or reporting.
- Additional promotional controls.
- Higher operational limits where justified by real service costs.
- Enhanced support or business services.

No subscription price, tier, renewal period or seller entitlement is approved by this document.

Before offering a plan:
- Describe each included benefit and material limitation.
- State the price, billing period, tax and payment method.
- Explain renewal, cancellation, expiry, grace period and downgrade.
- Do not promise sales, ranking, reach or earnings.
- Do not remove basic seller access merely to pressure an upgrade.
- Obtain affirmative seller consent.
- Provide a clear receipt and subscription status.
- Make cancellation reasonably accessible.
- Handle refunds and failed renewals under disclosed terms and applicable law.

### 4.4 Promoted listings and advertising

Promoted listings and advertising must remain optional and must be clearly labelled to customers.

Before charging a seller or advertiser, disclose:
- Placement and format.
- Audience or targeting criteria, where used.
- Price and billing basis.
- Start/end date or impression/click limits.
- What counts as a billable event.
- Cancellation, pause and refund conditions.
- Reporting limitations.
- Any category eligibility and moderation requirements.

Payment must not override seller approval, product safety, relevance or legal restrictions. Do not sell guaranteed ranking or sales unless a specific, measurable and supportable service has been defined and approved.

### 4.5 Delivery, hub and fulfilment charges

Delivery and fulfilment charges must relate to a service actually selected and provided.

Potential charge categories, if later approved, include:
- Customer delivery charge.
- Seller-to-hub collection or transfer.
- Hub intake, handling or consolidation.
- Additional packaging materials or repacking.
- Return transport.
- A disclosed coordination service margin where contractually permitted.

The route, charging party, calculation method, amount and tax treatment must be disclosed before acceptance. Do not double-charge a customer for one consolidated journey simply because multiple sellers contributed items.

A seller must not be charged for hub handling or transfer that it did not select or use. A customer must not be charged a new mandatory amount after checkout without the required disclosure and approval.

The amount collected for delivery is not automatically Everneed revenue or profit. Carrier/rider payments, hub labour, packaging, returns, failed attempts, insurance and other costs must be reconciled. Pass-through carrier amounts must be distinguished from any separately earned Everneed service revenue.

### 4.6 Payment-provider and payout fees

Any payment processing, transfer, payout, refund or chargeback fee must be:
- Confirmed against the provider's current commercial terms.
- Allocated to the correct party in the executed agreement.
- Disclosed before it is charged to the seller.
- Recorded with the provider transaction reference.
- Reconciled against actual provider statements.

Do not invent a provider fee or apply a generic percentage where the provider's actual pricing differs. Do not pass a provider charge to a seller where the seller agreement does not permit it.

### 4.7 Taxes and statutory deductions

Tax treatment depends on the legal entity, supply, seller status, payment flow and current Nigerian tax law. Obtain advice from a qualified Nigerian tax professional.

The commercial schedule should state:
- Whether a quoted fee is tax-inclusive or tax-exclusive.
- Which party issues the relevant invoice or receipt.
- Whether VAT or another tax applies.
- Whether a withholding or statutory deduction is legally required.
- How the deduction is calculated, remitted and evidenced.
- Which tax certificate or statement the seller may receive.

Everneed must not label a deduction as “tax” without identifying its legal basis and supporting record. Seller tax obligations and platform tax obligations must be distinguished.

## 5. Order-level money calculation

Every order should have an auditable transaction record that separates product value, delivery and service charges, provider costs, platform revenue and seller proceeds.

A typical statement may show, where applicable:

| Line item | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Product subtotal | Value of goods supplied by the seller before applicable adjustments |
| Seller-funded discount | Discount funded by the seller under an accepted promotion |
| Platform-funded discount | Discount funded by Everneed, recorded separately from seller proceeds |
| Customer delivery charge | Amount paid for the disclosed delivery service |
| Hub/handling/packaging | Separately disclosed service charge, if applicable |
| Tax | Applicable tax shown with its basis and recipient |
| Payment-provider charge | Actual provider charge, allocated under the agreed terms |
| Refund/reversal | Full or partial amount returned or reversed |
| Commission | Approved rate applied to the agreed calculation base after the qualifying event |
| Other adjustment | Itemised amount with contractual basis and evidence |
| Seller net proceeds | Amount payable to seller after lawful, agreed adjustments |

This table is a statement structure, not a fixed calculation formula. The actual formula must be approved and documented in the commercial schedule.

Do not:
- Count customer delivery collections as seller product revenue.
- Count seller proceeds as Everneed revenue.
- Count pass-through carrier money as unrestricted platform cash.
- Deduct commission twice across a parent order and its seller sub-orders.
- Apply a fee to the wrong seller package.
- apply an unexplained rounding adjustment.
- calculate commission on tax or delivery by default without an expressly approved basis.

## 6. Seller settlement and payout framework

### 6.1 Approved payment structure is a prerequisite

Customer payments, seller proceeds, split settlement, refunds and payouts must use a payment structure permitted by applicable law and approved by the selected licensed payment provider for the actual marketplace model.

This policy does **not** authorise Everneed to provide banking, deposit-taking, payment services, a stored-value wallet, escrow or money transmission. Everneed must not hold or use seller funds as its own operating cash.

Before live marketplace transactions, Everneed must obtain:
- Written confirmation of the provider's supported marketplace/merchant structure.
- Confirmation of who is the merchant of record or contracting payee for each flow, as advised.
- Provider-approved rules for split settlement, commissions, refunds and seller payouts.
- A documented treatment of chargebacks, reserves, failed transfers and reconciliation.
- Qualified Nigerian payments/legal advice on the proposed flow.
- Accounting advice on recognition and safeguarding of seller amounts.

A code integration, API key, webhook endpoint or successful test payment does not by itself prove that the commercial settlement structure is approved or that seller payouts are operational.

### 6.2 Settlement timetable

**Settlement timetable: NOT SET.**

Do not promise “instant,” daily, weekly or any other payout schedule until the selected provider has confirmed its settlement behaviour and the complete flow has been tested.

The final schedule must disclose:
- When the seller's sale becomes eligible for settlement.
- The relevant provider settlement dependency.
- Processing days and cut-off times, if applicable.
- Weekend and public-holiday treatment.
- Bank or provider delay conditions.
- Whether partial or item-level settlement is supported.
- How the seller is notified of a failed or returned payout.

The customer delivery event, payment provider's settlement event and Everneed's internal order status may occur at different times. The seller-facing statement must not confuse them.

### 6.3 Seller settlement details

Sellers must provide accurate bank or approved settlement details through a secure, access-controlled process. Everneed may request information reasonably needed to verify the seller and route payment.

Everneed must never ask a seller to disclose:
- Card PIN.
- One-time password (OTP).
- Online banking password.
- Full card security credentials.
- Unrelated account login details.

Changes to settlement details should require proportionate verification and an audit record. A change request must not be accepted solely on the basis of an unverified message from an account that may be compromised.

### 6.4 Seller statements

Provide sellers with a clear statement for each relevant settlement period, subject to product and provider capability.

A statement should include:
- Seller and store reference.
- Statement period and currency.
- Parent order and seller package references.
- Item/product subtotal.
- Discounts and who funded them.
- Qualifying sale event and date.
- Commission rate, base and calculation.
- Delivery or optional service charges, if applicable.
- Provider charges, where allocated to the seller.
- Refunds, reversals and chargebacks.
- Taxes or statutory deductions and their basis.
- Other adjustments with references and explanations.
- Gross amount and net amount due.
- Amount settled, date, provider reference and status.
- Pending, disputed or held amount and the reason where disclosure is lawful.

A seller must be able to reconcile the statement to individual orders. Do not provide only a single unexplained “balance” number.

### 6.5 Failed or returned payouts

If a payout fails or is returned:
1. Record the provider response and reference.
2. Notify the seller without exposing sensitive banking information.
3. Confirm whether the cause is incorrect details, provider/bank rejection, compliance review or a technical failure.
4. Provide a secure correction route where needed.
5. Retry only in accordance with provider instructions and duplicate-payment controls.
6. Keep the amount recorded as payable or pending according to the approved legal/accounting structure.
7. Do not treat a failed payout as Everneed revenue.

Any provider retry fee or seller charge must be disclosed and contractually authorised. Do not charge a seller for a platform or provider error solely outside the seller's control.

## 7. Holds, reserves and delayed settlement

### 7.1 No indefinite withholding

No seller proceeds may be held indefinitely or without an identifiable basis.

A hold or reserve may be used only where it is:
- Permitted by applicable law and the provider's contract.
- Included in the seller agreement or otherwise lawfully authorised.
- Reasonably necessary for a documented risk or obligation.
- Proportionate to the issue.
- Recorded with the affected amount, reason and review date.
- Communicated to the seller where legally permitted.
- Periodically reviewed and released when the basis ends.

Potential grounds may include a provider-imposed reserve, legal or regulatory requirement, credible suspected fraud, account compromise, chargeback exposure, a genuine transaction dispute or a documented refund obligation.

A general preference to retain cash, improve Everneed's working capital or pressure a seller is not a valid reason.

### 7.2 Hold notice and review

A notice should state, where lawful:
- The affected transaction or amount.
- The reason category and relevant contractual clause.
- Whether all or only part of the seller's proceeds is affected.
- What evidence or action is required from the seller.
- The next review date or review process.
- How the seller can challenge the decision.

Do not disclose confidential fraud-detection methods or information prohibited by law, but provide as much meaningful explanation as can safely be given.

Where only one transaction is disputed, do not hold unrelated undisputed proceeds if the provider structure and law allow separate settlement.

### 7.3 Reserves

**Reserve percentage, duration and release schedule: NOT SET.**

Do not create a reserve by informal practice. Any reserve must be provider-approved, legally reviewed, contractually disclosed and supported by a risk rationale. Define how it is calculated, which transactions it covers, the maximum review interval, release conditions and dispute process.

## 8. Refunds, returns, reversals and chargebacks

Refunds must follow the Returns, Refunds, Cancellations & Disputes Policy, customer terms, payment-provider rules and applicable law.

### 8.1 Refund accounting

For every refund or reversal, record:
- Parent order and affected seller package/item.
- Original payment reference.
- Refund amount and reason.
- Whether the refund is full or partial.
- Party responsible under the evidence and contract.
- Provider refund reference and status.
- Effect on commission and seller proceeds.
- Any provider fee or chargeback amount and its allocation basis.
- Customer communication and case reference.

Do not describe a refund as completed until the provider confirms completion.

### 8.2 Commission adjustment

Under the intended default:
- A fully refunded sale has no commission due.
- A partial refund requires an itemised proportional adjustment under the approved commission formula.
- A cancellation, payment reversal or failed payment does not attract commission.
- A later chargeback must be reconciled against the original transaction and the agreed risk allocation.

Do not recover the same amount twice through both a refund adjustment and a separate debit.

### 8.3 Chargeback and disputed payment

A chargeback must be assessed using the provider's rules, transaction evidence, delivery evidence, seller records and customer complaint history.

Do not automatically allocate every chargeback to the seller. Determine whether the cause relates to:
- Seller conduct or product fulfilment.
- Everneed's own system or representations.
- Payment-provider processing.
- Hub or carrier handling.
- Customer unauthorised use or other circumstances.
- An unresolved or shared cause.

Any seller debit must be permitted by the executed agreement, supported by evidence, proportionate, itemised and open to challenge. Statutory customer rights and provider rules remain applicable.

## 9. Deductions, adjustments and seller disputes

### 9.1 Permitted adjustment standard

Every deduction or adjustment must have all of the following:
1. A clear contractual or legal basis.
2. A transaction/order reference.
3. A calculation that can be checked.
4. Supporting evidence.
5. A description in the seller statement.
6. Notice to the seller, where legally permitted.
7. A process to challenge the amount.

Examples of potentially valid adjustments, only where agreed and lawful, include commission on a qualifying completed sale, an accepted optional service, an evidenced seller-caused direct cost, a properly allocated refund or a statutory deduction.

### 9.2 Prohibited practices

Everneed must not:
- Invent an administrative or penalty charge after an event.
- Deduct an unapproved commission rate.
- Apply fees retrospectively.
- charge a seller for a service it did not choose or receive.
- Deduct an amount solely because a customer complained, without investigation.
- Make a seller pay for a platform, hub or carrier failure solely caused by Everneed or its appointed provider.
- Apply a blanket deduction to every seller for a shared incident without evidence.
- Offset unrelated seller funds without a lawful and disclosed basis.
- Hold all seller proceeds because a small amount is disputed, where separate settlement is permitted.
- Treat a seller's payable balance as platform revenue.
- Change an order's commission calculation after the sale without an agreed legal basis.

### 9.3 Seller dispute process

Seller dispute channel: [insert monitored channel]  
Settlement support owner: [assign before launch]  
Acknowledgement and review targets: [set only after operational testing]

A seller should provide:
- Seller/store reference.
- Statement period.
- Transaction or order reference.
- Amount disputed.
- Reason for the challenge.
- Relevant supporting evidence.

Everneed should:
1. Issue a case reference.
2. Confirm the amount and transaction under review.
3. Preserve the relevant order, payment, provider and delivery records.
4. Review the calculation against the accepted commercial schedule.
5. Request further evidence only where reasonably needed.
6. Correct a verified error and update the seller statement.
7. Explain a decision not to adjust the amount.
8. Provide an appeal route, preferably to a reviewer not responsible for the original decision.

Do not promise a resolution time until the team and provider dependencies can meet it consistently.

## 10. Seller invoices, receipts and accounting records

The final invoicing model must be confirmed by a Nigerian accountant after the legal entity, merchant structure, tax treatment and provider flow are established.

The records should distinguish:
- Customer receipts for goods supplied by the seller.
- Everneed's commission or service invoices.
- Delivery and hub charges.
- Provider charges.
- Refunds and credit notes.
- Taxes collected or remitted.
- Seller proceeds payable.
- Amounts paid to sellers.
- Disputed or held amounts.

Everneed must not issue an invoice that falsely presents it as the supplier of a seller's goods where it is not. Sellers must receive the information needed to meet their own invoicing and tax obligations, subject to the agreed model and law.

Maintain accurate records of orders, fees, statements, provider settlements, payouts, refunds, adjustments and disputes for the retention period determined by applicable law and the approved records schedule.

## 11. Security and access controls

Financial and payout information requires restricted access.

Controls should include:
- Role-based access to seller statements and settlement details.
- Separation of ordinary support access from authority to change payout details or approve financial adjustments.
- Strong authentication for privileged accounts.
- Audit logs for rate changes, fee configuration, manual adjustments, holds and payout actions.
- Dual review for high-risk or exceptional financial actions where practical.
- Secure storage and transmission of bank details and provider references.
- No secrets, API keys or credentials in seller-facing statements or logs.
- Prompt revocation of access when a worker or provider no longer needs it.
- Monitoring and incident response for suspicious payout changes or account takeover.

A frontend control or hidden admin button is not a substitute for backend authorisation. Financial actions must be validated server-side and tested against unauthorised access.

## 12. Changes to rates, fees and terms

A commission rate, calculation base, mandatory service charge, settlement rule or material risk allocation must not change retrospectively.

Before a material change:
1. Approve the commercial rationale and effective date.
2. Complete legal, tax and provider review.
3. Update the seller agreement and commercial schedule.
4. Explain the change in plain language.
5. Give reasonable advance notice.
6. Obtain affirmative seller acceptance where the change materially increases obligations or the agreement requires it.
7. Preserve the accepted version and timestamp.
8. Allow a seller who does not accept to stop taking new orders and terminate under the agreement, subject to open orders and legal obligations.

Do not use a continued login or a preselected checkbox as acceptance where clear affirmative consent is required.
