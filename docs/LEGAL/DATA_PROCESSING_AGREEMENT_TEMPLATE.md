## 1. How to use this template

This Data Processing Agreement (**DPA**) is intended for use where one party processes personal data on behalf of another party in connection with an identified service. It must be adapted for each vendor, seller, delivery partner, hub operator, support provider or other service provider.

Do not attach this DPA indiscriminately to every Everneed relationship. A party that independently determines the purposes and essential means of its processing may be an independent controller rather than a processor. A payment service provider may, for example, act as an independent controller for some regulated payment, fraud-prevention or compliance activities. A seller may be an independent controller for its own customer relationship and legal obligations. Actual functions, not contractual labels, determine the appropriate role.

## 3. Definitions and interpretation

3.1 **Applicable Data Protection Law** means the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 (**NDPA**), the Nigeria Data Protection Commission's applicable General Application and Implementation Directive 2025 (**GAID 2025**), and other data-protection laws or binding regulatory requirements applicable to the relevant processing, including any lawful requirements in a country where affected individuals are located.

3.2 **Personal Data**, **Data Subject**, **Processing**, **Controller**, **Processor**, **Personal Data Breach** and other statutory terms have the meanings given by Applicable Data Protection Law.

3.3 **Documented Instructions** means the written instructions in this DPA, its completed schedules, the underlying services agreement and subsequent written instructions issued by the Controller's authorised contact. Instructions must be lawful and sufficiently clear.

3.4 **Subprocessor** means another processor engaged by the Processor to carry out specific processing activities on behalf of the Controller.

3.5 **Security Incident** means an actual or reasonably suspected event affecting the confidentiality, integrity, availability or security of Personal Data or systems used to process it. A Security Incident is not necessarily a legally reportable Personal Data Breach; the Parties must assess it promptly.

3.6 Headings are for convenience. References to laws include amendments and replacements. If there is a conflict, the Parties must apply the order of precedence stated in the underlying services agreement, subject always to mandatory law and the protections in this DPA.

## 4. Roles, scope and processing particulars

4.1 For the processing described in Schedule 1, the Controller determines the purposes and essential means of processing and the Processor processes Personal Data only on the Controller's behalf and under Documented Instructions.

4.2 The Parties must separately assess any activity for which either Party determines its own purposes and essential means. Such activity must not be incorrectly treated as processor activity merely to obtain the benefit of this DPA. Where the Parties are independent controllers, they must document their respective responsibilities in a separate controller-to-controller arrangement or suitable contract terms.

4.3 Schedule 1 must specify, at minimum:
- Subject matter and nature of processing.
- Purpose(s) and permitted operations.
- Duration and service period.
- Categories of Data Subjects.
- Types of Personal Data and any sensitive or high-risk data.
- Processing locations and access locations.
- Frequency, scale and relevant systems.
- Retention and deletion instructions.
- Whether the Processor may access production data or only test data.

4.4 The Processor must not begin processing until Schedule 1 and the security and subprocessor schedules have been completed and approved by authorised representatives.

## 5. Controller obligations and lawful instructions

5.1 The Controller is responsible for determining and documenting the lawful basis, purposes, transparency information, retention rules and other controller obligations applicable to its processing.

5.2 The Controller must provide instructions that are lawful, proportionate, sufficiently specific and consistent with the agreed services.

5.3 The Controller must not instruct the Processor to collect, disclose, retain or otherwise process Personal Data unlawfully. The Controller must provide only the data reasonably necessary for the service.

5.4 The Controller must provide relevant privacy notices and ensure that any required consent, authorisation or other legal condition is obtained before the Processor is asked to perform the relevant activity.

5.5 The Controller remains responsible for decisions reserved to it by law, including decisions about purposes, lawful bases, responses to Data Subject requests and regulatory engagement, except to the extent a statutory duty applies directly to the Processor.

## 6. Processor obligations

The Processor shall:

6.1 Process Personal Data only on Documented Instructions and only for the agreed service. The Processor must not use Personal Data for its own purposes, sell it, disclose it for unrelated commercial benefit, enrich unrelated profiles, train general-purpose models, create independent advertising audiences or conduct unrelated profiling unless a separate lawful basis and written arrangement expressly authorise that distinct activity.

6.2 Immediately notify the Controller if, in the Processor's reasonable opinion, an instruction infringes Applicable Data Protection Law. The Processor may suspend the affected instruction where reasonably necessary to avoid unlawful processing, while promptly explaining the issue and cooperating on a lawful alternative.

6.3 Ensure that persons authorised to process Personal Data:
- Are bound by enforceable confidentiality obligations or an appropriate statutory duty.
- Receive role-appropriate privacy and security training.
- Have access only to data necessary for their assigned duties.
- Use approved accounts, devices and systems.
- Do not share credentials or export data to unauthorised locations.

6.4 Maintain appropriate technical and organisational measures under clause 8 and Schedule 2, taking account of the nature, scope, context and purposes of processing, the state of the art, implementation cost and risks to individuals.

6.5 Not appoint a Subprocessor except in accordance with clause 9 and Schedule 3.

6.6 Assist the Controller, taking account of the nature of processing and information available to the Processor, with Data Subject requests, security obligations, breach response, data-protection impact assessments, prior consultation where required, regulatory enquiries and compliance evidence.

6.7 Keep records reasonably necessary to demonstrate compliance with this DPA and provide relevant information on request, subject to security, confidentiality and lawful restrictions.

6.8 Promptly notify the Controller if the Processor can no longer meet a material obligation under this DPA, or if a legal or technical change materially affects the agreed processing.

6.9 Not intentionally combine Controller Personal Data with data obtained for unrelated customers or purposes except where technically necessary, documented, access-controlled and lawful.

6.10 Not disclose Personal Data to a public authority or other third party unless required by law or authorised by the Controller. Where legally permitted, notify the Controller before disclosure, verify the legal demand, disclose only what is required and preserve a record.

## 7. Confidentiality and personnel

7.1 The Processor shall restrict access to authorised personnel with a genuine service-related need.

7.2 The Processor shall ensure personnel understand the confidential nature of Personal Data, permitted use, security procedures and incident-reporting route.

7.3 The Processor shall promptly revoke access when a person changes role, leaves the service or no longer needs access.

7.4 Confidentiality obligations survive termination for as long as the information remains confidential or protected by law.

7.5 The Processor shall not permit unapproved contractors, temporary staff or support personnel to access production Personal Data.

## 8. Security measures

8.1 The Processor shall implement and maintain security measures appropriate to the risks and shall document them in Schedule 2. The measures must be reviewed periodically and after material incidents or changes.

8.2 Depending on the service and risk assessment, measures should include:

- **Governance:** named security owner, documented policies, risk assessment, asset and data-flow inventory, supplier review and change control.
- **Access control:** unique user accounts, least privilege, role-based permissions, strong authentication, multi-factor authentication where supported and appropriate, periodic access reviews and prompt deprovisioning.
- **Secrets and credentials:** secure secret storage, no hard-coded production credentials, rotation and restricted access to API keys, signing secrets and administrative credentials.
- **Encryption:** encryption in transit using current secure protocols; encryption at rest where supported and appropriate; protected key management and restricted key access.
- **Application security:** secure development practices, code review, dependency and vulnerability management, input validation, output encoding, server-side authorisation and protection against common web and API vulnerabilities.
- **Database security:** least-privilege service roles, row-level or equivalent access controls where appropriate, tested authorisation boundaries, restricted administrative access and protected backups.
- **Logging and monitoring:** security-relevant audit logs, privileged-action logs, alerting for suspicious access, time synchronisation and controls against unauthorised log alteration. Avoid placing passwords, payment credentials or unnecessary Personal Data in logs.
- **Availability and recovery:** tested backups, restoration procedures, resilience planning and documented recovery priorities.
- **Endpoint and network protection:** supported software, secure configuration, malware protection where appropriate, device encryption, patching and secure remote access.
- **Data handling:** minimisation, secure transfer, approved storage locations, controlled exports, secure disposal and prevention of unauthorised removable-media use.
- **People and physical security:** staff screening where lawful and proportionate, training, visitor controls and protection of premises and devices.
- **Testing:** periodic security testing proportionate to risk, remediation tracking and verification of fixes.

8.3 The Processor shall not materially reduce the agreed security level during the term without prior written notice and the Controller's approval where the reduction affects the risk or agreed service.

8.4 The Processor shall promptly address vulnerabilities according to severity and risk, and notify the Controller of a vulnerability that materially affects Controller Personal Data or the service.

8.5 The Parties acknowledge that no security measure guarantees that an incident will never occur. This does not reduce either Party's legal obligations or the Processor's contractual duties.

## 10. International transfers and remote access

10.1 The Processor shall not transfer, host, permit remote access to, or otherwise make Personal Data available outside Nigeria unless the transfer or access has been assessed and authorised in accordance with Applicable Data Protection Law and the Controller's documented instructions.

10.2 Before a proposed international transfer, the Processor shall provide:
- Destination country and receiving entity.
- Purpose, data categories, Data Subjects and transfer frequency.
- Hosting, support and remote-access locations.
- The applicable lawful transfer basis or mechanism.
- Any required assessment of the destination's protection level and supplementary safeguards.
- Contractual protections, onward-transfer restrictions and deletion arrangements.

10.3 The Parties shall identify and document the lawful transfer condition or mechanism required by the NDPA and applicable GAID provisions. A vendor's general statement that it operates internationally, or the existence of standard contractual terms, does not by itself establish that a particular transfer is lawful.

10.4 The Processor shall not change the approved data location, introduce a new overseas support location or permit onward transfer without the approval required by this DPA.

10.5 If a transfer mechanism becomes invalid, inadequate or unavailable, the Parties shall promptly assess alternatives and suspend the affected transfer where necessary to prevent unlawful processing.

## 11. Data Subject rights and requests

11.1 The Processor shall promptly forward to the Controller any request, complaint or communication from a Data Subject concerning Controller Personal Data, unless the Processor is legally required to respond directly.

11.2 The Processor shall not independently disclose, correct, erase, restrict, export or otherwise act on a request except:
- On the Controller's Documented Instructions;
- Under an agreed response procedure; or
- Where required by law, in which case the Processor shall notify the Controller where legally permitted.

11.3 The Processor shall provide reasonable technical and organisational assistance to enable the Controller to respond within applicable statutory time limits. The Parties shall agree operational response targets in Schedule 5. These contractual targets must leave sufficient time for the Controller's legal assessment and response and must not purport to extend a statutory deadline.

11.4 Assistance may include locating records, verifying relevant identifiers, exporting data in a usable format, correcting records, applying restrictions, deleting data and documenting actions.

11.5 The Controller remains responsible for assessing identity, the scope and validity of a request, applicable exemptions, third-party rights and the final response, except where a statutory duty applies directly to the Processor.

## 12. Personal Data Breaches and security incidents

12.1 The Processor shall notify the Controller **without undue delay** after becoming aware of a Personal Data Breach affecting Controller Personal Data, and shall use the incident contact and communication method in Schedule 5.

12.2 Initial notification must not be delayed until every fact is known. To the extent available, it should state:
- Nature and approximate time of the incident.
- Date and time detected and when the Processor became aware.
- Systems, services and data categories affected.
- Approximate number or categories of affected Data Subjects and records, if known.
- Likely consequences and risk indicators.
- Containment and mitigation measures taken or proposed.
- Whether data was accessed, altered, lost, disclosed, encrypted or made unavailable.
- Relevant forensic evidence and incident reference.
- Contact for ongoing coordination.

12.3 The Processor shall provide further information in phases as the investigation develops, preserve relevant evidence, maintain an incident chronology and cooperate with the Controller's assessment and response.

12.4 The Processor shall not notify Data Subjects, regulators, media or other third parties about an incident on the Controller's behalf without prior written authorisation, unless legally required. If legally required to notify independently, it shall notify the Controller in advance where lawful and coordinate to avoid inaccurate or inconsistent statements.

12.5 The Controller shall determine and discharge its own statutory notification obligations, including whether notification to the Nigeria Data Protection Commission or affected individuals is required and within what period. The Processor must provide information and assistance quickly enough to support those obligations.

12.6 The Processor shall not conceal, misclassify or minimise an incident to avoid notification. Neither Party shall make a public statement that is misleading or prejudices the investigation.

12.7 The Processor shall, at its own cost where the incident resulted from its breach of this DPA or its negligence or wilful misconduct, take reasonable containment, investigation and remediation steps, subject to the final liability allocation and applicable law.

12.8 Schedule 5 must contain monitored 24-hour escalation details where the service risk warrants them. Do not insert a contact route that is not staffed and tested.

## 13. DPIAs, risk assessments and regulatory cooperation

13.1 The Processor shall provide reasonable assistance, information and access needed for the Controller to conduct a data-protection impact assessment, legitimate-interest or other risk assessment, records review, security assessment or prior consultation where required.

13.2 The Processor shall promptly inform the Controller of processing changes that may create a high risk to individuals, including new sensitive data, large-scale monitoring, novel profiling, new integrations, material changes in purpose or new international access.

13.3 The Processor shall cooperate with lawful enquiries from the Nigeria Data Protection Commission or another competent authority relating to the agreed processing. The Processor shall promptly notify the Controller of an enquiry unless prohibited by law.

13.4 The Parties shall identify who owns each regulatory response, who may communicate with the authority and how evidence will be preserved. Nothing in this DPA prevents a Party from complying with a legal obligation imposed directly on it.

## 14. Data accuracy, minimisation and privacy by design

14.1 The Controller shall determine the data required for the purpose and avoid unnecessary or excessive collection.

14.2 The Processor shall not require additional Personal Data merely for convenience, speculative future use or unrelated product development.

14.3 The Processor shall promptly correct or flag data it knows to be inaccurate where the correction is within its role and instructions.

14.4 The Processor shall support privacy by design and default, including restricted default access, minimal collection, limited retention, safe defaults and privacy-preserving testing.

14.5 Production Personal Data must not be copied into development, demonstration or test environments unless specifically authorised, necessary, protected and documented. Synthetic or properly anonymised data should be preferred.

14.6 The Processor shall not re-identify data that has been irreversibly anonymised, or attempt to circumvent agreed aggregation or de-identification controls.

## 15. Retention, return and deletion

15.1 The Processor shall retain Personal Data only for the period and purpose set out in Schedule 6 or otherwise instructed in writing.

15.2 On expiry or termination of the service, or earlier on the Controller's written request, the Processor shall, at the Controller's choice:
- Return the Personal Data in an agreed commonly usable format; and/or
- Securely delete or irreversibly anonymise it,

unless retention is required by applicable law. Any required retention must be limited to the legally required data, purpose and period, segregated from ordinary processing and protected against other use.

15.3 The Processor shall complete return or deletion within [agreed period] after the relevant instruction and provide written confirmation by an authorised representative.

15.4 Backup copies that cannot reasonably be deleted immediately shall be placed beyond ordinary use, protected from restoration except for disaster recovery, and overwritten or securely deleted in accordance with a documented backup lifecycle of [insert verified period]. If restored, the deletion instruction must be reapplied.

15.5 The Processor shall identify data retained under a legal hold, the legal basis, scope, access restrictions and review date, where disclosure is lawful.

15.6 The Processor shall ensure that approved Subprocessors return or delete Personal Data on equivalent terms and obtain confirmation where reasonably available.

15.7 Deletion must include active systems, exports, temporary files, support attachments and relevant Subprocessor environments to the extent technically and legally applicable. The Processor shall document any technical limitation and compensating control.

## 16. Audit and compliance evidence

16.1 On reasonable written notice, the Processor shall provide information reasonably necessary to demonstrate compliance with this DPA, including relevant policies, security summaries, certifications, independent audit reports, penetration-test summaries, subprocessor information and incident records, subject to lawful redaction and confidentiality.

16.2 Where documentary evidence is insufficient to address a material risk, the Controller or an independent auditor bound by confidentiality may conduct a proportionate audit of relevant systems, premises, controls and records.

16.3 Audits shall:
- Be limited to the processing and systems relevant to this DPA.
- Be scheduled during normal business hours where practicable.
- Avoid unreasonable disruption and exposure of other customers' data.
- Follow reasonable security and confidentiality requirements.
- Not require access to source code, third-party confidential material or unrelated systems unless genuinely necessary and legally approved.
- Protect privileged, security-sensitive and other customers' information.

16.4 The Processor shall cooperate with lawful regulatory inspections and provide evidence relevant to the processing, subject to applicable law.

16.5 The Parties shall agree in Schedule 7 the ordinary audit frequency, notice period, cost allocation and treatment of urgent audits. An urgent audit may be required after a material incident, credible evidence of non-compliance or regulatory direction.

16.6 A certification or audit report is evidence only of the scope, period and controls it actually covers. It is not a blanket guarantee of compliance.

## 17. Assistance, service changes and records

17.1 The Processor shall maintain records of processing activities to the extent required by law and reasonably necessary to evidence its work under this DPA.

17.2 The Processor shall keep an up-to-date description of systems, data flows, access roles, processing locations and approved Subprocessors relevant to the service.

17.3 The Processor shall notify the Controller before a material change to the service, architecture, hosting region, support access, data categories, retention, security controls or Subprocessor arrangements.

17.4 The Parties shall document new processing purposes, new categories of Personal Data, material feature changes and new integrations before deployment.

17.5 The Processor shall not use Controller Personal Data to benchmark, advertise or develop a separate product except with prior written authorisation and a lawful, transparent arrangement. Aggregated or anonymised service metrics may be used only where the method prevents identification and is approved in writing.

## 18. Marketplace-specific allocation guidance

The following are assessment prompts, not automatic role determinations:

| Participant / service | Role questions requiring actual assessment |
|---|---|
| Everneed platform operator | May be a controller for account administration, platform security, marketplace governance, customer support and its own business records. Identify the legal person actually operating each function. |
| Independent seller | May be a separate controller for its own sale, fulfilment, customer communications, tax and legal records. It may receive limited customer data to fulfil a specific order. Do not automatically label every seller a processor. |
| Payment provider | May be an independent controller for regulated payment processing, fraud prevention, compliance and its own records; may have a processor role for other narrowly defined activities. Review provider terms and data flows. |
| Hosting, database and infrastructure supplier | May be a processor or subprocessor for hosted data, subject to its actual service, contract, access, configuration and locations. Verify the deployed region and support access. |
| Hub operator / fulfilment contractor | May process customer and order information on instructions for receiving, sorting, custody and dispatch, while acting independently for its own employment, safety, tax or legal records. |
| Courier / rider / delivery company | May be an independent controller for parts of delivery, safety, route, fraud or legal records, or a processor for a tightly instructed service. Determine the role per activity and contract. |
| Customer support provider | May be a processor for support-ticket handling on instructions; any independent analytics, workforce or legal processing must be separately assessed. |
| Analytics, advertising or communications provider | Determine whether it receives personal identifiers, device identifiers, event-level data or only properly anonymised statistics. Do not enable tracking or audience sharing merely because an SDK is available. |

For Everneed's proposed multi-seller journey, the customer-facing parent order may contain seller-specific sub-orders or packages. The Parties must document which party may access the customer's name, phone number, delivery address, order contents, package reference and delivery instructions at each stage. Sellers should receive only the information needed for their own package and obligations; one seller must not access another seller's order, customer or earnings data without a lawful and necessary basis.

For hub-assisted fulfilment, document each handover, permitted recipient, custody record, package label, access role, retention period and incident route. Do not treat a proposed hub workflow as an existing data flow until a real hub and service are approved and tested.

## 19. Liability, indemnity and insurance

19.1 Each Party remains responsible for its own obligations under Applicable Data Protection Law. Nothing in this DPA transfers or removes a statutory duty that the law places on a particular Party.

19.2 The Parties shall allocate contractual responsibility for losses, claims, regulatory costs, remediation and third-party claims in the underlying services agreement, taking account of each Party's role, fault, causation, control and applicable law.

19.3 Any liability cap, exclusion, indemnity, insurance requirement or limitation must be completed in the underlying agreement after qualified legal and insurance review. Do not insert a blanket exclusion for data-protection breaches or purport to cap liability where doing so would be unlawful or unenforceable.

- Separate cap or uncapped categories, if lawful: [insert].

## 20. Term, suspension and termination

20.1 This DPA begins on the effective date and continues while the Processor processes Personal Data for the Controller under the underlying service.

20.2 The Controller may require the Processor to suspend affected processing where there is a material security risk, unlawful instruction, unauthorised transfer, material breach or regulatory direction. Suspension must be proportionate and documented.

20.3 A Party may terminate this DPA or the affected service if the other Party materially breaches it and fails to remedy the breach within [insert reasonable cure period], where the breach is capable of remedy. Immediate suspension or termination may be appropriate where continued processing would be unlawful or create serious risk.

20.4 Termination of the DPA does not excuse return, deletion, confidentiality, incident cooperation, audit evidence or other obligations intended to survive.

20.5 On termination, the Processor shall follow clause 15 and Schedule 6. The Parties shall agree a secure transition plan where service continuity or migration is required.

This DPA and any non-contractual obligations arising from it shall be governed by the laws of the Federal Republic of Nigeria, subject to mandatory data-protection law and any mandatory jurisdictional rules.

Disputes shall follow the escalation and dispute-resolution procedure in the underlying services agreement: [insert procedure, venue and competent courts or agreed lawful alternative]. Nothing prevents a Data Subject or regulator from exercising rights or powers provided by law.

## 23. General provisions

23.1 **Entire data-processing arrangement:** This DPA and its completed schedules describe the Parties' data-processing arrangement for the identified service. It does not replace the underlying services agreement.

23.2 **Amendments:** Amendments must be in writing and approved by authorised representatives. Changes to processing purposes, data categories, locations, Subprocessors or security risk must be assessed before implementation.

23.3 **Severability:** If a provision is invalid, the remaining provisions continue to the extent lawful. The Parties shall replace the invalid provision with a lawful provision that most closely achieves its intended protective purpose.

23.4 **No waiver:** A failure or delay in exercising a right is not a waiver.

23.5 **Assignment:** Neither Party may assign this DPA in a way that materially changes the processing risk or transfers Personal Data to a new party without the required notice, assessment and authorisation.

23.6 **Electronic execution:** The Parties may sign counterparts and use legally valid electronic signatures or acceptance methods, subject to legal review and reliable records.

23.7 **Survival:** Confidentiality, security incident cooperation, return/deletion, audit evidence, liability, dispute and any provision intended by its nature to survive shall continue after termination.

## 25. Everneed completion and deployment checklist

Before using this DPA with any supplier or partner:

- [ ] Confirm the legal identity, legal form, authority and address of the actual contracting operator and counterparty.
- [ ] Determine each party's controller, processor or independent-controller role for each processing purpose based on actual decisions and activities.
- [ ] Map the live app, public website, seller portal, admin tools, database, Edge Functions, payment integrations, support tools, analytics, communications and operational processes.
- [ ] Verify whether each feature is deployed and enabled; source code or a configuration entry is not proof of live processing.
- [ ] Verify actual Supabase project configuration, hosting region, backups, logs, support access, contractual terms and subprocessors. Do not assume a region from the project URL.
- [ ] Verify Paystack and other payment-provider data flows, legal roles, contracts, payment references, fraud/compliance purposes and retention. Do not assume the provider is a processor for every activity.
- [ ] Identify all sellers, hub operators, couriers, riders, support providers and other recipients, and determine their roles separately.
- [ ] Confirm which customer data each seller and delivery participant can access; test seller-to-seller and tenant isolation using separate accounts.
- [ ] Complete the processing register and Schedule 1 for each service.
- [ ] Complete Schedule 2 with evidence of actual security controls, access tests, backup restoration and incident procedures.
- [ ] Approve every Subprocessor and document its location, service, contract and access.
- [ ] Assess all international hosting, backup, support and remote-access routes under the NDPA and applicable GAID requirements.
- [ ] Approve lawful bases, notices, retention periods, rights-handling and incident procedures.
- [ ] Confirm whether a DPIA, DPO appointment, DCPMI assessment, registration or other regulatory step applies to the actual processing and organisation. Do not assume status without a documented assessment.
- [ ] Set incident contacts and test the notification channel, including out-of-hours escalation where the service requires it.
- [ ] Complete return, deletion and backup-expiry procedures and test evidence generation.
- [ ] Review audit scope, liability, indemnity, insurance, subcontracting, dispute and termination terms with counsel.
- [ ] Reconcile this DPA with the Privacy Notice, seller and customer contracts, delivery terms, provider agreements and actual product behaviour.
- [ ] Obtain Nigerian privacy counsel review and approval by authorised representatives before signature.
- [ ] Store the signed version, completed schedules, approvals and review evidence in a restricted-access legal records location.
