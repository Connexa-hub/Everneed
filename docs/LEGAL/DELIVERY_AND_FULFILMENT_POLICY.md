**Document type:** Internal delivery, hub-handling and fulfilment policy framework  
**Document status:** Working draft for founder approval, Nigerian legal review, partner confirmation and operational testing  
**Business status:** Founder-led startup; current company records state that Everneed is not yet registered with the Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC)  
**Initial market:** Nigeria  
**Project reference branch:** `web-portal`  
**Last updated:** November 2026

## 1. Purpose and status

This policy sets out how Everneed proposes to coordinate the movement of marketplace orders from sellers to customers. It is designed for a multivendor marketplace in which a customer may buy from one seller or several sellers in a single checkout.

The intended model is flexible:

- A qualifying single-seller order may be delivered directly by the seller.
- A single-seller order may use hub-assisted fulfilment where that service is available, agreed and operationally ready.
- A multi-seller order may be split into seller packages, received at a designated hub, checked for visible external condition, consolidated or grouped where practical, and dispatched to the customer as one consignment where suitable.
- Where consolidation is unsafe, impractical, legally restricted or operationally unavailable, the order may travel as multiple parcels with clear customer disclosure.

**Important status statement:** This is a proposed operating and policy framework. It is not evidence that Everneed currently owns or operates a physical hub, employs riders, has a fleet, has contracted couriers, has insurance, serves any particular delivery zone, or has a functioning end-to-end tracking or fulfilment operation. No delivery area, tariff, delivery time, hub address or service guarantee is approved by this document. Each route must be enabled only after the relevant operational, contractual, legal and technical checks have been completed.

This policy must be read with the Seller Marketplace Agreement, Customer Terms of Use, Returns, Refunds, Cancellations & Disputes Policy, Privacy Notice, Seller Onboarding Policy, Prohibited & Restricted Products Policy, Business Model and Operations Playbook. Where a conflict exists, applicable law and the executed contract govern; the documents must be reconciled before launch.

## 2. Principles

Everneed's fulfilment model must follow these principles:

1. **Choice where practical:** Do not force a seller or customer into a fulfilment route that is unavailable or unsuitable. Offer only routes that can actually be delivered.
2. **Transparent total cost:** Show all mandatory delivery, handling and other charges before the customer confirms payment. Do not add undisclosed fees later.
3. **No charge for unused services:** Do not charge a seller or customer for hub handling, collection, packaging or another service that was not provided, subject to a clearly disclosed and lawful cancellation or failed-attempt arrangement.
4. **Fair allocation of fault:** Allocate loss, damage, delay and additional costs based on evidence, custody, conduct, contract and applicable law. Do not automatically penalise a seller for an Everneed, hub or carrier failure.
5. **Safe handling:** Do not consolidate or transport goods in a way that creates avoidable safety, contamination, damage, temperature or regulatory risk.
6. **Traceable custody:** Record material handovers, package counts, exceptions and delivery outcomes.
7. **Customer clarity:** Explain the delivery route, expected window, parcel count where known, tracking limitations and what happens when delivery cannot be completed.
8. **Data minimisation:** Share only the customer and seller information needed to fulfil the order.
9. **Accessible support:** Customers and sellers must have a practical route to report a missing, delayed, damaged or misdelivered package and to challenge a decision.
10. **Evidence before claims:** Do not advertise coverage, speed, tracking, insurance or delivery protection until the specific service has been verified.

## 3. Parties and responsibilities

The precise legal identity of the contracting operator must be inserted after the appropriate legal structure is established. Until then, references to “Everneed” describe the proposed platform operation and do not create or imply a registered legal entity.

### 3.1 Everneed platform operator

Depending on the actual transaction and contracts, Everneed may provide marketplace technology, order coordination, customer support, hub handling or delivery coordination. It must accurately describe which service it provides in each route.

Proposed responsibilities include:
- Displaying the available delivery options and full mandatory charges.
- Passing accurate order and package instructions to the relevant parties.
- Maintaining order and fulfilment status records.
- Coordinating the customer-facing support and complaint process.
- Selecting and monitoring approved hub and delivery partners where used.
- Maintaining role-based access to fulfilment and customer information.
- Investigating reported incidents and communicating outcomes.
- Reconciling service charges, seller balances, refunds and partner invoices.
- Avoiding claims that exceed the actual service being provided.

Everneed remains responsible for its own acts, omissions, representations and contractual obligations. It cannot remove a responsibility imposed by law simply by describing itself as a marketplace intermediary.

### 3.2 Seller

The seller is responsible for:
- Accurate product listings, stock and order acceptance.
- Preparing the correct goods and quantities.
- Suitable packaging and truthful package dimensions and weight.
- Disclosing fragile, perishable, hazardous, age-restricted or special-handling characteristics.
- Meeting the agreed dispatch or hub-handover arrangement.
- Providing the package reference and required handover evidence.
- Responding to reasonable order, product and incident enquiries.
- Complying with product, consumer, tax, privacy and other applicable requirements.

A seller must not hand over prohibited goods or a restricted product that has not received the required approval.

### 3.3 Hub operator

A hub may be operated by Everneed or by an independent service provider. If a third party operates it, a written agreement must define the operator's duties and liability.

Subject to the agreed service, proposed duties include:
- Recording package receipt and the receiving party.
- Matching packages to order and seller references.
- Recording package count and visible external condition.
- Separating and securely storing packages awaiting dispatch.
- Recording exceptions, discrepancies and custody transfers.
- Consolidating or repacking only within the agreed scope and safe handling rules.
- Protecting packages from unauthorised access, loss and avoidable damage.
- Maintaining records and cooperating with investigations.

A hub receipt is not proof that the contents have been inspected, authenticated or found defect-free. Unless the service expressly includes an authorised contents inspection, the hub checks only package identity, count and visible external condition.

### 3.4 Carrier, courier or rider

A carrier may be an independent company, contracted operator, or another lawfully engaged delivery provider. The relevant agreement must define:
- Service area and accepted package types.
- Collection and delivery process.
- Rates and any permitted surcharges.
- Custody and risk allocation.
- Tracking and proof-of-delivery records.
- Failed attempts, returns and redelivery.
- Loss, damage, theft and claims handling.
- Insurance and exclusions.
- Driver/rider identity, safety and conduct standards.
- Customer data handling and confidentiality.
- Subcontracting and responsibility for subcontractors.

Do not represent a carrier as an Everneed employee or agent unless the actual legal and contractual relationship supports that description.

### 3.5 Customer and recipient

The customer should provide an accurate delivery address, accessible contact details and any necessary delivery instructions. The recipient should be available within the disclosed delivery window or use an approved alternative arrangement.

The customer must not be required to accept an unsafe handover or waive a lawful complaint simply because a delivery status was marked complete.

### 3.6 Payment provider and other service providers

A licensed payment provider may process customer payments and refunds. Its role, settlement process, fees, chargeback rules and supported refund methods must be confirmed in the applicable contract and technical integration. A payment status in Everneed's interface must not be treated as conclusive provider confirmation unless verified against the provider's authoritative response or records.

## 4. Approved route types and route selection

A route may be offered only after Everneed has confirmed that the route, destination, package type, partner capacity, pricing, support process and required controls are ready.

### 4.1 Seller-direct delivery

For an eligible single-seller order, the seller may arrange delivery directly where:
- The seller is permitted and able to fulfil the order.
- The destination is within the seller's disclosed service area.
- The seller can provide a realistic delivery estimate and total delivery charge.
- The delivery arrangement is compatible with Everneed's order records and customer support process.
- Any required delivery evidence and complaint route are defined.

The customer must be told that the seller is arranging delivery. Everneed must not claim to control the carrier or guarantee performance where it does not.

### 4.2 Hub-assisted single-seller delivery

A single-seller order may use a hub-assisted route where this is available and agreed. The seller may deliver or arrange transfer of the package to a designated hub, after which an approved carrier may deliver it.

Before enabling this route, Everneed must define:
- Who arranges and pays for seller-to-hub transfer.
- The permitted drop-off or collection window.
- Package acceptance criteria and intake evidence.
- When custody transfers to the hub.
- Hub handling and storage charges, if any.
- The carrier dispatch process and customer-facing estimate.
- What happens if the package is late, rejected, damaged or not received.

Do not charge for a hub service that the seller or customer did not select or that was not performed.

### 4.3 Multi-seller order with hub-assisted consolidation

Where one checkout includes items from multiple sellers, the system may create one customer-facing parent order with separate seller sub-orders or packages.

The proposed flow is:

1. **Order separation:** Create a traceable sub-order/package reference for each participating seller while retaining a single parent order reference for the customer.
2. **Seller preparation:** Each seller confirms availability, prepares the correct items and packages them separately according to the packaging requirements.
3. **Handover:** Each seller transfers its package to the designated hub or approved collection route within the disclosed handover window.
4. **Hub intake:** The hub records the seller/package reference, receipt time, package count, receiving person and visible external condition. Any discrepancy is recorded before the package is accepted or moved into normal storage.
5. **Reconciliation:** The hub checks which expected seller packages have arrived and flags missing, late, duplicated or mismatched packages.
6. **Consolidation decision:** Packages may be grouped into one outer consignment only where size, weight, product compatibility, safety, temperature, fragility, legal restrictions and carrier rules permit.
7. **Dispatch:** The hub records the outbound consignment reference and the package references contained in it, then transfers custody to the approved carrier.
8. **Customer update:** The customer receives a clear status. If the order must travel in separate parcels, the customer is told how many parcels are expected where known and how each will be tracked.
9. **Delivery completion:** Each consignment's delivery evidence is linked back to the parent order and relevant seller packages.

A parent order must not be marked fully delivered merely because one of several parcels has arrived. The customer interface must distinguish partially delivered, in transit, failed, returned and completed states.

Consolidation is not compulsory. Packages must remain separate where combining them could cause damage, contamination, unsafe handling, a breach of temperature requirements, excessive weight, carrier refusal or regulatory non-compliance.

### 4.4 Multi-seller order without consolidation

If no suitable hub or consolidation service is available, Everneed may offer separate seller or carrier deliveries only if the customer is informed before payment of the expected parcel arrangement, applicable charges and material delivery differences.

Do not describe separate deliveries as a single consolidated delivery. Do not conceal multiple delivery charges in a vague fee.

### 4.5 Route selection and customer consent

Route selection may consider destination, seller capability, package characteristics, available partners, service cost, delivery reliability and operational capacity. The route must not be changed after checkout in a way that materially increases the customer's price or reduces the promised service without informing the customer and obtaining any approval required by law or contract.

If a route becomes unavailable after payment:
- Notify the customer promptly.
- Explain the reason in plain language.
- Offer a reasonable alternative without an undisclosed additional charge, or a cancellation/refund option as required by the applicable terms and law.
- Do not silently substitute a materially different service.

## 5. Service areas, availability and delivery estimates

Everneed must publish only service zones that have been operationally verified. A city name or broad map boundary is not enough to establish coverage.

Before a zone is enabled, confirm:
- Address format and location validation.
- Partner coverage and actual collection/delivery capacity.
- Hub access and operating hours, if applicable.
- Package restrictions and route feasibility.
- Customer support and failed-delivery handling.
- Current delivery charges and any zone-based differences.
- Contingency arrangements for service interruption.

Delivery estimates must be based on actual route and partner information. Distinguish an estimate from a guarantee. Do not invent or copy generic delivery windows.

If an estimate changes materially, communicate the revised estimate and available options. Do not use an estimated delivery date to exclude statutory rights or excuse avoidable service failures.

## 6. Customer disclosures and charges

Before the customer confirms an order, the checkout or other appropriate pre-payment screen must show:
- Seller identity for each item.
- Delivery route or route type.
- Delivery charge for each route or package arrangement, as applicable.
- Hub handling, consolidation, packaging or other mandatory service charges, if any.
- Any optional service and its price, separately identified.
- Total amount payable, including all mandatory charges and applicable taxes.
- Estimated delivery window and material limitations.
- Whether the order may arrive in multiple parcels.
- Whether tracking is available and what it covers.
- Any relevant collection, recipient availability or failed-attempt condition.

Charges must be understandable and tied to a service or lawful cost. The platform must not add arbitrary handling fees after payment.

### 6.1 Fair charge principles

- No hidden delivery, hub, packaging or redelivery charges.
- No charge for a hub or delivery service that was not used, except a clearly disclosed, lawful and proportionate fee for a service already performed or a customer-caused event.
- No seller charge for a platform, hub or carrier failure solely attributable to Everneed or its chosen provider.
- No automatic seller deduction for a disputed loss or damage event without evidence, contractual basis, notice and a fair opportunity to respond.
- No duplicate collection of a delivery charge for the same service.
- No delivery collection treated automatically as Everneed revenue.

Where a carrier's fee is collected through Everneed, the accounting must distinguish pass-through amounts payable to the carrier from any separately disclosed Everneed service revenue. Reconcile the actual provider invoice and any adjustment or refund.

### 6.2 Seller-facing service charges

Any seller-facing fee must be disclosed before the seller selects the service and must identify:
- The service provided.
- The amount or calculation method.
- Whether it is mandatory or optional.
- When it becomes payable.
- What happens if the service is cancelled, not performed or fails.
- How the seller can dispute an incorrect charge.

The initial commercial recommendation is to keep basic seller participation accessible and avoid compulsory hub or fulfilment fees. Optional paid services should be introduced only when they provide measurable value and have founder approval. This is a recommendation, not an approved price schedule.

## 7. Seller preparation, packaging and handover

### 7.1 Order preparation

The seller must:
- Confirm that the item is available and can be lawfully supplied.
- Check product, variant, size, quantity and order reference.
- Avoid substituting a different product without customer approval.
- Remove or secure any unrelated customer information from the package.
- Prepare the package within the disclosed or agreed timeframe.
- Notify Everneed promptly if the order cannot be fulfilled as accepted.

### 7.2 Packaging

The seller must use packaging suitable for the product, journey, handling method and weather exposure reasonably expected on the route. Packaging should protect against ordinary movement, pressure, moisture and handling without creating avoidable hazards.

Requirements may include:
- Secure closure and tamper-evident packaging where appropriate.
- Protective cushioning for fragile items.
- Leak-resistant containment for permitted liquids.
- Separation of items that may contaminate or damage one another.
- Clear orientation and handling labels where relevant.
- Accurate weight and dimensions.
- Product and package labels that do not expose unnecessary personal data.

The seller must disclose special handling requirements before accepting an order. A label alone does not make an otherwise unsafe product suitable for transport.

### 7.3 Package identification

Each package should carry or be associated with:
- Parent order reference.
- Seller sub-order/package reference.
- Seller/store identifier.
- Package count where an order contains multiple packages.
- Handling information required for the route.
- Destination information in a form accessible only to parties who need it.

Do not place full customer personal information on externally visible labels unless necessary for delivery. Use an order reference or limited contact details where operationally possible.

### 7.4 Handover evidence

At seller handover, record as appropriate:
- Package reference and order reference.
- Date and time.
- Handover location or approved collection point.
- Person or provider receiving the package.
- Package count and visible external condition.
- Any exception, refusal or discrepancy.
- Confirmation by the handing-over and receiving parties.

A seller must retain its own order preparation and handover evidence for the period specified in the approved records schedule and applicable law.

### 7.5 Late, incomplete or rejected handovers

If a seller cannot meet the handover window, it must update the order promptly. Everneed should notify affected customers and assess whether to continue, revise the estimate, split the order or cancel/refund the affected item.

A hub or carrier may refuse a package that is prohibited, unsafe, inadequately packed, incorrectly labelled, materially different from the declared package, leaking, damaged or outside agreed limits. The reason and condition must be recorded. Any return or re-preparation charge must be assessed under the contract and fault evidence, not imposed automatically.

## 8. Hub intake, custody and storage

This section describes proposed controls for any hub that Everneed or a contracted operator may later use. It does not confirm that a hub exists or that these controls are currently operating.

### 8.1 Intake record

For each accepted package, the hub should record:
- Unique package and parent order references.
- Seller identity/reference.
- Date and time received.
- Receiving staff or operator identity.
- Number of packages received.
- Visible external condition and any apparent damage.
- Whether the package was accepted, conditionally accepted or refused.
- Any photograph or supporting record reasonably needed to evidence an exception.

Photographs must be proportionate, securely stored and limited to the package or relevant exception. Avoid photographing people, private documents or package contents unnecessarily.

### 8.2 Scope of inspection

Unless the seller has expressly agreed to a defined inspection service and that service is lawful, hub intake is an external package check only. Staff must not open sealed packages, test products, authenticate goods or certify contents as safe merely because a package was received.

Where an authorised inspection is required:
- Define its scope in writing.
- Obtain appropriate seller authority and any required customer/legal basis.
- Train the personnel performing it.
- Record the inspection and any seal or packaging changes.
- Protect confidential and personal information.
- Escalate suspected prohibited or unsafe goods.

### 8.3 Custody and storage

The hub operator must maintain a record of each material custody transfer. Packages awaiting dispatch should be stored securely and segregated where needed by seller, order, product type, fragility, temperature, hazard or other compatibility requirement.

The hub plan must address:
- Access control and visitor records.
- Secure storage and protection from weather, theft and unauthorised handling.
- Fire, electrical and workplace safety.
- Inventory and package reconciliation.
- Incident reporting and escalation.
- Business continuity and emergency procedures.
- Retention and secure disposal of records.

Any applicable insurance, security, premises, fire, employment and local regulatory requirements must be assessed before the hub opens.

### 8.4 Discrepancies and visible damage

If a package is missing, duplicated, mismatched, open, leaking or visibly damaged:
1. Stop normal processing where necessary to preserve evidence and prevent further harm.
2. Record the package reference, time, condition and person reporting it.
3. Take proportionate photographs where appropriate.
4. Notify the responsible Everneed contact and relevant seller/provider.
5. Keep the package separate if safety or investigation requires it.
6. Decide whether to reject, repackage, return or continue only after an authorised review.
7. Update the order status and customer where the issue materially affects fulfilment.

Do not conceal a visible exception or mark a package as received in good condition when the record shows otherwise.

## 9. Consolidation, repacking and dispatch

Consolidation must be based on suitability, not merely the desire to reduce delivery cost.

Before combining packages, the authorised operator should confirm:
- All expected packages are present or the customer has been informed of a partial dispatch.
- Package references match the parent order.
- Goods are compatible for combined transport.
- Combined weight and dimensions meet carrier limits.
- Fragile, liquid, food, chemical, battery or other restricted goods are not placed together in a way that creates risk.
- Temperature and handling requirements can be maintained.
- The outer package provides appropriate protection.
- The consignment can be traced back to each seller package.

Sealed seller packages should remain sealed unless the service, contract and law authorise opening. Repacking must not remove essential product labels, safety information, expiry information, warranties or traceability details.

At dispatch, record:
- Outbound consignment reference.
- Included seller package references.
- Package count, weight and dimensions where required.
- Dispatch time and location.
- Carrier or rider identity/provider.
- Handover confirmation.
- Visible condition and any exception.
- Tracking reference, if provided.

If one seller package is missing or delayed, do not silently dispatch an incomplete order as if complete. Determine whether partial dispatch is appropriate and disclose it to the customer.

## 10. Carrier selection, delivery and proof of delivery

### 10.1 Provider due diligence

Before appointing a delivery provider, Everneed should verify and document, as relevant:
- Legal identity and business details.
- Service coverage and actual operating capacity.
- Permitted vehicle and package types.
- Rider/driver onboarding and safety arrangements.
- Relevant permits, licences and regulatory requirements.
- Insurance scope, limits, exclusions and claims process.
- Subcontracting arrangements.
- Data protection and confidentiality controls.
- Incident, complaint and escalation process.
- Pricing, surcharges, invoicing and reconciliation.
- Business continuity and service performance evidence.

Do not claim a provider is insured or licensed until current evidence has been checked. Obtain qualified advice on whether Everneed, the provider or both need particular cover.

### 10.2 Dispatch and tracking

The dispatch record should connect the parent order, seller packages, consignment and provider tracking reference. Customer-facing status should reflect verified events rather than unsupported estimates or manually invented milestones.

Potential statuses include:
- Preparing.
- Awaiting seller handover.
- Received at hub.
- Exception recorded.
- Ready for dispatch.
- Dispatched / in transit.
- Out for delivery.
- Partially delivered.
- Delivered.
- Delivery attempted.
- Delivery failed.
- Returned / return in progress.
- Cancelled.

The exact status model must be implemented and tested before it is represented as a live feature. A tracking number does not guarantee real-time location tracking.

### 10.3 Recipient and safe handover

Delivery instructions must explain any recipient identification or confirmation method before delivery. The provider should obtain proportionate proof, such as a recipient confirmation, delivery code or other agreed record.

Do not collect more identity information than necessary. Do not disclose a customer's full order details to an unauthorised person.

Alternative-recipient, neighbour, reception or safe-place delivery must be used only where disclosed, authorised and appropriate to the goods and applicable law. Do not leave high-value, age-restricted, temperature-sensitive or otherwise unsuitable goods unattended.

### 10.4 Proof of delivery

Proof may include a timestamp, delivery status, recipient confirmation, delivery code or other agreed evidence. A photograph should be taken only where appropriate, disclosed and proportionate; it should not unnecessarily capture faces, private interiors, identity documents or unrelated people.

A delivery record must be open to investigation if the customer reasonably reports non-receipt, wrong-recipient delivery, missing contents or a materially inaccurate status.

## 11. Failed attempts, rescheduling and undeliverable packages

A delivery attempt may fail because of an incorrect or incomplete address, recipient unavailability, unsafe access, customer request, provider capacity, weather, security incident or other cause.

The provider or responsible party should record:
- Attempt date and time.
- Package/consignment reference.
- Reason for failure.
- Contact or notification attempted.
- Any relevant evidence.
- Next available action.

The customer should receive a clear notice and a practical route to correct the address, arrange another attempt, use an approved collection option or cancel where the terms and law permit.

Any redelivery, return-to-seller or storage charge must be disclosed in advance, proportionate, supported by the contract and attributable to the relevant cause. Do not apply an automatic fee where the failed attempt was caused by Everneed, the hub or the chosen carrier.

If a package becomes undeliverable:
- Secure it and maintain custody records.
- Notify the customer and relevant seller.
- Determine whether redelivery, collection or return is appropriate.
- Preserve the order and payment record.
- Apply the Returns and Refunds Policy and relevant statutory rights.
- Reconcile any provider charge or refund transparently.

## 12. Loss, damage, theft, delay and incident responsibility

Responsibility must be determined from the facts, the point of custody, the actions of each party, the applicable contract and law. A policy cannot fairly or lawfully make one party automatically responsible for every incident regardless of cause.

### 12.1 Investigation framework

For a reported incident, Everneed should:
1. Create a case reference and acknowledge the report through the approved support process.
2. Identify the affected parent order, seller package and consignment.
3. Establish the last verified custody event and subsequent handovers.
4. Obtain relevant seller, hub and carrier records.
5. Review package condition, handover evidence, tracking and delivery proof.
6. Determine whether the issue relates to product quality, seller packaging, hub handling, carrier handling, customer-provided information, force majeure or an unresolved cause.
7. Decide the customer remedy under the applicable law and Returns and Refunds Policy.
8. Separately determine any recovery or allocation between commercial parties under their contracts.
9. Explain the decision and available appeal route.
10. Record corrective action to reduce recurrence.

### 12.2 Fair allocation

Examples of factors to consider:
- Seller may be responsible where evidence shows incorrect goods, inadequate packaging within the seller's control, false package declarations, late handover or a seller breach caused or contributed to the loss.
- A hub operator may be responsible where evidence shows loss, unauthorised access, mishandling or a custody failure while the package was under its control.
- A carrier may be responsible under its contract and applicable law for loss, damage, theft or delivery failure during its custody.
- Everneed may be responsible for its own system, staff, coordination, representations or contractual failures.
- A customer may be responsible for a loss caused by materially incorrect delivery information or a customer-caused event, where supported by evidence and permitted by law.
- Some incidents may have shared or indeterminate causes. Do not allocate liability by assumption.

A seller must not be charged for a loss solely caused by an Everneed-appointed hub or carrier unless the seller caused or contributed to it and the contractual/legal basis is established. Any seller debit must be itemised, supported by evidence, notified and open to challenge. No arbitrary penalty or indefinite hold is permitted.

### 12.3 Customer remedy

The customer-facing remedy must not be delayed merely because Everneed is still pursuing a claim against a seller or delivery provider where the law or customer contract requires Everneed to act sooner. Customer rights and inter-party cost recovery are separate questions.

## 13. Returns, refunds, cancellations and reverse logistics

Returns and reverse movement must follow the separate Returns, Refunds, Cancellations & Disputes Policy and the Customer Terms of Use. This policy does not create a blanket right to reject returns or a blanket promise to accept every return.

Before a return route is enabled, define:
- The authorised return request and approval process.
- The return address or approved handover point.
- Whether the seller, hub or carrier receives the returned item.
- Package and condition evidence.
- Chain of custody and receipt confirmation.
- Who pays return transport and when.
- How refunds, replacements and partial multi-seller refunds are handled.
- How disputed, unsafe, recalled or prohibited products are isolated.
- How seller balances and provider invoices are reconciled.

A return label, pickup or hub drop-off option must not be displayed unless the route is actually available. Do not tell a customer to send goods to an unverified address or an employee's personal location.

For a multi-seller order, a return may concern only one seller's item or package. The system and support process should preserve item-level and seller-level records without requiring the customer to return unaffected items.

## 14. Prohibited, restricted and special-handling goods

The Prohibited & Restricted Products Policy governs what may be listed or sold. A product being listed does not mean it is automatically suitable for every delivery route.

Before accepting a package, the seller and fulfilment operator must consider:
- Legal restrictions and required authorisations.
- Carrier acceptance rules.
- Dangerous-goods classification and packaging.
- Battery, electrical, gas or flammable characteristics.
- Leakage, contamination or cross-contamination risk.
- Food safety, expiry and temperature requirements.
- Fragility, size, weight and value.
- Age verification or recipient restrictions.
- Recall or safety alerts.

Keep prohibited goods out of the network. Keep restricted categories disabled until both legal approval and a safe, tested handling route exist. Never transport hazardous or regulated goods based solely on a seller's informal assurance.

## 15. Privacy, customer data and security

Fulfilment requires limited personal information, but it does not justify unrestricted access to customer accounts or order history.

Everneed and each service provider must follow the Privacy Notice, applicable data-protection law and their written data-processing or service obligations.

Controls should include:
- Share only the name, delivery address, contact method, package/order reference and instructions reasonably needed for the route.
- Limit access by role and provider assignment.
- Do not share a customer's unrelated purchases, payment credentials or account history with a seller or courier.
- Do not use delivery contact details for unsolicited marketing.
- Do not retain identity or delivery photographs longer than necessary under the approved retention schedule.
- Use secure transfer and storage; avoid public spreadsheets, personal messaging accounts and exposed group chats for customer records.
- Remove or revoke access when a worker or provider no longer needs it.
- Record and escalate suspected unauthorised access, disclosure, loss or misuse.
- Define controller/processor roles and cross-border transfers where applicable.

Payment card credentials must be handled by the approved payment provider and compliant payment flow. Hub or delivery personnel must never request a customer's card PIN, one-time password or payment password.

## 16. Insurance, safety and legal due diligence

Before any hub, collection, storage or delivery service begins, obtain appropriate Nigerian legal and insurance advice on the actual operating model.

Assess, as applicable:
- Public liability and premises risks.
- Goods-in-transit and carrier liability cover.
- Goods held in custody or storage.
- Theft, fire, flood and business interruption.
- Employer, worker, contractor and rider injury risks.
- Motor vehicle and third-party liability.
- Product liability and recall exposure.
- Occupational health and safety.
- Fire safety, premises approvals and local authority requirements.
- Labour classification, minimum employment standards and contractor arrangements.
- Consumer protection, tax, privacy and transport regulation.

Review the wording, limits, deductibles, exclusions, claims conditions and named insureds. A provider's statement that it is “covered” is not sufficient. Do not promise customers or sellers that goods are insured unless the precise coverage, beneficiary, exclusions and claim route are confirmed and accurately disclosed.

## 17. Customer support, seller support and appeals

Everneed should provide one coordinated customer-facing route for delivery complaints, even when investigation involves a seller, hub or carrier.

Before launch, insert and test:
- Customer support channel: [insert monitored channel]
- Seller support channel: [insert monitored channel]
- Urgent safety/security escalation: [insert monitored contact and backup]
- Delivery partner escalation: [insert contracted contact]
- Hub incident escalation: [insert responsible operator/contact]
- Complaint acknowledgement and resolution targets: [set only after staffing and testing]

Support must:
- Issue a case reference.
- Identify the relevant order and package.
- Explain the current verified status.
- Avoid promising an unverified delivery date or refund.
- Request only evidence reasonably needed.
- Provide updates when an investigation is pending.
- Explain the decision and applicable appeal route.
- Escalate safety, fraud, privacy and serious loss incidents promptly.

Sellers and customers must be able to challenge material decisions or charges. Where practical, an appeal should be reviewed by someone other than the original decision-maker.

## 18. Records, reconciliation and audit trail

Keep proportionate, access-controlled records for:
- Order and seller sub-order references.
- Route selected and customer disclosures.
- Delivery charges and payment confirmation.
- Seller preparation and package handover.
- Hub intake, storage, exception and dispatch.
- Carrier assignment, tracking and custody transfer.
- Delivery attempts and proof of delivery.
- Customer complaints, investigations and outcomes.
- Returns, refunds, claims and seller adjustments.
- Provider invoices and service reconciliation.
- Training, partner due diligence, insurance and incident reviews.

Retention periods must be set in the Privacy Notice, records schedule and applicable legal requirements. Do not keep personal data indefinitely “just in case”. Preserve records subject to a lawful dispute, investigation or regulatory hold.

Monthly reconciliation should distinguish:
- Customer delivery collections.
- Carrier/courier amounts payable.
- Hub and packaging costs.
- Seller-to-hub transfer costs.
- Refunds and failed-delivery adjustments.
- Any separately earned Everneed fulfilment or coordination revenue.

Do not count seller funds or pass-through delivery money as unrestricted Everneed cash or revenue.

## 19. Service performance and continuous improvement

After a route is live, review performance using actual, consistently defined records. Potential measures include:
- Seller handover punctuality.
- Hub intake discrepancies.
- Time from hub receipt to dispatch.
- On-time delivery against the disclosed estimate.
- First-attempt delivery success.
- Missing, damaged and misdelivered package rates.
- Multi-seller order completion and parcel-split frequency.
- Return and failed-delivery rates.
- Customer complaints and resolution time.
- Provider invoice accuracy.
- Cost per successful delivery and route contribution.
- Safety, privacy and security incidents.

Do not invent targets or publish performance claims before a reliable baseline exists. Set targets only after route design, partner capability and pilot data have been reviewed. Investigate recurring problems and pause a route if safety, legal compliance, data protection or customer trust cannot be maintained.

## 20. Operational launch gates

A delivery route must not go live until the founder or delegated owner has recorded evidence that all applicable gates are complete.

### 20.1 Legal and commercial
- [ ] Legal operator identity and contracting authority are established or appropriately documented.
- [ ] Customer and seller terms accurately describe the route and responsibility.
- [ ] Hub, collection and carrier agreements are signed where required.
- [ ] Charges, taxes, cancellation rules and provider payment terms are approved.
- [ ] Product and package restrictions are documented.
- [ ] Insurance and liability arrangements have been reviewed by a qualified adviser.
- [ ] Any permits, premises, transport or other approvals are confirmed.

### 20.2 Operational
- [ ] The actual hub or collection location is confirmed and ready, if used.
- [ ] Service zones and address validation have been tested.
- [ ] Package limits and packaging rules are documented.
- [ ] Intake, custody, reconciliation, storage and dispatch procedures are trained and tested.
- [ ] Failed delivery, loss, damage, returns and emergency procedures are workable.
- [ ] Staff and partner escalation contacts are monitored.
- [ ] Partner capacity and backup arrangements are evidenced.

### 20.3 Product and technical
- [ ] Parent orders and seller sub-orders/packages have stable references.
- [ ] Partial delivery and multiple-parcel states are represented correctly.
- [ ] Package-to-consignment relationships can be reconciled.
- [ ] Customer charges and route disclosures are shown before payment.
- [ ] Tracking statuses come from reliable events and do not falsely imply live GPS.
- [ ] Proof of delivery is stored securely and can be reviewed.
- [ ] Refunds and seller balance adjustments are auditable.
- [ ] Role-based access prevents sellers and providers from seeing unrelated orders or customer data.
- [ ] Failure, duplicate event, webhook retry and offline scenarios have been tested.
- [ ] Support staff can trace an order from seller preparation through final delivery.

### 20.4 Pilot and approval
- [ ] A controlled test has covered a single-seller direct route.
- [ ] A controlled test has covered a hub-assisted single-seller route, if offered.
- [ ] A controlled test has covered a multi-seller order with consolidation, if offered.
- [ ] A controlled test has covered separate parcels where consolidation is unsuitable.
- [ ] Failed attempt, missing package, damaged package, partial delivery and refund scenarios have been exercised.
- [ ] Customer and seller feedback has been reviewed.
- [ ] Founder approval and any required legal/partner sign-off are recorded.

If a gate is not met, keep the route disabled or restrict it to a clearly controlled test that does not expose ordinary customers to an unready service.
