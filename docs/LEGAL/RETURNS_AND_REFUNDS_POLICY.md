## 1. Purpose and customer commitment

Everneed is intended to make shopping across independent businesses easier while treating customers and sellers fairly.

This policy explains how customers can report an order problem, request cancellation, return an item, seek a refund or challenge a decision. It is designed around five commitments:

1. **A fair outcome:** assess what actually happened and who was responsible.
2. **No hidden rules:** explain return eligibility, costs, evidence and next steps clearly.
3. **No automatic blame:** do not automatically charge a seller or customer before investigating.
4. **No false promises:** do not promise a refund date, delivery collection or return route that has not been confirmed.
5. **Legal rights preserved:** this policy does not replace or reduce rights available under Nigerian consumer-protection law.

The policy applies to purchases made through the Everneed Platform, subject to the specific seller, product, transaction and service terms shown at checkout.

## 2. Who is responsible?

The seller identified on the product listing is generally responsible for supplying the goods and ensuring that the goods meet the description and applicable legal requirements. Everneed operates the marketplace interface and may coordinate customer support, payment-provider communication or fulfilment services where expressly offered.

Everneed remains responsible for its own representations, platform services, payment-related conduct, hub handling or delivery coordination to the extent required by law and the actual transaction.

## 3. When to contact us

Contact the seller through the approved Platform channel or contact Everneed support if:

- The item is damaged, defective, unsafe or not working as reasonably expected.
- The item is materially different from its description, image, selected size, colour, model or specification.
- You received the wrong item or quantity.
- An item is missing from the parcel or a multi-seller order.
- The parcel appears tampered with or was damaged during delivery.
- The seller has not dispatched or fulfilled the order within the confirmed arrangement.
- You were charged incorrectly, charged more than once or payment status is unclear.
- You wish to cancel before fulfilment has reached a stage where cancellation is no longer practical.
- You want to exercise another right available under applicable law.

**Report promptly:** Contact us as soon as reasonably possible after discovering a problem. For visible delivery damage or an incorrect parcel, reporting promptly and keeping the packaging can help the investigation. However, a reporting window in this policy must not be treated as an automatic forfeiture of a statutory right. The legal team must confirm any lawful time limits before publication.

## 4. Reasons for a return, replacement or refund

A request may be considered where one or more of the following applies:

### 4.1 Wrong or incomplete order
You received a different product, variant, quantity or package from what was confirmed, or part of the order is missing.

### 4.2 Damaged or defective goods
The item arrived damaged, is defective, does not function as represented, or has a fault that makes it unsuitable for its ordinary or stated purpose.

### 4.3 Materially inaccurate description
The item is materially different from the description, specifications, condition, images or options presented at the time of purchase.

### 4.4 Seller cannot fulfil
The seller confirms that the item is unavailable or cannot be supplied as agreed.

### 4.5 Delivery or fulfilment failure
The order or parcel is lost, materially delayed beyond the agreed arrangement, delivered to the wrong destination, or otherwise not fulfilled as promised.

### 4.6 Customer change of mind
A customer may ask to cancel or return an item because they no longer want it. Whether this is available depends on the product, whether fulfilment has begun, the seller's disclosed policy and applicable law. A change-of-mind request must not be confused with a defect, misdescription or other legal remedy.

### 4.7 Other legal remedy
A customer may have rights or remedies under applicable law even where the reason is not listed above. Everneed and the seller must assess the actual facts and cannot rely on this list to deny a non-excludable right.

## 5. Items that may have special return conditions

Some products may require special handling for health, safety, hygiene, personalisation, perishability, digital delivery or legal reasons.

Examples may include:
- Perishable or time-sensitive goods.
- Personalised or custom-made items.
- Sealed hygiene or personal-care goods where opening affects safe resale.
- Digital content or services once supply has begun, where applicable law permits special treatment.
- Products restricted by law or subject to safety, licensing or handling rules.

These examples do **not** create a blanket “no returns” category. Any exclusion or limitation must be lawful, proportionate, disclosed before purchase and consistent with statutory remedies for defective, unsafe, misdescribed or wrongly supplied goods. A seller cannot use a hygiene, clearance, custom-order or “final sale” label to remove rights that the law protects.

Before opening a category, Everneed must approve category-specific wording and confirm the relevant legal position.

## 7. Assessment and fair investigation

When a request is received, the case owner should:

1. Confirm receipt and provide a case reference.
2. Identify the order, seller package and payment status.
3. Record the customer's description and requested outcome.
4. Review listing information, checkout disclosures, seller messages and order events.
5. Request relevant information from the seller or delivery provider where necessary.
6. Review available dispatch, handover, hub receipt, delivery and proof-of-delivery evidence.
7. Consider the customer's evidence and any reasonable explanation from the seller or provider.
8. Determine the appropriate remedy under the facts, contract and applicable law.
9. Explain the decision, next action and any review route in clear language.

The investigation must be proportionate. Do not make a customer repeat information already held in the case. Do not require a customer to negotiate privately with a seller where Everneed has offered to manage the complaint.

### Evidence principles

- A delivery status alone is not conclusive proof that the correct customer received the correct goods.
- A customer photograph or video may help but should not be the only evidence considered.
- A seller's denial is not, by itself, proof that the order was correct.
- A carrier or hub record should be checked against the correct parcel and order reference.
- Where evidence is incomplete, record that limitation and make a fair decision based on what is reasonably available.
- Preserve relevant records while a dispute is active, subject to the Privacy Notice and retention rules.

## 8. Cancellation before fulfilment

A customer may request cancellation through the Platform or support channel.

### 8.1 Before seller acceptance or preparation
Where the seller has not accepted or begun preparing the package, cancellation should ordinarily be processed if technically and operationally possible.

### 8.2 After preparation or dispatch has begun
If the seller has prepared the item, transferred it to a hub or delivery provider, or dispatched it, the request may need to be handled as a return or delivery interception rather than an immediate cancellation.

Any cancellation or return charge must have a lawful and disclosed basis and must reflect actual responsibility and costs. Do not impose an arbitrary penalty or charge for a service that was not provided.

### 8.3 Seller cannot fulfil
If a seller cannot fulfil an accepted order, the customer must be informed promptly and offered the appropriate cancellation and refund or other lawful remedy. Do not substitute a different seller or product without the customer's informed agreement.

### 8.4 Payment pending or failed
If payment has not been confirmed, do not describe the order as paid or promise a refund of money that was not received. If a payment provider shows a pending, failed or reversed transaction, explain the status and help the customer trace any debit or reversal.

## 9. Multi-seller orders and partial remedies

A multi-seller checkout may create one customer-facing parent order with separate seller orders or packages.

A problem affecting one seller package should not automatically cancel or delay unrelated packages unless the customer chooses that outcome or the order cannot reasonably be separated.

Examples:
- If one seller cannot supply an item, the affected package may be cancelled and refunded while other packages continue, subject to customer choice and the checkout terms.
- If one item in a package is defective, the remedy may apply to that item rather than the entire parent order.
- If one package is lost in transit, investigate and resolve that package without treating correctly delivered packages as undelivered.
- If a delivery charge was calculated for combined fulfilment, reassess any adjustment fairly where the final parcel count or route changes.

Before a partial refund or cancellation is processed, explain how it affects the remaining order, delivery charge, any promotion or discount, and seller/package status. Do not silently alter the customer's order.

The customer must be able to see which package or item is affected and the status of the remaining order. The production system must support accurate parent-order and seller-package reconciliation before this policy is presented as a live capability.

## 10. Returns and physical handover

A return route must be agreed before a customer is told to send or hand over an item.

Depending on the service actually available, the approved route may be:
- Collection from the customer by an authorised delivery provider.
- Drop-off at a verified and published return point.
- Return directly to the seller using an agreed method.
- Another route communicated and accepted for the particular case.

Do not tell customers to send items to an unverified address, an employee's private address or a hub that has not been confirmed as operating.

### 10.1 Return authorisation
Where an authorisation is required for safe tracking, provide a return reference, item/package details, destination or collection instructions and any approved packaging requirements.

### 10.2 Packaging and condition
Customers should use reasonable packaging to protect the item in transit and include the return reference. Do not require original packaging where it is unavailable and the law does not permit that condition to defeat a valid claim.

A customer should not be required to return an item in a condition that is impossible because the defect or damage occurred before delivery. The remedy must reflect the actual circumstances and applicable law.

### 10.3 Return custody
Every physical handover should record:
- Return reference and linked order/package.
- Sender and receiving party or provider.
- Date and time.
- Package condition and visible damage.
- Tracking or handover evidence where available.

The person or organisation accepting custody must be identifiable. Everneed must not mark a return as received merely because a label was generated.

## 11. Who pays return and delivery costs?

Responsibility for costs depends on the reason for the return, the disclosed terms, the actual service and applicable law.

### 11.1 Seller or platform responsibility
Where the issue is caused by an incorrect listing, wrong item, defective or damaged goods before the agreed handover, seller failure, or another matter for which the seller is responsible, the customer should not automatically bear the cost of obtaining the remedy. The responsible party should bear reasonable, evidenced costs as determined under the contract and law.

Where the issue is caused by Everneed's own hub handling, platform error or chosen delivery arrangement, Everneed should not automatically pass the resulting cost to the seller or customer if they did not cause or contribute to the failure.

### 11.2 Customer change of mind
Where a return is genuinely for change of mind and the law and disclosed policy permit it, the customer may be responsible for reasonable return costs if this was clearly disclosed before purchase. No hidden or arbitrary fee may be introduced after the order.

### 11.3 Shared or uncertain responsibility
Where responsibility is disputed or unclear, investigate before allocating costs. Any allocation must be reasoned, proportionate, supported by evidence and open to review.

Do not deduct a disputed amount from seller proceeds without a contractual basis, evidence, notice and a fair challenge process.

## 12. Refund methods and timing

Refunds should ordinarily be made through the original payment route where the payment provider supports it and this is lawful and practical. An alternative method may be used only where appropriate, securely verified and agreed or otherwise legally permitted.

A refund is not complete merely because Everneed has approved it internally or submitted a request. The payment provider or receiving institution may require additional processing time.

Before publication, Everneed must approve and disclose realistic service targets for:
- Acknowledging a complaint.
- Completing an ordinary investigation.
- Making a cancellation decision.
- Submitting an approved refund.
- Updating the customer after a provider delay.
- Escalating an overdue or disputed refund.

No specific number of days is inserted in this draft because the actual provider flow, operational capacity and applicable legal deadlines must first be verified. Statutory deadlines, where they apply, take precedence over internal service targets.

For every refund, record:
- Order and item/package reference.
- Approved amount and reason.
- Whether the amount is full or partial.
- Decision maker and approval timestamp.
- Payment provider refund reference and status.
- Customer notification.
- Any related seller-ledger or delivery-cost adjustment.

Do not claim that a refund has reached the customer's bank or wallet until the available provider evidence supports that statement.

## 13. Refund calculation and charges

The refund calculation must be clear and itemised.

Depending on the case, it may include:
- Price paid for the affected item.
- Applicable taxes or mandatory charges attributable to that item.
- Delivery or fulfilment charge where the service was not supplied, was incorrectly charged or must be refunded under law or the disclosed terms.
- Any other amount that the customer is legally entitled to recover.

For a partial order, calculate the affected item/package amount transparently. Explain any delivery-charge or discount adjustment before processing where practicable.

Everneed must not:
- Keep a platform fee from a customer refund where doing so would be unlawful or unfair.
- Deduct a cancellation or restocking fee without a lawful, disclosed and factually justified basis.
- Charge a customer for correcting a seller or platform error.
- Retain money for an item that was not supplied where a refund is due.
- Treat a seller's commission, hub fee or carrier cost as a reason to deny a customer's statutory remedy.

## 14. Exchanges and replacements

A replacement or exchange may be offered where:
- The correct replacement is available.
- The customer agrees to the proposed option.
- The replacement can be supplied within a reasonable and disclosed arrangement.
- The remedy is consistent with applicable law.

A customer must not be forced to accept store credit, a voucher or a replacement where the law entitles them to another remedy. If the replacement is unavailable, explain the alternatives and process the appropriate refund or other remedy.

## 15. Delivery failure, loss and damage

For failed, delayed, lost or damaged delivery:

1. Open a case against the correct parent order and parcel reference.
2. Confirm the last verified custody event.
3. Request relevant evidence from the seller, hub or delivery provider.
4. Keep the customer informed without making unsupported promises.
5. Determine whether another delivery attempt, replacement, cancellation or refund is appropriate.
6. Allocate costs according to proven responsibility, contract and law.
7. Record the outcome and any corrective action.

A customer should not be required to resolve a carrier dispute alone where Everneed arranged or represented the delivery service. A seller should not automatically bear a loss occurring after a documented handover to a hub or carrier unless the evidence and agreed custody terms support that allocation.

## 16. Seller fairness and seller-side adjustments

Everneed's seller relationship must remain transparent and evidence-based.

- Do not charge a seller a joining fee, subscription or advertising fee merely because a customer requested a return.
- Do not charge transaction commission on a cancelled, unpaid, reversed or otherwise non-qualifying sale, subject to the agreed commercial schedule and law.
- Do not make arbitrary or unexplained deductions from seller proceeds.
- Do not debit a seller for a platform, hub or carrier failure that the seller did not cause or contribute to.
- Do not automatically assume a customer complaint is valid or invalid; investigate.
- Give the seller the relevant case details and a reasonable opportunity to respond, subject to customer privacy and safety.
- Record the contractual basis, evidence, calculation and approval for any seller-side adjustment.
- Provide a statement and a route to challenge an incorrect debit or withheld settlement.

Seller liability must be based on the seller's actual obligations and proven responsibility. Any reserve, set-off, hold or deduction must be disclosed in the Seller Marketplace Agreement, proportionate, time-bounded where appropriate and legally reviewed.

## 17. Complaints, appeals and escalation

If a customer or seller disagrees with an initial outcome, they may request a review through:

**Appeals channel:** [insert monitored support/privacy or complaints channel]  
**Case reference:** [include existing case reference]

The review should, where practicable, be conducted by someone who did not make the original decision. The reviewer should examine the evidence, policy, relevant contract and applicable legal rights and provide a reasoned response.

Escalate immediately where a case involves:
- Suspected fraud, payment compromise or account takeover.
- Unsafe, counterfeit, prohibited or potentially harmful products.
- Serious injury or a credible safety risk.
- Personal data exposure.
- A missing parcel with disputed custody.
- Repeated seller or delivery-provider misconduct.
- A legal or regulatory notice.

Customers retain the right to use applicable external consumer complaint and dispute-resolution channels. Nothing in this policy requires a customer to waive statutory rights or prevents a lawful complaint to a regulator or court.

## 18. Abuse prevention without unfair treatment

Everneed may investigate suspected return or refund abuse, including repeated false claims, altered evidence, collusion, payment fraud or deliberate damage after delivery.

Any protective action must be based on relevant evidence and proportionate to the risk. Do not automatically reject a legitimate complaint because a customer has made earlier returns. Do not publish or share accusations without a proper basis. Allow a user to explain or challenge an adverse decision, except where temporary action is necessary to prevent immediate harm or comply with law.

## 19. Records, privacy and security

Return, refund and dispute cases may contain order details, contact information, messages, images, payment references and delivery evidence. These records must be handled under the Everneed Privacy Notice and access-control rules.

Only authorised personnel should access a case. Sellers and delivery providers should receive only the information necessary to investigate their part of the transaction. Do not expose one seller's private records or another customer's information to unrelated sellers.

Retain records according to an approved schedule that accounts for consumer claims, accounting, tax, payment-provider disputes, security and legal requirements. Do not keep case evidence indefinitely without a documented reason.
