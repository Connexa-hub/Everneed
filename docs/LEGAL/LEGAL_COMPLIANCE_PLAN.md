## 1. Executive Purpose

This plan establishes a practical route for Everneed to become a trustworthy, legally prepared digital commerce ecosystem in Nigeria without placing unnecessary financial or administrative pressure on small sellers or customers.

Everneed's commercial objective is to create sustainable value and earn fairly from services it genuinely provides. Compliance should protect that objective, not become a reason to impose arbitrary charges, complicated onboarding, hidden deductions or excessive obligations on marketplace participants.

The guiding principle is:

> **Make participation accessible, make every charge understandable, protect both sides of the marketplace, and earn by delivering measurable value.**

This plan covers:
- Corporate identity, ownership and business registration.
- Marketplace role and contractual responsibilities.
- Seller fairness and customer protection.
- Privacy, data protection and cybersecurity.
- Payments, seller funds, refunds and settlement.
- Tax, accounting and financial records.
- Product safety, intellectual property and advertising.
- Delivery, hub operations, liability and insurance.
- People, contractors and intellectual property ownership.
- Regulatory monitoring, incident handling and launch approval.

This is a readiness framework, not a legal opinion or a substitute for advice from a Nigerian-qualified lawyer, accountant or licensed specialist. The precise obligations depend on Everneed's legal structure, actual business activities, transaction flows, data processing, products, locations and contracts.

## 3. The Fair Marketplace Compact

Everneed should adopt a written fairness standard as a core commercial policy. The marketplace must be useful to small merchants and customers, not merely extractive.

### 3.1 Seller-first accessibility

Recommended initial principles:

1. **No compulsory joining fee:** do not charge a seller merely to apply, create a basic store or be considered for the initial pilot.
2. **No compulsory subscription:** a seller must be able to participate in the basic marketplace without buying an upgrade.
3. **No compulsory advertising:** promoted listings and advertising must be optional and clearly labelled.
4. **No undisclosed commission:** disclose the rate, calculation base, deductions and when commission is earned before the seller accepts the terms.
5. **No charge for an uncompleted sale:** do not charge transaction commission on a cancelled, unpaid, reversed or otherwise non-qualifying order.
6. **No forced discounts:** sellers choose whether to participate in promotions and understand who funds each discount.
7. **No arbitrary deductions:** every seller-account debit must have a contractual basis, transaction reference and explanation.
8. **No indefinite withholding:** publish settlement timing, reserve rules if any, permitted reasons for a hold and a route to challenge errors.
9. **No forced fulfilment route:** allow direct seller delivery where it is suitable and agreed; do not charge sellers for hub services they did not use.
10. **No platform-fault penalty:** do not make sellers bear costs caused solely by Everneed's system, staff, hub or chosen carrier where the seller did not cause or contribute to the failure.
11. **Proportionate onboarding:** request only information reasonably needed for identity, seller eligibility, payment, safety, tax or legal obligations.
12. **Fair suspension and appeal:** give reasons, an opportunity to correct ordinary issues and a review route, except where immediate action is needed for safety, fraud, legal compliance or protection of users.

A seller may be responsible for losses caused by the seller's own proven breach, misrepresentation, negligence or failure to meet agreed obligations. Responsibility must be evidence-based, proportionate, disclosed and legally reviewed—not imposed automatically.

### 3.2 Customer-first transparency

Recommended customer principles:

- Show the seller identity and accurate product description.
- Show the complete payable amount before the customer commits, including delivery and other mandatory charges.
- Clearly distinguish optional add-ons from required charges.
- Explain whether an order will be delivered directly, through a hub or by another disclosed route.
- Give realistic delivery estimates only for areas and routes actually supported.
- Explain parcel count where a multi-seller order may arrive in more than one parcel.
- Provide an accessible support route and order reference.
- Publish clear cancellation, return, refund, complaint and dispute processes.
- Do not use preselected paid extras, misleading countdowns, false scarcity or confusing consent controls.
- Do not make a customer waive statutory rights through a disclaimer.
- Do not advertise a refund as completed until the payment provider confirms it.

### 3.3 Earn by creating value

Everneed should prioritise the following revenue sequence:

1. A modest, transparent commission on qualifying completed marketplace sales, once the marketplace demonstrably creates value.
2. A clearly disclosed delivery or fulfilment service margin only where Everneed actually coordinates or provides that service and can explain the cost.
3. Optional seller tools or subscriptions after sellers have a useful free/basic experience and understand the extra benefit.
4. Optional, clearly labelled promoted listings after there is meaningful customer traffic.
5. Business services and partnerships where there is genuine demand and clear value.

Do not use customer delivery collections or seller funds as disguised platform revenue. Do not introduce multiple small charges simply because they are technically possible.

## 4. Corporate Registration, Identity and Governance

### 4.1 Choose the appropriate legal structure

Before signing material contracts, collecting marketplace funds at scale or employing staff, obtain advice on the most appropriate legal form.

Options to discuss with a Nigerian corporate lawyer include:
- A registered business name for an individual or partnership activity.
- A private company limited by shares for a scalable venture intending to contract, hire, raise investment and allocate ownership.
- Other structures only where the actual purpose and statutory requirements justify them.

A business name and an incorporated company are not interchangeable. A company has its own legal personality; a registered business name does not create the same separate corporate entity. Confirm the legal and tax implications before choosing.

The founder should consider:
- Who legally owns the Everneed brand, domain, source code and other intellectual property.
- Whether any co-founder, employee, contractor or adviser has rights that need documenting.
- Share ownership, voting, founder vesting and decision rights if a company is formed.
- Whether the intended investor structure is compatible with the chosen entity.
- The cost and ongoing obligations of incorporation, annual returns, accounting and statutory filings.

### 4.2 CAC preparation

The official Corporate Affairs Commission provides the registration process and company/business-name services. Before filing:

1. Search and reserve an appropriate name.
2. Decide the legal form and ownership structure.
3. Prepare accurate registered-office, director, shareholder, proprietor or partner information as applicable.
4. Select suitable business activity descriptions with professional input.
5. Prepare constitutional and incorporation documents where required.
6. Review beneficial ownership and persons-with-significant-control disclosures where applicable.
7. Submit through the official CAC portal and pay official fees.
8. Obtain and securely retain the registration documents and certified extracts.
9. Confirm post-registration tax identification and other applicable registrations.
10. Set reminders for annual returns, statutory updates and record maintenance.

Do not use an invented registered address, nominee owner or inaccurate business activity to complete registration.

**Official reference:** [CAC company registration](https://cac.gov.ng/services/company-registration).

### 4.3 Governance and records

Maintain a secure corporate record set, appropriate to the chosen structure:
- Registration and constitutional documents.
- Ownership and beneficial ownership records.
- Founder and director decisions.
- Contracts, approvals and delegated authority.
- Accounting records and tax filings.
- Intellectual property assignments and licences.
- Material supplier, payment and delivery agreements.
- Insurance policies and claims.
- Regulatory correspondence and compliance assessments.
- Incident, complaint and dispute records.

Record major decisions in `docs/GOVERNANCE/DECISION_LOG.md`. Do not treat this internal Markdown file as a statutory register where the law requires a prescribed register or filing.

## 5. Marketplace Legal Role and Contract Architecture

Everneed must define its actual legal role in each part of the transaction. Calling itself “only a platform” does not automatically remove responsibilities created by its conduct, representations, payment handling, control over delivery or applicable law.

Obtain legal advice on whether Everneed acts as:
- Marketplace operator connecting independent sellers and customers.
- Agent or disclosed intermediary for specified activities.
- Provider of its own services, including platform, hub handling or delivery coordination.
- Seller/reseller or principal for any products it buys and resells.
- Data controller, joint controller or processor for different data activities.
- Contracting party for payment, delivery, support or other services.

Do not use one generic disclaimer to describe every relationship.

### 5.1 Required contract set

Prepare and legally review, as applicable:

| Document | Parties / purpose | Core protections |
|---|---|---|
| Seller Marketplace Agreement | Everneed and each seller | Roles, listings, commission, settlement, fulfilment, customer care, liability, suspension, termination and disputes |
| Customer Terms of Use | Everneed and customers | Platform role, ordering, payment, delivery, cancellations, returns, complaints and liability |
| Privacy Notice | Users and data subjects | Data collected, purposes, lawful bases, sharing, retention, rights and contact route |
| Cookie / SDK Notice | Website and app users, where relevant | Analytics, device identifiers, advertising technologies and consent/preferences |
| Payment Provider Agreement | Everneed and licensed provider | Merchant role, fees, settlement, refunds, chargebacks, reserves, security and prohibited activities |
| Delivery / Carrier Agreement | Everneed and delivery partner | Service area, rates, custody, proof of delivery, insurance, loss, damage, failed attempts and data protection |
| Hub Services Agreement | Everneed and hub operator, if external | Receiving, inspection, custody, storage, security, access, incidents and service levels |
| Contractor / Employment Agreement | Everneed and personnel | Role, pay, confidentiality, IP, data access, termination and workplace obligations |
| IP Assignment / Licence | Founder, developer, designer, contractor or rights holder | Ownership or licence of code, designs, brand assets, content and deliverables |
| Seller Promotion / Advertising Terms | Everneed and participating sellers/advertisers | Opt-in, price, duration, placement, labelling, performance claims and cancellation |

Do not copy templates from another platform without adapting them to Everneed's real payment, seller and fulfilment model.

### 5.2 Seller agreement: minimum fairness clauses

The seller agreement should clearly state:

- The seller remains the seller of the listed goods unless a specific transaction is structured otherwise.
- The seller controls its product prices and stock, subject to lawful platform rules and truthful pricing.
- Everneed's commission, calculation base and earning event.
- All other optional fees and services, with no hidden deductions.
- Who funds promotions and discounts.
- How orders are accepted, prepared, cancelled and handed over.
- Whether direct delivery, seller drop-off or arranged collection is available.
- When custody and risk transfer at each handover.
- How customer complaints, returns, refunds and defective products are handled.
- What information Everneed shares with the seller and why.
- Seller access to order, sales, commission and settlement statements.
- Payout timing, reconciliation, permitted holds and dispute procedures.
- Fair notice and appeal for policy changes, restrictions or suspension.
- Termination rights and handling of open orders, seller balances and customer cases.
- Intellectual property and permission to display store/product content.
- Applicable law and practical dispute resolution.

Avoid unilateral rights allowing Everneed to change commission, retain balances indefinitely, impose unexplained penalties or cancel earned payouts without a defined legal and contractual basis.

### 5.3 Customer terms: minimum protections

Terms should explain:
- Everneed's identity and contact details.
- The role of independent sellers.
- Who supplies each product.
- How product prices, delivery fees, taxes and optional charges are shown.
- When an order is accepted and when payment is confirmed.
- What happens if a seller cannot fulfil an item.
- How multi-seller parent orders and separate parcels work.
- Delivery estimates and service-area limits.
- Cancellation, returns, refunds, complaints and dispute escalation.
- Privacy and marketing preferences.
- Any legally required limitation or exclusion of liability, brought to the customer's attention before purchase and never used to waive non-excludable rights.

## 6. Nigerian Consumer Protection

The Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Act 2018 (FCCPA) is a central consumer-protection statute. The FCCPC publishes the Act, guidance and consumer rights information.

The FCCPC's published summary highlights rights including understandable information, full price disclosure, transaction records, fair dealings, accurate representations, quality and safety, and return/refund protections subject to the law and applicable circumstances.

Official references:
- [FCCPA and resources](https://fccpc.gov.ng/resources-library/fccpa/)
- [Consumer rights and responsibilities](https://fccpc.gov.ng/consumers/consumer-rights-responsibilities/rights-responsibilities/)
- [Business obligations](https://fccpc.gov.ng/businesses/business-obligations/)

### 6.1 Product and price disclosures

Before the customer orders, the product page and checkout should disclose:
- Seller identity.
- Product name, material features, condition and relevant specifications.
- Whether the product is new, used, refurbished or reconditioned.
- Variant, size, quantity and availability.
- Accurate images and material limitations.
- Product warranty or guarantee information where offered.
- Merchandise subtotal, discount, delivery, mandatory service charges and applicable taxes.
- Total amount payable.
- Delivery route and estimate where confirmed.

The checkout total must not be materially different from the displayed total without the customer's informed approval.

### 6.2 Returns, cancellations and refunds

Create a legally reviewed policy that distinguishes:
- Customer change of mind.
- Defective, unsafe, damaged or materially misdescribed goods.
- Wrong item or missing quantity.
- Seller cancellation or inability to fulfil.
- Delivery failure.
- Fraud, unauthorised transaction or payment reversal.
- Digital services or product categories with different legal treatment.

Do not adopt a blanket “no refund/no return” rule. Do not assume every return must be accepted in every circumstance either. The applicable rights, reasonable time, condition requirements, statutory exceptions and remedy depend on the FCCPA, transaction and product facts.

For each case:
1. Give the customer a clear case reference.
2. Identify the affected seller item/package.
3. Preserve order, payment and delivery evidence.
4. Apply the published policy and statutory rights.
5. Identify the party responsible based on facts and contract.
6. Process the appropriate remedy through the approved payment method.
7. Confirm the provider has completed the refund before describing it as paid.
8. Update seller balances and platform accounting transparently.
9. Record the decision and give the customer a route to challenge it.

### 6.3 Complaints and redress

Provide a visible customer support channel, a case-tracking method and a defined escalation owner. Keep complaints accessible to the team responsible for resolution.

Do not make customers contact multiple sellers, couriers and platform teams without coordination. Everneed should own the customer-facing complaint process even where the underlying issue must be investigated with a seller or carrier.

### 6.4 Promotions and advertising

Ensure product claims, endorsements, discounts, rankings and paid placements are truthful and not misleading.

The FCCPC lists sales-promotion registration among its business obligations. Obtain legal advice and confirm current FCCPC requirements before running raffles, prize promotions, competitions, major discount campaigns or other regulated promotional activity. Do not assume every ordinary price reduction is treated identically.

Clearly label paid placement and sponsored content. Never sell an “organic” ranking while secretly giving it paid priority.

**Regulatory watch:** FCCPC has published 2026 exposure drafts for proposed consumer-protection and authorisation/exemption guidance instruments. An exposure draft is not automatically binding final regulation. Monitor official publication and commencement status before treating any draft as law.

## 7. Data Protection and Privacy

The Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 (NDPA) is the principal Nigerian personal-data law. The NDPC's General Application and Implementation Directive 2025 (GAID) provides further implementation requirements and became operative after its transition period.

Official references:
- [NDPC official site and guidance](https://ndpc.gov.ng/)
- [NDPA and GAID 2025 — official NDPC document](https://ndpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/NDP-ACT-GAID-2025-MARCH-20TH.pdf)
- [NDPC Guidance Notice on DCPMI registration](https://ndpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/NDP-ACT-GAID-2025-MARCH-20-1.pdf)

### 7.1 Map the data Everneed handles

Likely data categories include:
- Customer name, email, telephone number and delivery address.
- Seller identity, business, contact and payout details.
- Product orders, messages, complaints and support records.
- Payment references and transaction status.
- Device, session, security and diagnostic data.
- Delivery status, recipient confirmation and proof-of-delivery information.
- Staff, contractor and partner account information.
- Marketing preferences and waitlist information.

Do not collect identity documents, precise location, sensitive information or additional profile details merely because they might be useful later. Collect the minimum necessary for a defined purpose.

### 7.2 Identify data roles by activity

For each processing activity, document whether Everneed is:
- A controller deciding why and how data is processed.
- A joint controller with another organisation for a shared purpose.
- A processor acting on another controller's documented instructions.

A seller may be an independent controller for its own customer records. Everneed may separately be a controller for account administration, platform security, marketplace support or its own analytics. A delivery provider may be a processor or an independent controller depending on its actual role.

Do not assume every seller, PSP, courier, cloud host or analytics vendor has the same legal role. Put the role, permitted use, security and deletion obligations into the relevant agreement.

### 7.3 Privacy notice and lawful basis

Prepare a plain-language privacy notice that explains:
- Who controls the data and how to contact them.
- What information is collected and from which sources.
- Why each category is used.
- The lawful basis relied upon for each purpose.
- Which sellers, couriers, payment providers, cloud services and other recipients receive data.
- Whether data is transferred outside Nigeria and the applicable safeguards.
- How long records are retained and why.
- How users can exercise their rights and complain.
- Which communications are transactional and which are marketing.

Consent is not the only possible lawful basis. Do not use a single blanket consent checkbox for every activity. Where consent is the appropriate basis, make it specific, informed, freely given, recorded and withdrawable.

### 7.4 Data subject rights

Build a practical process to receive, verify and respond to requests concerning applicable rights, including access, correction, deletion, restriction, objection, portability and withdrawal of consent where relevant.

The process should:
1. Record the request and date.
2. Verify identity proportionately.
3. Identify systems, vendors and records involved.
4. Assess legal retention, third-party rights and applicable exceptions.
5. Respond within the statutory timeframe.
6. Record action taken and any lawful reason for refusal or limitation.

Do not promise deletion where records must lawfully be retained, such as certain accounting, payment, fraud or dispute records. Explain the applicable limitation and retain only what is required.

### 7.5 Security and breach response

Minimum controls:
- Role-based access and least privilege.
- Strong authentication for privileged accounts.
- Separate production and test credentials.
- No payment secrets or private keys in client-side code or public repositories.
- Encryption in transit and appropriate protection at rest.
- Secure backups and tested restoration.
- Audit logging for administrator, payout, refund and seller-account changes.
- Access reviews and prompt removal of departed personnel/partners.
- Vendor due diligence and data-processing terms.
- Secure software development, dependency updates and vulnerability handling.
- Data minimisation in logs, screenshots, analytics and support exports.

Create a breach response plan that identifies the incident lead, containment steps, evidence preservation, risk assessment, regulator notification obligations and communication to affected individuals. Legal deadlines and notification thresholds must be confirmed against the NDPA and GAID for the specific incident; do not invent a universal deadline.

### 7.6 NDPC registration and DPO assessment

Do not assume Everneed is automatically exempt or automatically required to register as a Data Controller or Processor of Major Importance (DCPMI). The NDPA, GAID and current NDPC guidance use statutory criteria and regulator designations. The current NDPC guidance includes processing-volume and activity-based criteria; the correct category and obligations must be assessed against Everneed's actual data and services.

Before launch:
- Map data volumes and processing activities.
- Review the current NDPC Guidance Notice and classification criteria.
- Determine whether DCPMI registration is required and which tier applies.
- Determine whether a Data Protection Officer (DPO) is required and whether a qualified external resource is appropriate.
- Confirm audit/return, renewal and change-notification obligations for the applicable category.
- Record the conclusion and review it as the platform grows.

Do not pay a third party claiming mandatory registration without verifying its authority, service and the current official NDPC requirement.

### 7.7 Children and vulnerable users

Set an age and account policy with legal advice. Avoid knowingly collecting children's personal data or designing marketing directed at children without the required safeguards and lawful basis. Where a service may be accessed by children, implement age-appropriate notices and appropriate consent/verification measures as required by law.

## 8. Payments, Seller Funds and Financial Regulation

This is a high-priority legal workstream because Everneed's planned model involves customer payments, independent sellers, refunds, commissions and possible delivery collections.

### 8.1 Use appropriately licensed payment providers

Everneed should use a payment provider authorised for the relevant services and ensure the proposed marketplace configuration is permitted by the provider's terms and applicable CBN rules.

Before enabling live transactions, obtain written confirmation from the provider on:
- The correct merchant/account structure for a multi-seller marketplace.
- Whether split settlement or sub-account arrangements are supported.
- Who is the merchant of record or contracting payee for each transaction.
- Whether Everneed may collect customer funds and under what authority.
- How seller settlements are initiated and evidenced.
- Refunds, partial refunds, reversals, chargebacks and disputes.
- Reserves, settlement delays, fees and account restrictions.
- Required seller KYC and prohibited categories.
- Reconciliation reports and webhook/event responsibilities.

Do not assume that a standard merchant account automatically authorises Everneed to operate a wallet, escrow, payment aggregation, stored-value or money-transfer service.

### 8.2 Do not create an unlicensed wallet or deposit-taking model

Unless properly authorised and legally structured:
- Do not invite customers to store money in an Everneed wallet.
- Do not represent seller balances as bank deposits or guaranteed funds.
- Do not hold customer money indefinitely pending an informal release decision.
- Do not use seller or carrier funds to pay Everneed operating expenses.
- Do not provide credit, lending, cash advances or money transmission as an incidental marketplace feature.

Use provider-supported settlement arrangements and obtain specialist payments advice if the desired flow goes beyond ordinary marketplace collection and settlement.

### 8.3 Seller settlement fairness

Publish a settlement policy before a seller accepts orders. It should specify:
- The settlement trigger.
- The expected settlement timetable.
- The calculation of commission and permitted deductions.
- The treatment of delivery collections and carrier pass-through costs.
- Refund, chargeback and dispute adjustments.
- Any narrowly defined reserve or temporary hold.
- Evidence and notice required for a hold.
- A maximum review period where legally and contractually appropriate.
- How the seller can dispute a statement or deduction.
- What happens to open balances when a seller leaves.

Recommended design:
- No joining fee or mandatory subscription for basic participation.
- Commission only on the agreed qualifying completed sale.
- No commission on delivery charges unless expressly agreed and legally/accountingly appropriate.
- No fee for optional services the seller did not choose.
- Seller statements show gross merchandise value, discount funding, commission, refunds, other deductions and net payable.
- Pay sellers on a predictable published cycle, subject to provider settlement and genuine legal/fraud holds.
- Give a reason, evidence category and review contact for every exceptional hold.

Do not promise instant payouts if the provider, risk process or bank settlement cannot support them.

### 8.4 Payment security

- Never store card PAN, CVV, PIN or one-time passcodes.
- Use provider-hosted or provider-approved payment components.
- Verify webhooks server-side using the provider's current signature method.
- Make webhook processing idempotent.
- Reconcile provider events to internal order and settlement records.
- Separate payment initiated, payment confirmed, refunded, reversed and settled states.
- Do not fulfil based only on a browser redirect or client-side success message.
- Restrict access to keys, provider dashboards and refund actions.

### 8.5 Delivery collections

Delivery collections must be accounted for separately from merchandise and platform commission.

If Everneed collects a carrier charge as an agent or pass-through, do not automatically treat the full collection as revenue. If Everneed contracts to provide the delivery service and bears the relevant performance obligations, the accounting and tax treatment may differ. Obtain an accountant's written position for the chosen model.

## 9. Tax and Accounting Readiness

Nigeria's tax framework changed with the 2025 tax legislation, including the Nigeria Tax Act 2025 and Nigeria Tax Administration Act 2025. The official Nigeria Revenue Service lists the current tax laws. The applicability of a rate, threshold, exemption or filing depends on the entity, income, turnover, transaction and commencement provisions.

Official reference: [Nigeria Revenue Service — tax laws](https://www.nrs.gov.ng/tax-laws/nrs-act).

### 9.1 Obtain a tax classification memo

Before material trading, ask a Nigerian tax practitioner to document:
- The chosen legal form and taxpayer registration.
- Income tax treatment of the marketplace and any logistics service.
- VAT treatment of commissions, subscriptions, advertising, delivery and other supplies.
- Whether Everneed is principal or agent for each transaction.
- Withholding tax obligations on relevant payments.
- PAYE and payroll obligations if staff are employed.
- State and local obligations where applicable.
- Invoicing and fiscal record requirements.
- Tax treatment of refunds, discounts, seller funds and carrier pass-through amounts.
- Any small-company relief, threshold or exemption and the conditions for qualifying.
- Required tax returns even where no tax is payable, where the law requires them.

Do not rely on old tax articles or automatically apply historic thresholds. Confirm the law in force for the relevant accounting period.

### 9.2 Separate accounting ledgers

Maintain distinct records for:
1. Marketplace merchandise transaction value.
2. Seller payable balances.
3. Everneed commission revenue.
4. Customer delivery charges.
5. Carrier/rider payable amounts.
6. Hub handling and packaging charges.
7. Payment-provider fees.
8. Refunds, reversals and chargebacks.
9. Promotional discounts and the party funding them.
10. Operating expenses and taxes.

Gross order value is not automatically company revenue. Money passing through an account is not automatically company income.

### 9.3 Recordkeeping

Retain invoices, receipts, provider statements, seller statements, contracts, payroll records, tax filings, expense evidence and reconciliation records for the statutory period applicable to each record. Set retention rules with the accountant and privacy adviser so legal recordkeeping does not become indefinite collection of personal data.

## 10. Product Safety, Restricted Goods and Seller Verification

Create a category and product compliance policy before activating sellers.

### 10.1 Seller checks proportionate to risk

Collect only what is necessary to:
- Identify the seller and authorised business contact.
- Confirm business legitimacy where appropriate.
- Validate payout details through the payment provider's required process.
- Assess restricted-category eligibility.
- Contact the seller for safety, recall or complaint issues.
- Meet applicable tax, consumer and legal obligations.

Use stronger checks for higher-risk categories. Do not make every small seller submit unnecessary documents or pay for third-party verification when a proportionate alternative is lawful.

### 10.2 Product controls

Define:
- Prohibited products.
- Restricted products requiring licences, age controls or specialist approval.
- Product categories requiring safety, standards, labelling or warranty evidence.
- Rules for counterfeit, stolen, recalled, unsafe or misrepresented goods.
- How customers report a listing.
- How listings are reviewed, suspended and appealed.
- How urgent safety recalls are handled.

Do not assume that a seller's declaration removes Everneed's own legal obligations.

### 10.3 Product claims and intellectual property

Seller terms should require sellers to have the right to sell products and use submitted images, descriptions, logos and trademarks.

Everneed should:
- Provide a notice-and-report process for infringement.
- Remove or restrict content where legally required or credibly unsafe.
- Preserve evidence and communicate decisions.
- Avoid using seller content beyond the agreed marketplace licence.
- Obtain rights for Everneed-created photographs, designs, software and marketing assets.
- Keep records of licences for fonts, images, music, code and other third-party material.

## 11. Advertising, Rankings and Commercial Fairness

A marketplace may earn from optional promoted listings, advertising and seller services, but commercial ranking must not undermine customer trust.

Policy requirements:
- Clearly label sponsored or paid placement.
- Explain material ranking factors in plain language where appropriate.
- Do not promise guaranteed sales, search position or conversion.
- Do not sell fake reviews, ratings or engagement.
- Do not suppress a seller solely for declining a paid upgrade.
- Do not make organic visibility secretly conditional on buying advertising.
- Give sellers clear prices, duration, placement and cancellation terms.
- Do not use customer personal data for targeted advertising without a lawful basis and required transparency/consent.
- Obtain regulatory advice before running regulated sales promotions, prize draws or competitions.

A seller's basic ability to operate and receive fair marketplace exposure should not depend on paying for optional advertising.

## 12. Delivery, Hub and Logistics Legal Readiness

Everneed's intended multi-seller model may involve sellers sending packages to an Everneed hub, where packages are received, checked, consolidated and dispatched. Single-seller orders may use direct seller delivery or hub-assisted delivery.

The legal structure must match what physically happens.

### 12.1 Before a hub opens

Confirm:
- Who operates and controls the premises.
- Whether the premises may lawfully be used for the proposed activity.
- Lease, licence, landlord and local authority requirements.
- Fire, health, workplace and building safety obligations.
- Security, access, CCTV and personal-data notices.
- Storage, sanitation, pest control and waste arrangements appropriate to the goods.
- Staff/contractor status and safety responsibilities.
- Opening hours, receiving cut-offs and capacity.
- Insurance for premises, goods in custody, public liability and employees where applicable.
- Procedures for restricted, hazardous, fragile, perishable or high-value goods.

### 12.2 Carrier and rider arrangements

For each delivery partner, verify:
- Legal identity and authority to provide the service.
- Operating area and vehicle/driver documentation as applicable.
- Insurance and exclusions.
- Service levels, rates and surcharges.
- Package acceptance and prohibited-goods rules.
- Custody transfer and proof of delivery.
- Lost, stolen, damaged and delayed package process.
- Failed delivery and return-to-sender rules.
- Data confidentiality and permitted use of customer information.
- Subcontracting and responsibility for subcontractors.
- Complaint handling, claims and termination rights.

Do not describe a partner as insured or vetted until the relevant documents have been checked and remain current.

### 12.3 Custody and liability

Define when custody transfers:
- Seller to seller-arranged collector.
- Seller to Everneed hub.
- Hub to carrier.
- Carrier to customer or authorised recipient.
- Carrier/hub back to seller during a return.

Record package reference, condition, sender, receiver, time and exception evidence at each material handover.

Contracts must allocate responsibility fairly and consistently with applicable law. Do not use a clause that attempts to exclude liability for fraud, negligence, misrepresentation or other liability that cannot lawfully be excluded.

### 12.4 Do not launch an unsupported delivery promise

Do not advertise nationwide delivery, guaranteed delivery windows, insured shipments, same-day service or one-package consolidation until actual route capacity, terms, costs and performance support the claim.

## 13. Employment, Contractors and Intellectual Property

As Everneed grows, distinguish employees, independent contractors, advisers, agencies and commercial partners correctly. Labels in a contract do not override the real working relationship or applicable law.

For each engagement:
- Define scope, deliverables, fees and payment milestones.
- Confirm confidentiality and data access.
- State who owns work product and when ownership transfers.
- Include IP assignment or an appropriate licence.
- Confirm third-party and open-source components are disclosed.
- Define security, access, offboarding and return/deletion of company data.
- Address conflicts of interest and permitted portfolio use.
- Obtain legal review of employment, tax, pension, workplace and termination obligations where applicable.

The company should obtain written IP assignments or licences from anyone who contributes material code, designs, brand assets, content or other work. Do not assume paying an invoice automatically transfers all intellectual property rights.

## 14. Cybersecurity and Platform Governance

Legal readiness depends on the actual product architecture and operational controls.

Priority controls:
- Enforce role permissions on the server/database, not only in the interface.
- Prove one seller cannot read another seller's private orders, customer details or earnings.
- Restrict administrators and require strong authentication.
- Keep audit trails for approvals, suspensions, refunds, payout changes and manual balance adjustments.
- Secure uploads and validate file type, size and access.
- Protect API keys and payment secrets.
- Test backup restoration and incident response.
- Maintain dependency and vulnerability management.
- Separate test data from real customer/seller information.
- Review third-party analytics, messaging, hosting and support vendors.
- Provide a process for vulnerability reports and responsible disclosure.

The current product status record identifies several production and live-operation items as not verified. Legal readiness must therefore include technical evidence; the existence of code, a migration or an interface is not proof of compliance.

## 15. Cross-Border Expansion

Do not automatically extend Nigerian terms and controls to Ghana, Kenya, South Africa, the UK or the US.

Before serving a new country, assess:
- Local entity, branch, tax and permanent-establishment implications.
- Consumer, e-commerce and marketplace laws.
- Privacy and cross-border data-transfer requirements.
- Payment licensing and local settlement rules.
- Foreign exchange and currency display/collection.
- Import, customs, duties and product standards.
- Restricted goods and seller verification.
- Delivery partner, insurance and product liability.
- Local language, disclosures, returns and dispute resolution.
- Local support capacity and emergency escalation.

Country launch requires a separate legal and operational readiness record. A customer being able to access the app from another country does not mean Everneed is ready to serve that market.

## 17. Recommended Low-Pressure Commercial Policy

This is the recommended commercial starting position for the founder to consider and formally approve.

### 17.1 Seller participation

- Basic seller application: free.
- Basic store presence: free during initial validation.
- Basic product listing: free within a reasonable fair-use policy.
- Optional premium services: defer until there is proven demand and clear added value.
- Paid advertising: optional and clearly labelled.
- Commission: no rate approved yet; test seller willingness and actual marketplace economics before deciding.
- Seller onboarding: provide simple guidance and avoid requiring paid training.
- Fulfilment: seller may use direct delivery where appropriate; charge only for hub/collection services actually selected or agreed.
- Promotions: opt-in, with discount funding agreed in advance.

This approach reduces friction while Everneed builds seller supply, trust and transaction evidence. It does not mean every service must remain free forever. Any future charge should be introduced transparently, prospectively and with a clear value proposition.

### 17.2 Customer participation

- No customer membership fee required for ordinary shopping unless a future optional service has a clear benefit.
- No hidden checkout or handling charges.
- Delivery pricing shown before payment.
- No forced tips, donations or add-ons.
- Clear cancellation and refund information.
- A visible support route.
- No paid ranking presented as neutral search results.

### 17.3 Protecting Everneed's margin

A low-pressure model still needs sustainable economics. Before approving a commission or delivery price, calculate:

`Net Marketplace Contribution = Earned Commission - Payment Costs - Marketplace Variable Costs - Refund/Dispute Costs`

`Net Logistics Contribution = Earned Delivery/Handling Revenue - Carrier/Rider Cost - Hub Labour - Packaging - Transfer Cost - Failed Delivery/Return Cost`

`Seller Net Proceeds = Eligible Merchandise Collections - Disclosed Commission - Seller-Agreed Deductions - Refund/Adjustments`

These are management formulas, not tax or statutory accounting definitions. An accountant must confirm the final accounting treatment.

Do not subsidise every order indefinitely. Instead:
- Start with a narrow service area.
- Negotiate transparent partner rates.
- Consolidate eligible multi-seller orders efficiently.
- Reduce avoidable failed deliveries through address confirmation.
- Keep hub processes lean and measurable.
- Introduce optional services only when customers or sellers value them.
- Pause routes or categories that lose money or create unacceptable risk.
- Use actual completed-order data before setting fees.

The goal is a business that earns through efficiency, reliability and useful services—not by surprising or overcharging participants.

## 20. Legal Review Questions for Professional Advisers

### Corporate lawyer
- Which legal structure best supports a founder-led Nigerian marketplace intending to raise investment?
- What registration, beneficial ownership, annual return and post-incorporation obligations apply?
- How should the company describe its role in seller transactions and hub-supported fulfilment?
- What consumer and marketplace liabilities may attach to Everneed's actual activities?
- Which contract terms are enforceable and fair under Nigerian law?

### Payments lawyer / PSP compliance team
- Is the proposed multi-seller collection and settlement flow permitted under the PSP's licence and terms?
- Who is merchant of record and who owes the customer the refund?
- Can Everneed collect and settle seller proceeds, or must funds flow through a provider-controlled split settlement structure?
- Would any proposed wallet, escrow, reserve or stored-value feature trigger additional regulation?
- How should chargebacks, failed payments and partial refunds be handled?

### Privacy adviser / DPO
- Which entities are controllers, joint controllers or processors for each data flow?
- Does Everneed meet current DCPMI criteria and, if so, which tier and obligations apply?
- Is a DPO required, and what audit/return and renewal duties apply?
- What safeguards are required for cloud hosting and cross-border transfers?
- What breach and data-subject request procedures should be implemented?

### Tax adviser
- How are commission, subscriptions, promoted listings, delivery and hub fees treated under current tax law?
- Which amounts are gross revenue versus seller or carrier liabilities?
- What VAT, withholding, income tax, payroll and filing duties apply?
- Which small-company reliefs or thresholds may apply, and what conditions or exclusions matter?
- What invoices and transaction records must Everneed issue and retain?

### Logistics / insurance adviser
- What licences, permits, local approvals and safety controls apply to the proposed hub and routes?
- What insurance is available for premises, goods in custody, carrier transit, public liability and staff?
- At which point does custody and liability transfer?
- What exclusions, claims evidence and service levels should the contracts contain?
