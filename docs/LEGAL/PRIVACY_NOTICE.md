## 2. Who and what this notice covers

This notice is intended to cover people who interact with Everneed, including:

- Visitors to the public website or application.
- Prospective and registered customers.
- People who place, receive or enquire about an order.
- Sellers, store owners and prospective sellers.
- Employees or authorised representatives of seller businesses.
- People who contact support or communicate with a user.
- Delivery recipients and other people whose details are supplied for fulfilment.
- People who apply to join a waitlist, where that feature is actually enabled.

This notice applies to the Everneed website, Android application, seller portal, administration and support tools, and related services only to the extent those surfaces are operated and process personal data. Separate notices or contractual terms may apply to a specific service or third-party provider.

It does not automatically cover a seller's independent website, a payment provider's own services, a courier's own systems, or a third-party social network. Those organisations should provide their own privacy information for their separate processing.

## 4. Where personal data comes from

Depending on the feature and actual configuration, information may come from:

1. **Directly from you:** when you create an account, submit a seller application, enter an address, place an order, contact support, submit a review or complete a form.
2. **Generated through use:** account identifiers, order references, timestamps, security logs, transaction state and records of actions taken on the Platform.
3. **From a seller:** information necessary to fulfil an order, respond to a complaint or resolve a product or delivery issue.
4. **From a payment provider:** payment status, transaction references, refund status and reconciliation information. The exact fields and provider flow must be confirmed.
5. **From a delivery or fulfilment provider:** collection, parcel, route, delivery-attempt and proof-of-delivery updates, where such a provider is actually used.
6. **From integrations you choose to use:** for example, social sign-in or connected services only if those integrations are actually enabled and disclosed.
7. **From public or verification sources:** only where lawful, proportionate and relevant to seller verification, fraud prevention or a legal obligation.

We should explain at the point of collection whether a field is required, optional, or required only for a particular service. If you do not provide information essential to an order or seller service, we may be unable to complete that service.

## 5. Why we use personal data and our lawful bases

The NDPA requires a lawful basis for each processing purpose. The basis must be assessed for the actual activity; consent is not a blanket basis for all Platform processing.

| Purpose | Examples | Potential lawful basis — subject to documented assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Create and manage accounts | Registration, authentication, profile settings and account support | Performance of a contract or steps requested before entering a contract; legitimate interests for proportionate security |
| Operate marketplace features | Display stores and products, manage listings, carts, checkout and orders | Contract or pre-contractual steps; legitimate interests for platform integrity where balanced against users' rights |
| Fulfil orders | Share necessary order details with the relevant seller and, where used, fulfilment or delivery providers | Contract; legal obligations where applicable |
| Process and reconcile payments | Send payment instructions to a licensed provider, confirm payment status, handle refunds and reconcile records | Contract; legal obligation; legitimate interests in accurate financial controls |
| Onboard and support sellers | Review applications, manage stores, communicate decisions and provide seller support | Contract or pre-contractual steps; legal obligation where applicable; legitimate interests where balanced |
| Protect the Platform | Detect abuse, investigate suspicious activity, secure accounts, prevent fraud and maintain audit records | Legitimate interests following a documented necessity and balancing assessment; legal obligation where applicable |
| Handle complaints and disputes | Investigate order issues, returns, refunds, delivery incidents and claims | Contract; legal obligation; legitimate interests in resolving disputes and protecting rights |
| Meet legal and regulatory duties | Accounting, tax, lawful requests and recordkeeping | Compliance with a legal obligation |
| Send service communications | Order updates, security notices, important account or policy notices | Contract and/or legitimate interests, depending on the message |
| Send marketing | Promotional emails, SMS, push messages or personalised offers | Consent where required by law, or another specifically assessed lawful basis where permitted; provide an effective opt-out |
| Improve the service | Diagnostics, performance analysis, usability research and product improvement | Legitimate interests only after necessity and balancing assessment; consent where required for cookies, SDKs or similar technologies |
| Operate a waitlist or event | Register interest and respond to a request | Steps requested by the individual, consent where appropriate, or another documented basis depending on the form |

Where we rely on legitimate interests, we should record the specific interest, why the processing is necessary, whether a less intrusive method is available, the impact on individuals, reasonable expectations and safeguards. We should not rely on legitimate interests where the individual's rights and interests override the interest.

Where consent is required, it must be freely given, specific, informed, unambiguous and capable of withdrawal. Refusing optional marketing or non-essential tracking should not prevent access to core marketplace functions. Withdrawal does not make earlier lawful processing unlawful.

## 7. Payments and financial records

Payments may be handled through a third-party payment provider. The provider's own privacy notice and terms also apply to its processing.

Everneed should receive and retain only the payment references and status information reasonably needed for order confirmation, refunds, reconciliation, fraud prevention and legal recordkeeping. Full card numbers, CVV values, payment PINs and other authentication secrets must not be stored in Everneed application databases, logs, support records or analytics.

A payment success screen shown by a user's device is not, by itself, proof of payment. The server-side provider confirmation and reconciliation process must determine transaction status.

Seller proceeds, carrier funds, customer refunds and platform earnings must be distinguished in financial records. Personal data in these records must be access-controlled and retained only under a documented schedule.

## 8. International transfers and remote access

Some hosting, software, support or other service providers may process or access data from outside Nigeria. The actual locations, subprocessors, support access and transfer arrangements are not verified by this draft.

Before enabling a provider or international transfer, Everneed must:
- Identify the countries where data is stored, accessed or transferred.
- Determine whether the arrangement is a cross-border transfer under the NDPA.
- Assess the permitted transfer mechanism and required safeguards.
- Review the provider's contract, security controls, subprocessors and deletion/return terms.
- Complete any required transfer risk assessment, notice or documentation.
- Avoid transferring more data than the service requires.

Do not state that data is stored only in Nigeria, or that it is transferred to a particular country, unless supported by current provider and configuration evidence. Do not assume that a provider's global infrastructure makes every transfer automatically lawful.

## 9. Data retention and deletion

We keep personal data only for as long as it is reasonably necessary for the purpose for which it was collected, including applicable legal, accounting, security, contractual and dispute requirements. When data is no longer needed, it should be securely deleted, anonymised or otherwise disposed of in accordance with a documented schedule.

The following is a **proposed retention framework**, not a statement of current system settings or approved retention periods:

| Record category | Proposed retention criterion | Required decision / verification |
|---|---|---|
| Account and profile | While the account is active and for a defined period after closure where needed for disputes, fraud prevention or legal duties | Set a deletion workflow and approved post-closure period |
| Seller onboarding and verification | While the seller relationship is active and for the period required to evidence eligibility, contractual duties and legal obligations | Identify required documents, access restrictions and statutory periods |
| Orders and transaction records | For the period required by applicable tax, accounting, consumer, payment and dispute rules | Obtain accountant/legal advice and define the statutory schedule |
| Payment references and reconciliation | For the period necessary for payment disputes, refunds, reconciliation and legal duties | Confirm provider records, financial controls and legal periods |
| Delivery and proof-of-delivery records | Until fulfilment disputes and claims are resolved, plus any justified legal period | Define evidence type, retention and redaction |
| Support and complaint records | Until the case is closed and for a justified limitation, regulatory or quality period | Define severity-based periods and access roles |
| Security and access logs | A proportionate period based on threat detection, incident investigation and legal needs | Set log retention and prevent indefinite collection |
| Marketing preferences and suppression records | Until withdrawal or preference change; retain minimal suppression data as needed to honour opt-outs | Document how opt-outs are propagated |
| Waitlist and enquiry records | Until the request is fulfilled, withdrawn or no longer relevant, subject to any legal requirement | Confirm whether the feature is live and set a deletion rule |
| Backups and archives | According to a documented backup rotation and secure expiry process | Confirm backup schedule, restore controls and deletion propagation |

Retention periods must be set with qualified advice and actual system capabilities in mind. A backup may retain a deleted record for a limited rotation period; this must be disclosed where relevant and the record must not be restored into active use without appropriate controls.

A legal hold, active complaint, fraud investigation or regulatory requirement may justify retaining particular records for longer. The reason, scope, approver and review date should be documented.

## 10. Security and confidentiality

Everneed intends to use appropriate technical and organisational measures proportionate to the nature, volume and risks of the personal data processed. Controls should include, where implemented and verified:

- Role-based access and least-privilege permissions.
- Separation between customer, seller and administrator data.
- Server-side authorisation and database row-level security where applicable.
- Secure authentication, session management and account recovery.
- Encryption in transit and suitable protection at rest through the relevant infrastructure.
- Secret management; no API secrets or private keys in public client code or repositories.
- Input validation, output encoding and secure file-upload controls.
- Audit records for privileged actions and sensitive changes.
- Dependency updates, vulnerability review, backups and tested recovery.
- Staff and contractor confidentiality, training and prompt access revocation.
- Incident detection, response, evidence preservation and regulatory assessment.

These are control objectives, not a warranty that every control is currently deployed or effective. The technical owner must maintain evidence of configuration, testing, access review, backup restoration and remediation. Do not describe the Platform as “fully secure” or “unhackable”.

## 12. Marketing and communication preferences

Everneed may need to send essential service communications such as account security notices, order updates, payment status, delivery exceptions, refund information and material policy changes. These are distinct from optional promotional messages.

Promotional email, SMS, push notifications or other direct marketing must use a lawful basis and comply with applicable consent, preference and opt-out rules. Where consent is required, it must not be bundled with acceptance of customer or seller terms.

Every marketing channel must provide a clear and usable way to stop further promotional messages. An opt-out should be honoured promptly and applied across relevant systems. A minimal suppression record may be retained where necessary to ensure the person is not contacted again.

Do not assume that a user who provides a phone number for delivery has agreed to receive unrelated marketing.

The Platform may use software rules to display products, sort information, route orders, flag unusual activity or support moderation. The actual rules, data inputs and effects must be documented.

Do not claim that Everneed does not profile users, personalise recommendations or use automated decision-making until the live application, analytics and third-party tools have been reviewed. Paid placement, if introduced, must be clearly distinguished from organic ranking.

The Platform is intended for people who can lawfully enter into the relevant account or transaction. The customer terms require users who cannot independently enter into a transaction to involve a parent or legal guardian where required.

Everneed must assess the NDPA rules and any applicable child-protection requirements before knowingly collecting children's personal data or offering services directed to children. Where a child's data is processed, apply the legally required age checks, parental or guardian authorisation where applicable, clear child-appropriate information and heightened safeguards.

## 16. Seller, hub and delivery partner responsibilities

A seller may receive customer information needed to fulfil an order. The seller must:
- Use it only for the relevant sale, delivery, support or lawful recordkeeping.
- Limit access to people who need it for that purpose.
- Keep it secure and prevent unauthorised disclosure.
- Not sell, rent, scrape, profile or independently market to the customer using Platform data without a separate lawful basis and any required notice or consent.
- Notify Everneed promptly of suspected loss, unauthorised access or disclosure involving Platform data.
- Cooperate with reasonable data-subject requests and incident investigations.
- Delete or return information when no longer required, subject to lawful retention duties.

A hub or delivery partner, where used, should receive only the package and recipient information needed for its assigned task. Its contract must address confidentiality, security, permitted use, subcontracting, incident notification, retention, deletion and assistance with rights requests.

The actual controller/processor roles and contract terms must be determined for each partner. This section does not imply that Everneed currently operates a physical hub, employs riders, or has an established courier network.

## 17. Personal data incidents and breaches

A personal data breach may include accidental loss, destruction, alteration, unauthorised disclosure, unauthorised access, account compromise, misdirected delivery information, exposed seller records or a compromised service provider.

Everneed must maintain an incident response procedure that:
1. Provides a monitored channel for reporting suspected incidents.
2. Restricts access and contains the incident without destroying evidence.
3. Records the discovery time, affected systems, categories and approximate scale of data, and likely consequences.
4. Assesses risks to affected individuals and the applicable legal notification requirements.
5. Notifies the NDPC and affected individuals within the time and circumstances required by the NDPA and applicable guidance.
6. Coordinates with relevant processors, sellers and providers.
7. Documents decisions, remediation, lessons learned and any required follow-up.
8. Reviews whether credentials, tokens, access permissions or exposed records need to be revoked or secured.

The response owner must verify the current statutory thresholds, notification deadlines and content requirements with qualified advice. Do not publish a specific response deadline here until confirmed against the current law and guidance.
