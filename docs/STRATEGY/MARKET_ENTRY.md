# EVERNEED
## Market Entry Strategy

**Document type:** Initial market selection and launch strategy  
**Document status:** Working strategy — location, categories and operating assumptions require founder approval and field validation  
**Business status:** Founder-led startup; not yet registered with Nigeria's Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC)  
**Initial country:** Nigeria  
**Initial product:** Multivendor digital marketplace with hub-supported fulfilment  
**Project reference branch:** `web-portal`  
**Last updated:** November 2026

---

## 1. Purpose

This document defines how Everneed should enter its first Nigerian market without expanding faster than its product, seller network and fulfilment operations can support.

Everneed's ambition is to become **a digital commerce ecosystem**. Market entry must begin with a focused, operationally manageable marketplace pilot. The first objective is not nationwide visibility; it is to prove that a defined group of sellers can list genuine products, customers can complete purchases, and orders can be fulfilled with clear accountability.

This strategy is a decision framework. It does not declare a launch city, hub address, delivery zone, launch date, seller cohort or operating partner that has not yet been confirmed.

## 2. Market Entry Principles

1. **Start narrow, learn quickly.** Select one practical launch area before considering additional cities or nationwide promotion.
2. **Build supply before creating broad demand.** Customers should encounter useful, available products from sellers who understand the operating process.
3. **Prove the complete transaction.** A working screen or successful sign-up is not proof of a functioning marketplace.
4. **Treat fulfilment as part of the product.** Seller handovers, hub custody, consolidation, packaging and final delivery must be designed and tested together.
5. **Be transparent.** Do not promise delivery coverage, timelines, returns, seller verification or payment outcomes that have not been operationally established.
6. **Protect the economics.** Track marketplace revenue separately from logistics collections and pass-through delivery costs.
7. **Expand by evidence, not excitement.** Each new area or category must pass product, supply, fulfilment, support and financial readiness checks.

## 3. First-Market Selection

### 3.1 Location decision

The first launch location remains an open founder decision. Choose the smallest city, district or connected set of neighbourhoods that can support a meaningful seller and customer pilot.

Assess candidate areas using the following criteria:

| Criterion | What to investigate | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Seller density | Number of suitable shops and merchants willing to participate | Determines whether the catalogue can be useful |
| Customer access | Reachable communities, existing seller customers and shopping demand | Helps generate early transactions without expensive mass advertising |
| Hub practicality | Affordable, secure and accessible location with suitable operating hours | Supports receiving, checking, sorting and dispatch |
| Seller-to-hub movement | Travel time, transport cost and ease of package drop-off | Affects seller participation and fulfilment cost |
| Last-mile capacity | Availability, reliability and cost of suitable riders or delivery partners | Determines whether customer promises can be met |
| Road and traffic conditions | Typical routes, peak periods and access constraints | Affects delivery reliability |
| Support coverage | Ability to handle enquiries, exceptions and returns during operating hours | Protects customer trust |
| Competitive context | Existing marketplaces, social commerce and local delivery alternatives | Helps define a credible value proposition |
| Regulatory and safety considerations | Applicable trading, premises, product and transport requirements | Reduces avoidable legal and operational risk |

Score each candidate area consistently, then validate the strongest option through seller visits, route checks and customer conversations. Do not select a location solely because it has a large population.

### 3.2 Service boundary

Before accepting live orders, define:

- Exact neighbourhoods or postcodes served.
- Whether sellers outside the area may join but remain inactive until coverage is available.
- Where the hub receives packages and its operating hours.
- Which routes support seller-to-hub movement.
- Which areas have last-mile delivery coverage.
- Any excluded products, destinations or delivery conditions.
- How customers are informed when an address is outside the service boundary.

The service boundary must be reflected consistently in the product, seller guidance, checkout and customer support scripts.

### 3.3 Location decision record

Record the final decision in the company decision log with:

- Selected launch area and boundaries.
- Alternative areas considered.
- Seller and customer evidence collected.
- Proposed hub arrangement and whether it is secured or still under consideration.
- Delivery coverage and partner status.
- Estimated operating costs and assumptions.
- Decision owner and review date.

Until this record is approved, the launch location should be described as **not yet selected**.

## 4. Initial Seller and Category Strategy

### 4.1 Seller profile

Prioritise independent retailers and small businesses that:

- Have genuine, lawful products and clear ownership or authority to sell them.
- Can provide accurate descriptions, images, prices and stock information.
- Can respond to orders within an agreed preparation window.
- Can package items safely and label them correctly.
- Understand the selected fulfilment route.
- Can provide a reliable contact for operational issues.
- Accept the marketplace's seller rules, customer service standards and commercial terms.

A seller's interest or application is not the same as approval, catalogue readiness or activation.

### 4.2 Candidate categories

Potential starting categories include:

- Fashion and accessories.
- Beauty and personal-care products.
- Home and household goods.
- General retail and everyday items.
- Electronics and accessories, subject to authenticity, warranty and product-safety controls.
- Specialist local products from established merchants.

These are candidate categories, not a final launch catalogue. Choose a limited number based on local seller availability, customer demand, product quality, fulfilment practicality and applicable restrictions.

Avoid categories that create disproportionate legal, safety, storage, handling or returns complexity during the first pilot.

### 4.3 Category selection test

For each candidate category, assess:

1. Are there enough credible sellers in the chosen area?
2. Can sellers keep prices and stock accurate?
3. Are product descriptions and variations easy to represent correctly?
4. Can items be packaged and transported safely?
5. Are returns, defects and warranty responsibilities clear?
6. Is there evidence of customer interest?
7. Can the category generate repeat or complementary purchases?

Select categories where the answers are strongest. Do not publish a broad catalogue merely to make the marketplace appear large.

## 5. Seller Cohort and Onboarding

### 5.1 Build a pilot cohort

Recruit a manageable first group of sellers that the founder and available operations capacity can support closely. The exact cohort size should be set after the launch area, support capacity and fulfilment process are understood.

Recruit for catalogue quality and operational reliability rather than registration count alone.

### 5.2 Seller onboarding sequence

1. Identify and contact a suitable business.
2. Explain Everneed's marketplace and fulfilment model without promising guaranteed sales.
3. Record seller interest and operational requirements.
4. Collect application and verification information through the approved process.
5. Review the application and approve or decline it.
6. Complete the store profile and seller contact details.
7. Prepare a small, quality-controlled initial catalogue.
8. Confirm prices, stock, product details and permitted categories.
9. Train the seller on order acceptance, preparation, packaging, labelling and handover.
10. Run a test order or operational rehearsal before the seller is treated as launch-ready.
11. Activate the seller only when the required product and operating checks pass.

### 5.3 Seller readiness checklist

A seller is ready for the pilot only when:

- The seller is approved under the defined onboarding rules.
- Store information is complete and accurate.
- Products have been reviewed and published through the intended workflow.
- Prices and stock are confirmed.
- Seller notifications and order access have been tested.
- The seller understands cancellation and customer issue escalation.
- The seller understands direct delivery versus hub handover.
- Package labelling and handover instructions have been provided.
- Commercial terms and settlement expectations have been explained and accepted.

## 6. Customer Entry Strategy

### 6.1 Start with reachable demand

The first customer audience should come from the selected launch area and the participating sellers' existing communities.

Initial acquisition channels may include:

- Seller WhatsApp status and customer communications, with appropriate consent.
- Store posters, QR codes and product links.
- Seller social media content.
- Founder-led demonstrations and local outreach.
- Referral invitations from early customers.
- Community channels where promotion is permitted.
- Useful product and store content that links directly to available listings.

Avoid large paid campaigns until product availability, checkout, payment, support and delivery are reliable.

### 6.2 Customer promise

The initial customer message should be based on verified capabilities:

- Discover products from participating independent sellers.
- See which seller offers each product.
- Shop through one connected marketplace experience.
- Receive clear order and delivery information for the areas actually served.
- Contact a defined support channel when something goes wrong.

Do not promise the same-day delivery, nationwide coverage, guaranteed savings, one-parcel delivery or universal returns unless the relevant service has been tested and can be consistently provided.

### 6.3 Customer trust essentials

Before public promotion, customers should be able to understand:

- Who the seller is.
- What product is being purchased and its total price.
- Which delivery method applies.
- Delivery charge and expected timing, where confirmed.
- How order status is communicated.
- How to contact support.
- How cancellations, returns, refunds and complaints are handled.

## 7. Hub and Fulfilment Pilot

The hub model is a defining part of Everneed's intended operating proposition. It must be proven in a small area before being used as a broad marketing promise.

### 7.1 Multi-seller order flow

For a customer order containing products from multiple sellers:

1. Checkout creates one customer-facing parent order.
2. The platform records separate seller orders or packages with their own item, quantity and seller references.
3. Each seller receives only the information and tasks needed for that seller's package.
4. Each seller prepares, checks and labels the package against the order reference.
5. The seller hands the package to the agreed collection service or drops it at the designated Everneed hub.
6. Hub staff record receipt, time, package condition and order reference.
7. Hub staff reconcile each package against the expected seller package and flag missing, damaged or incorrect items.
8. Eligible packages are consolidated and packed for the customer.
9. The consignment is assigned to a delivery route or partner.
10. Dispatch, delivery attempt, customer handover and proof of delivery are recorded.
11. The parent order presents a coherent customer view while retaining package-level accountability internally.

Where items cannot safely or practically be consolidated, use clearly identified separate parcels and communicate that to the customer. Never mark a package as received or delivered without the corresponding event.

### 7.2 Single-seller order flow

For an order from one seller, the agreed route may be:

- **Direct seller delivery:** when the seller can serve the destination reliably and the route is approved; or
- **Hub-assisted delivery:** when the seller requests it, distance or route conditions make it more suitable, or the operating rules require hub handling.

The selected route, responsible party, charge and tracking method must be known before the order is accepted. The platform should not imply hub handling when the package is travelling directly from the seller.

### 7.3 Minimum hub operating controls

Before receiving customer orders, establish:

- A confirmed and suitable operating location.
- Named responsibility for opening, closing and package custody.
- Receiving and dispatch logs.
- Unique order/package references.
- Package condition and discrepancy recording.
- Secure temporary storage and access control.
- Sorting and consolidation rules.
- Packaging materials and safe packing standards.
- Handover records for riders or delivery partners.
- Failed delivery, return-to-hub and lost-package escalation.
- Customer and seller communication procedures.
- Daily reconciliation of packages received, dispatched, delivered and outstanding.

If these controls cannot be staffed and maintained, do not activate hub-dependent live orders.

### 7.4 Delivery partner controls

Where external riders or carriers are used, assess:

- Service area and operating hours.
- Pricing structure and surcharges.
- Package size and weight limits.
- Proof-of-pickup and proof-of-delivery methods.
- Failed delivery and reattempt rules.
- Loss, damage and claims process.
- Cash handling, if any, and reconciliation.
- Customer communication and data handling.
- Insurance and contractual responsibility where applicable.

A partner discussion or quotation is not a signed agreement or proven service. Keep partner status explicit.

## 8. Product and Technology Launch Gates

The marketplace and web portal are under development. A feature is not launch-ready merely because its interface or database object exists.

Before the pilot accepts real orders, verify the following end-to-end.

### 8.1 Customer journey

- Customer can discover only approved stores and published products.
- Product identity, seller, price, stock and variations are accurate.
- Cart calculations are correct.
- Multi-seller checkout creates one parent order and the correct seller-specific records.
- Payment success, failure, cancellation and delayed confirmation are handled safely.
- The customer sees accurate order and package status.
- Customer support, cancellation and refund paths are available.

### 8.2 Seller journey

- Application submission and review work.
- Only approved sellers can operate approved stores.
- Seller can create and manage permitted products.
- Product review and publication states are reliable.
- Seller receives and can act on relevant orders.
- Seller can see only their own private orders, customer details, earnings and records.
- Seller understands package preparation and handover.
- Seller-facing status matches actual operational events.

### 8.3 Administration and security

- Privileged actions are restricted to authorised administrators and validated server-side.
- Row-level security prevents cross-seller access.
- Anonymous access exposes only intended public information.
- Payment amounts are recalculated and validated server-side.
- Payment webhooks verify authenticity and handle retries/idempotency.
- No card PAN or sensitive payment credentials are stored by Everneed.
- Refunds, disputes and adjustments have auditable records.
- Sensitive customer and seller data is not exposed through public pages or logs.

### 8.4 Operational technology

- Hub staff can identify each package and parent order.
- Package receipt, discrepancy, consolidation, dispatch and delivery events can be recorded.
- Customer-facing tracking does not show unverified events.
- Support staff can locate an order and identify its responsible seller and delivery party.
- The process has a manual fallback for outages, with later reconciliation.

All critical workflows must pass documented tests using realistic scenarios before live customer transactions begin.

## 9. Pilot Design

### 9.1 Pilot stages

| Stage | Scope | Exit evidence |
|---|---|---|
| A. Internal rehearsal | Founder, developer/test accounts and controlled test data | Critical customer, seller, admin and order flows work in a test environment |
| B. Seller preparation | Selected sellers; no broad public promotion | Approved stores, quality catalogue, trained sellers and confirmed handover process |
| C. Fulfilment rehearsal | Simulated or explicitly agreed test packages | Package custody, reconciliation, consolidation, dispatch and exception handling work |
| D. Closed customer pilot | Invited customers within the confirmed service boundary | Real transactions complete with support and fulfilment monitored |
| E. Controlled public launch | Public access within proven service coverage | Capacity, quality and unit economics remain within approved thresholds |
| F. Area expansion | Additional neighbourhoods or categories | Existing area remains stable and the new area passes readiness checks |

Stages are gates, not calendar promises. A stage should not advance simply because a target date has arrived.

### 9.2 Closed pilot rules

During the closed pilot:

- Limit customer access to the confirmed service area.
- Limit participating sellers to those who passed readiness checks.
- Set order volume according to real support and hub capacity.
- Monitor every order from payment through final resolution.
- Keep a record of late, missing, damaged, incorrect and cancelled packages.
- Contact customers after fulfilment to collect feedback.
- Pause new orders if a critical payment, security, custody or delivery issue appears.
- Resolve and document incidents before resuming affected workflows.

### 9.3 Pilot success measures

Set numeric thresholds only after baseline testing and partner capacity are known. Track at least:

**Marketplace**
- Approved sellers.
- Operational and activated sellers.
- Published products with confirmed stock.
- Product views and add-to-cart activity.
- Checkout completion.
- Paid and successfully completed orders.
- Cancellation, refund and dispute rates.
- Repeat customer rate.

**Seller experience**
- Seller response time.
- Order acceptance rate.
- Preparation time.
- Correct package handover rate.
- Seller retention and catalogue freshness.
- Seller feedback and support issues.

**Hub and delivery**
- Packages expected versus received.
- Package discrepancy and damage rates.
- Time from seller handover to hub receipt.
- Consolidation turnaround time.
- Dispatch-to-delivery time.
- First-attempt delivery success.
- Failed delivery and return rate.
- Cost per package and per completed consignment.

**Financial**
- Merchandise value, separately from platform revenue.
- Commission earned on eligible completed transactions.
- Delivery and hub fees collected.
- Carrier/rider costs.
- Hub labour, packaging and operating costs.
- Payment-provider fees, refunds and chargebacks.
- Contribution margin by marketplace order and logistics consignment.

Downloads, registrations, impressions and social followers may help explain acquisition, but they are not proof of commercial success.

## 10. Pricing and Commercial Readiness

Before the pilot, document and disclose the commercial terms that apply to participating sellers and customers.

Resolve:

- Seller commission basis and trigger.
- Any seller subscription or optional service charges.
- Which party funds discounts and promotions.
- Customer delivery charges by route or service area.
- Any seller-to-hub transfer charge.
- Hub handling, consolidation or packaging charges, if applicable.
- Payment-provider charges and their treatment.
- Refund, cancellation and failed-delivery cost allocation.
- Seller settlement timing and reconciliation.
- Tax and invoicing responsibilities.

Do not use delivery collections as a proxy for logistics profit. Calculate logistics contribution after rider/carrier costs, hub labour, packaging, failed attempts, returns and other attributable expenses. Keep pass-through collections distinct from Everneed's earned revenue.

No commission rate, subscription price or delivery tariff is approved by this strategy.

## 11. Customer Support and Incident Management

A small pilot still needs clear ownership.

Establish a support channel and operating hours before inviting customers. Prepare procedures for:

- Payment made but order not confirmed.
- Duplicate or delayed payment notifications.
- Seller does not accept or prepare an order.
- Package not handed over to the hub.
- Package missing, damaged or incorrect at receipt.
- Consolidation mismatch.
- Delivery delay or failed attempt.
- Customer unavailable or address issue.
- Cancellation, return or refund request.
- Suspected fraud, unsafe product or account compromise.

Every issue should have an owner, status, customer communication record and resolution outcome. Escalate payment, security, safety and package-loss incidents promptly. Pause the affected route or workflow when continuing would create unacceptable risk.

## 12. Legal, Trust and Compliance Preparation

Before commercial launch, obtain appropriate advice and complete the obligations that apply to the actual operating model. Areas to review include:

- CAC registration and appropriate business structure.
- Founder ownership, intellectual property and contractor agreements.
- Seller terms and customer terms.
- Privacy notice and personal-data handling.
- Consumer protection, returns, refunds and complaint handling.
- Product restrictions, authenticity and safety.
- Tax, invoicing and financial recordkeeping.
- Payment-provider and settlement arrangements.
- Hub premises, worker safety and package custody.
- Delivery partner agreements, liability and insurance.
- Advertising claims and promotional terms.

Everneed is currently documented as a founder-led startup that has not completed CAC registration. Do not present it as an incorporated entity until registration is confirmed. Do not begin regulated or otherwise restricted activities without confirming the applicable requirements.

## 13. Expansion Rules

Expansion can mean adding a neighbourhood, a category, sellers, hub capacity or a new city. Each expansion must be assessed separately.

Do not expand an area until:

- The current service area has stable fulfilment performance.
- Seller and customer support can handle current demand.
- Hub capacity is not routinely exceeded.
- Package reconciliation and incident handling are dependable.
- Delivery coverage and costs are understood.
- Marketplace and logistics contribution are measured.
- The proposed new area has sufficient seller and customer evidence.
- The new area's hub and last-mile arrangements are confirmed.
- Product configuration and customer-facing coverage information are ready.

Do not expand internationally as part of the initial Nigerian pilot. Ghana, Kenya, South Africa, the United Kingdom and the United States remain potential later markets requiring separate legal, payment, tax, currency, seller and logistics validation.

## 15. Immediate Execution Priorities

1. Compare realistic candidate launch areas and select one based on seller density, customer access, hub practicality and delivery capacity.
2. Conduct structured conversations with prospective sellers in the strongest candidate area.
3. Select a small initial category mix based on real supply and customer need.
4. Map seller-to-hub and hub-to-customer routes and estimate their costs.
5. Decide whether a physical hub can be secured and operated safely; do not imply one exists until confirmed.
6. Define the seller package, parent order and consignment operating procedure.
7. Have the development workflow verify the critical marketplace and portal acceptance tests on the current `web-portal` branch.
8. Prepare seller terms, customer information, privacy and support procedures.
9. Run internal and fulfilment rehearsals before accepting live customer orders.
10. Open a limited customer pilot only when all critical gates are satisfied.

## 16. Market Entry Decision Rule

Everneed should enter its first market when it can reliably connect three things:

- **Supply:** credible sellers with accurate, available products.
- **Demand:** customers who can discover, understand and purchase those products.
- **Fulfilment:** a clear, accountable route from each seller to the customer.

If any one of these is not ready, continue preparation rather than disguising the gap with marketing.

**Build locally. Prove the operation. Expand with evidence.**
