# EVERNEED
## Growth Strategy

**Document type:** Market growth and go-to-market strategy  
**Document status:** Working strategy — experiments and targets require validation  
**Business status:** Founder-led startup; not yet registered with Nigeria's Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC)  
**Initial market:** Nigeria  
**Initial product:** Multivendor marketplace with hub-supported fulfilment  
**Project reference branch:** `web-portal`  
**Last updated:** November 2026

---

## 1. Purpose

This document defines how Everneed should attract and retain sellers, customers and fulfilment partners while growing at a pace its product and operations can reliably support.

Everneed's positioning is **a digital commerce ecosystem**. Its first growth engine is a multivendor marketplace supported by a coordinated delivery model:

- Customers can discover and buy from independent sellers.
- A multi-seller checkout is organised as one customer-facing parent order with separate seller packages.
- For multi-seller purchases, participating sellers send their packages to an Everneed hub, where eligible packages are checked, consolidated and prepared for delivery to the customer.
- For single-seller purchases, the seller may deliver directly or route the package through an Everneed hub when distance or operating conditions make that more suitable.

Growth must therefore build three connected capabilities: relevant seller supply, genuine customer demand and dependable fulfilment capacity.

This is a strategic plan, not a claim that the hub network, delivery fleet, all platform workflows or marketing channels are already operational.

## 2. Growth Objective

Build a trusted, useful marketplace in a deliberately limited Nigerian launch area, prove that customers complete purchases and sellers receive value, and then expand coverage based on repeat demand and fulfilment performance.

The goal is not to maximise downloads, registrations or social media followers in isolation. The goal is to create a functioning marketplace in which:

1. Customers find products worth buying.
2. Sellers can list, prepare and fulfil orders reliably.
3. Multi-seller orders can be consolidated without losing package accountability.
4. Single-seller orders use a practical direct or hub-assisted route.
5. Delivery promises, charges and status updates are clear.
6. The economics of marketplace and logistics activity can be measured.

## 3. Initial Market Focus

### 3.1 Start with a defined service area

Everneed should not market nationwide delivery until it can consistently serve the locations advertised.

Choose an initial city or service area based on:

- Concentration of suitable sellers.
- Customer demand and reachable communities.
- A practical hub location and affordable premises.
- Seller-to-hub travel time.
- Last-mile delivery availability and cost.
- Road access, traffic patterns and operating hours.
- Ability to provide customer support and resolve delivery exceptions.

The initial service boundary should be explicit in the product and customer communications. Expansion beyond it should happen only after the current area meets agreed service standards.

### 3.2 Initial seller segments

Prioritise sellers who have relevant products, reliable stock information and the ability to prepare orders on time. Potential starting segments include:

- Independent retail shops.
- Fashion and accessories merchants.
- Beauty and personal-care retailers.
- Home and household goods sellers.
- Electronics and accessories merchants, subject to product safety and authenticity controls.
- Specialist local businesses with distinctive products.

These are candidate segments, not a final category list. Select the first categories based on seller interviews, customer demand, fulfilment practicality and applicable product rules.

### 3.3 Initial customer segments

Focus on identifiable groups whose shopping needs can be served well in the launch area:

- Mobile-first shoppers who want to discover several local sellers.
- Customers who value a clear, coordinated delivery for purchases from multiple stores.
- Buyers looking for products from specialist local merchants.
- Existing communities around participating sellers.

Avoid broad, expensive mass-market advertising before the marketplace has enough relevant products and reliable fulfilment coverage.

## 4. The Three-Sided Growth Model

Everneed must grow three connected sides together.

| Growth side | What must be built | Evidence of progress |
|---|---|---|
| Seller supply | Approved, active sellers with accurate and available products | Active stores, in-stock catalogue, seller order acceptance and retention |
| Customer demand | Relevant visitors who browse, purchase and return | Product engagement, checkout conversion, completed orders and repeat purchases |
| Fulfilment network | Hub capacity, seller handovers and reliable delivery coverage | Package accuracy, consolidation time, on-time delivery, delivery cost and exception rate |

Growth in one area should not outrun the others. For example, a large customer campaign is premature if products are unavailable or the hub cannot handle the expected package volume.

## 5. Seller Acquisition Strategy

### 5.1 Founder-led seller discovery

Start with direct conversations and visits to relevant businesses in the launch area.

The purpose is to understand:

- How each seller currently receives orders.
- Which channels they use to sell.
- Their product and stock-management practices.
- How quickly they can prepare an order.
- Whether they can deliver directly to customers.
- Whether they can drop packages at an Everneed hub or need a collection service.
- What concerns they have about marketplace fees, delivery charges, returns and settlement.
- What would make them continue using Everneed.

Record feedback consistently. Do not treat a verbal expression of interest as an activated seller.

### 5.2 Seller onboarding funnel

Use a measurable funnel:

1. **Identified:** suitable seller discovered.
2. **Contacted:** seller receives a clear explanation of Everneed.
3. **Interested:** seller agrees to explore participation.
4. **Applied:** seller submits the required information.
5. **Approved:** required checks are completed.
6. **Store ready:** storefront information is complete.
7. **Catalogue ready:** quality products are published and available.
8. **Operational:** seller understands order acceptance, preparation, handover and support.
9. **Activated:** seller receives and successfully fulfils a qualifying order.
10. **Retained:** seller continues to maintain products and participate over time.

Report conversion between stages to identify onboarding friction.

### 5.3 Seller value proposition

Explain the practical value rather than promising sales:

- A digital storefront within a shared marketplace.
- Product discovery by customers beyond the seller's immediate walk-in audience.
- A structured way to receive and manage marketplace orders.
- A coordinated hub option for multi-seller orders.
- A direct delivery option for suitable single-seller orders.
- A potential route to broader customer reach as the marketplace grows.

Never guarantee order volume, income, search ranking or delivery times that the platform cannot substantiate.

### 5.4 Seller activation programme

For the first seller cohort:

- Provide a clear onboarding guide.
- Help sellers prepare accurate product descriptions and images.
- Confirm prices, stock and order-preparation expectations.
- Explain package labelling and hub handover.
- Test at least one complete seller workflow before scaling recruitment.
- Collect feedback after the first completed order.
- Identify sellers who need support before sending more demand their way.

## 6. Customer Acquisition Strategy

### 6.1 Start with seller-connected demand

Each participating seller can help introduce Everneed to customers who already know the business.

Possible low-cost methods:

- Store posters or QR codes.
- WhatsApp status and customer broadcasts, following applicable consent and messaging rules.
- Seller social media posts.
- Product links shared by sellers.
- Referral invitations for existing customers.
- Demonstrations in participating stores.
- Local community groups where promotion is permitted.

The message should show the customer what they can actually do on Everneed, not simply announce that an app exists.

### 6.2 Product-led discovery

Build customer interest around useful catalogue content:

- Real products with accurate prices and availability.
- Clear seller identities and storefronts.
- Category and location relevance where supported.
- Helpful product images and descriptions.
- Transparent delivery options and charges.
- Clear order tracking and customer support.

Content should lead to a real product or store experience. Avoid advertising products that are unavailable or sellers who are not operational.

### 6.3 Social and content strategy

Use social channels to demonstrate the marketplace and its seller network.

Content themes may include:

- Meet the seller.
- Store and product spotlights.
- How multi-seller shopping works.
- How the Everneed hub consolidation process works.
- How direct and hub-assisted delivery differ.
- Product discovery collections.
- Customer education and order walkthroughs.
- Seller onboarding stories, with permission.
- Platform development updates that clearly distinguish completed features from work in progress.

Do not publish private seller/customer information, misleading order claims or mockups presented as live functionality.

### 6.4 Referral strategy

Test referrals after the customer journey works reliably.

Potential experiments include customer-to-customer referrals and seller invitations. Any reward must have a defined budget, eligibility rules, fraud controls and a clear explanation. Do not introduce incentives that make unprofitable orders appear to be successful growth.

## 7. Everneed Hub and Delivery Network Growth

The hub model is a differentiating part of Everneed's intended service. Its capacity must grow in step with marketplace demand.

### 7.1 Multi-seller consolidation

For a customer order containing items from several sellers:

1. The platform groups the purchase under one parent order.
2. Each seller package retains its own seller and item references.
3. Sellers prepare and send/drop their packages at the designated hub.
4. Hub staff record receipt, package count and visible condition.
5. Packages are checked against the expected seller packages.
6. Eligible packages are consolidated and packed for the customer.
7. A customer delivery consignment is dispatched.
8. Delivery outcome and proof of delivery are recorded.

A single combined parcel is the target where items can safely and practically be consolidated. If product size, handling requirements or operational constraints require multiple parcels, the customer should receive accurate information rather than an unsupported one-parcel promise.

### 7.2 Single-seller fulfilment

For a purchase from one store:

- Use direct seller-to-customer delivery when the seller can serve the destination reliably and the route is practical.
- Use hub-assisted delivery when the distance is far, the seller requests it, or operational conditions make hub routing more suitable.
- Show the selected route and applicable delivery charge before the customer pays.
- Keep the seller package traceable even if it passes through a hub.

### 7.3 Delivery partner strategy

Initially, compare vetted delivery partners with the cost and complexity of employing riders.

Assess partners on:

- Geographic coverage.
- Price by route, distance or zone.
- Parcel size and weight limits.
- Pickup reliability and operating hours.
- Tracking and proof-of-delivery capability.
- Failed-delivery and reattempt policies.
- Returns handling.
- Claims for lost or damaged packages.
- Customer communication and support.
- Contract terms, insurance and regulatory suitability.

Do not promise same-day, next-day or other delivery windows until real route data supports them.

### 7.4 Hub capacity gates

Before increasing customer acquisition, verify that the hub can manage:

- Expected daily package arrivals.
- Peak-period package volumes.
- Package identification and sorting.
- Secure temporary storage.
- Consolidation and packaging time.
- Dispatch cut-off times.
- Returns and exception handling.
- Staff coverage and customer support.

Track packages per hour, average hub dwell time, consolidation accuracy, dispatch turnaround and cost per package.

### 7.5 Expand by operational zones

Add a new delivery zone only when:

- There are enough active sellers or customer demand to justify service.
- A practical route and delivery partner are available.
- Delivery pricing covers the expected cost or a deliberate, budgeted launch subsidy.
- Customer support can handle exceptions.
- The existing hub and delivery network can absorb the additional work.

A new hub should be justified by package density, travel time, capacity constraints and contribution—not by geographic ambition alone.

## 8. Partnerships

Prioritise partnerships that solve immediate marketplace constraints.

| Partner type | Potential value | Validation required |
|---|---|---|
| Local retailers and seller associations | Relevant seller supply and local credibility | Seller quality, participation and onboarding effort |
| Delivery companies and independent carriers | Last-mile coverage and route capacity | Service levels, price, tracking, claims and contract |
| Packaging suppliers | Reliable materials and potentially lower unit cost | Quality, availability, minimum order and total cost |
| Payment providers | Secure collection and transaction processing | Marketplace suitability, settlement model, fees and support |
| Local business communities | Seller introductions and customer awareness | Audience relevance and measurable outcomes |
| Technology providers | Infrastructure and operational capability | Security, reliability, integration cost and data terms |

Partnerships should have named owners, written terms, clear service levels and a review process.

## 9. Launch and Growth Phases

| Phase | Main work | Exit criteria |
|---|---|---|
| Foundation | Interview sellers/customers; select categories and launch area; map hub and delivery process | Clear target segment, service boundary, operating design and cost assumptions |
| Supply preparation | Recruit initial sellers; approve stores; publish accurate products; train sellers | Useful catalogue and sellers ready to prepare and hand over orders |
| Operational pilot | Test direct seller delivery, hub-assisted single-seller delivery and multi-seller consolidation | Complete test orders, package traceability, customer communication and cost records |
| Controlled customer beta | Invite a limited customer cohort and monitor every order | Reliable order completion, support response and delivery performance |
| Local growth | Expand seller/customer acquisition within the proven area | Repeat customers, retained sellers and manageable hub capacity |
| Zone expansion | Add new service areas and partners selectively | Positive or deliberately funded contribution and repeatable operating standards |

These phases are gates, not fixed calendar promises. Do not advance solely because a date has passed.

## 10. Growth Experiments

Run small experiments with a clear hypothesis, owner, budget, measurement window and stop/continue decision.

| Experiment | Hypothesis | Primary measure | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seller QR cards | Store visitors will explore the seller's Everneed catalogue | Visits and purchases from seller QR links | No misleading availability claims |
| Seller social spotlights | Seller audiences can generate qualified marketplace traffic | Product views and completed orders | Seller consent and accurate product details |
| Local customer referrals | Existing customers can introduce similar buyers | Referred first purchases and repeat rate | Incentive cost and referral abuse |
| Multi-seller basket demonstration | Customers value combined ordering from several stores | Multi-seller checkout completion | Consolidation accuracy and delivery cost |
| Hub-assisted single-seller route test | Hub routing can improve delivery reach for selected distant orders | On-time delivery and cost per order | Additional handling must be justified |
| Seller onboarding clinic | Guided onboarding increases store activation | Approved-to-active seller conversion | Support time per activated seller |
| Category-focused campaign | A concentrated catalogue performs better than a broad catalogue | Conversion and repeat purchases in that category | Stock accuracy and fulfilment capacity |

Do not run multiple overlapping campaigns without tracking their sources. Use campaign codes or tagged links where practical.

## 11. Growth Metrics and Dashboard

### Seller metrics

- Sellers identified, contacted, applied, approved and activated.
- Active sellers and retained sellers.
- Time from application to approved store.
- Time from approval to first published product.
- Seller order acceptance and preparation times.
- Seller cancellation and stock-accuracy rates.

### Customer metrics

- New and active customers.
- Product and store views.
- Add-to-cart and checkout conversion.
- First completed purchase.
- Repeat purchase rate.
- Customer acquisition cost by channel.
- Complaints and support resolution time.

### Marketplace metrics

- Published and available products.
- GMV and average order value.
- Paid, cancelled, refunded and completed orders.
- Single-seller versus multi-seller order share.
- Commission revenue and contribution per order.
- Seller retention and customer repeat behaviour.

### Hub and delivery metrics

- Packages expected, received, mismatched and missing.
- Average seller preparation-to-hub arrival time.
- Hub dwell time and consolidation turnaround.
- Packages consolidated per parent order.
- Delivery cost and delivery revenue per consignment.
- On-time delivery rate.
- Failed delivery and reattempt rate.
- Lost/damaged package rate.
- Returns and reverse-logistics cost.
- Hub cost per package and logistics contribution.

### Growth quality

A campaign should be judged by activated sellers, completed orders, repeat purchases and contribution—not impressions, followers or downloads alone.

## 12. Growth Economics and Budget Discipline

For every acquisition channel, estimate:

`Channel Contribution = Attributable Net Platform Revenue - Acquisition Spend - Incremental Variable Costs`

For fulfilment, calculate separately:

`Logistics Contribution = Delivery/Hub Service Revenue - Direct Fulfilment Costs`

Direct fulfilment costs may include seller-to-hub transfers, hub labour, packaging, carrier/rider charges, failed deliveries, reattempts and returns.

Do not use GMV as Everneed revenue. Do not count seller proceeds or carrier pass-through funds as platform earnings. Do not hide delivery subsidies inside marketplace commission.

Maintain a small experiment budget and record spend before launching a campaign. Scale only when the channel produces customers or sellers who remain active and can be served reliably.

## 13. Trust and Brand Growth

Everneed's reputation will be built through the accuracy of its promises and the consistency of its operations.

Core trust commitments should include:

- Clearly identified sellers.
- Accurate product descriptions, prices and availability.
- Transparent delivery options and charges.
- Clear distinction between direct delivery and hub-assisted delivery.
- Traceable package handovers.
- Honest order and delivery status.
- A visible way to contact support.
- Fair handling of cancellations, refunds and disputes.
- Responsible handling of customer and seller information.

Do not use fake reviews, fabricated order counts, false scarcity, misleading delivery promises or unverified seller badges. Any verification mark must have a documented verification standard.

## 14. Key Growth Risks

| Risk | Impact | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Recruiting sellers before customer demand | Sellers become inactive | Recruit in focused categories and validate customer interest |
| Acquiring customers before catalogue readiness | Poor conversion and lost trust | Confirm stock, product quality and seller readiness first |
| Marketing outside delivery coverage | Failed or expensive fulfilment | Enforce clear service areas and checkout eligibility |
| Hub overload | Late dispatch and package errors | Set capacity limits and pause campaigns when necessary |
| Delivery pricing below cost | Growing logistics losses | Cost routes and review charges or service design |
| Unreliable seller handovers | Incomplete consolidated orders | Set preparation deadlines and package tracking controls |
| Poor partner performance | Delays, loss and customer complaints | Use service levels, scorecards and backup providers |
| Incentive abuse | Artificial growth and financial loss | Set eligibility, budget and fraud controls |
| Premature geographic expansion | Operational complexity and cash strain | Require service and unit-economic gates |
| Misleading public claims | Reputation and legal risk | Publish only verified capabilities and results |

## 15. Immediate Execution Priorities

1. Select the initial launch area and define exactly where Everneed can accept orders.
2. Identify the first seller categories and interview prospective merchants.
3. Determine the first hub's proposed location, capacity, operating hours and cost assumptions before committing to premises.
4. Map the seller-to-hub, hub-consolidation and last-mile process.
5. Compare delivery partners and direct seller delivery using actual route quotations.
6. Prepare seller onboarding and package handover instructions.
7. Define a small pilot cohort and test all three fulfilment cases: multi-seller hub consolidation, single-seller direct delivery and single-seller hub-assisted delivery.
8. Establish seller, customer, hub and delivery performance reporting.
9. Start with low-cost, attributable acquisition experiments.
10. Expand campaigns and service zones only after the pilot meets agreed standards.

## 16. Document Control

This is a working growth strategy. Update it when Everneed confirms the launch area, seller categories, hub arrangements, delivery partners, pilot results and actual acquisition economics.

All targets should be labelled as proposed, measured or achieved. Public claims must reflect verified platform and operating capabilities.

**Guiding principle: Grow the seller network, customer demand and fulfilment capacity together—never faster than Everneed can serve reliably.**
